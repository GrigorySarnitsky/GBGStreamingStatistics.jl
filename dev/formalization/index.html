Formalization

The typed statements behind the prose. Each operation gets one entry stating its estimand, its estimator, and the consistency claim — the bias and variance with every normalization constant pinned — each linked to the test that verifies the convergence rate numerically. The chain is prose ↔ formalization ↔ test: the prose (docstrings, ARCHITECTURE.md) agrees with the typed statement, the statement is verified against the code, and the linked test enforces it.

A nontrivial claim with no entry here, or an entry with no test link, is a recorded coverage gap (CLAUDE.md, Definition of done).

Throughout, $K$ is the smoothing kernel with $\int K = 1$, $\mu_2(K) = \int u^2 K(u)\,\mathrm{d}u$ its second moment, and $R(K) = \int K^2$ its roughness; $h$ is the bandwidth, $n$ the number of samples, $f_X$ the marginal density of $X$ (the conditioning variable in the joint estimators).

The entries are grouped by the lens of ARCHITECTURE.md §2: the atoms (the power-sums summary, the cone, the kernel-weighting atom & jet) first, then the two readout families — the kernel-weighting readouts (§4.A) and the power-sum quadrature readouts (§4.B). A forthcoming operation gets its entry — in the same shape (estimand / estimator / consistency / test) — when it lands, its estimand recorded in ARCHITECTURE.md §4 meanwhile.

Per-bin power-sums about the bin center (Histograms, the summaries)

  • Object. per bin, the count and the power-sums about the fixed bin center $S_p = \sum_{i\in\text{bin}}(X_i - c_j)^p$ for $p = 1 \dots \texttt{order}$ (and order-2 bivariate $S_{pp'}$ per cell for Histogram2D). Intrinsically finite-sample — no population estimand.

  • Accumulation. an atomic scatter-add (observe! / observe_pairs!): one work-item per sample adds $(X_i - c_j)^p$ to its bin's $S_p$, in the accumulator type (Float64 by default). merge! is elementwise addition (same binning $\Rightarrow$ same centers). The local offsets are well-conditioned, so no compensated summation is used.

  • Mergeability (a monoid morphism). Write $(\mathcal{M}, \uplus, \varnothing)$ — finite sample collections (multisets over the sample space) under disjoint union, $\varnothing$ the empty collection — and $(\mathcal{S}, \oplus, 0)$ — summaries under merge! (elementwise addition of counts and power-sums), $0$ the all-zero summary. Both are commutative monoids. Accumulation $T : \mathcal{M} \to \mathcal{S}$ is the unique homomorphism extending the summary of a single sample (one sample adds $1$ to its bin's count and $(X_i - c_j)^p$ to that bin's $S_p$), so $T(\mathcal{A}) = \bigoplus_{X_i \in \mathcal{A}} T(\{X_i\})$ and the square

    \[\begin{CD} \mathcal{M} \times \mathcal{M} @>{\uplus}>> \mathcal{M} \\ @V{T \times T}VV @VV{T}V \\ \mathcal{S} \times \mathcal{S} @>{\oplus}>> \mathcal{S} \end{CD}\]

    commutes: $T(\mathcal{A} \uplus \mathcal{B}) = T(\mathcal{A}) \oplus T(\mathcal{B})$, which is accumulate-then-merge $=$ accumulate-together. Commutativity and associativity of $\oplus$ then make any reduction order or tree (multi-thread, multi-device, time-chunk) yield the same summary. The morphism is the unnormalized summary only: the normalize-and-smooth readout $\mathcal{R}_h$ (the $1/n$, the shift formula, the kernel) that turns a summary into estimates is not a homomorphism — which is exactly why merge! has no population estimand (pool summaries first, estimate once, never the reverse). Exact for the integer counts; the Float64 power-sums merge up to round-off (non-associative floating-point addition), so the test asserts $=$ on counts and $\approx$ on the $S_p$.

  • Recovery. the global central moments are the shift formula

    \[\begin{aligned} \sum_i (X_i - \bar X)^p &= \sum_j \sum_{k=0}^{p} \binom{p}{k} (c_j - \bar X)^{p-k} S_k^{(j)}, \\ \bar X &= \tfrac{1}{n} \sum_j \bigl(c_j n_j + S_1^{(j)}\bigr), \qquad S_0^{(j)} = n_j, \end{aligned}\]

    which is exact (the $S_p$ use the actual $X_i$, not the center) and numerically stable for the even $p$ the consumers need; the recovered moments are exposed publicly (in Expectations, §4.B, the $g = x^p$ quadrature case) as mean, var, std, and central_moment — var and std apply Bessel's $n-1$ correction by default (matching Statistics), corrected = false the $1/n$ form.

  • Quadrature readout (the power-sum atom; §4.B). the same power-sums give any smooth functional by Taylor contraction about the bin centers, $\mathbb{E}[g(X)] = \tfrac{1}{n} \sum_j \sum_{p=0}^{\texttt{order}} g^{(p)}(c_j)/p! S_p^{(j)} + O(\Delta^{\texttt{order}+1})$ — exact for polynomials of degree $\le \texttt{order}$ (the moment recovery above is the $g = x^p$ case), $O(\Delta^{\texttt{order}+1})$ for general smooth $g$. The general $\mathbb{E}[g(X)]$, its standard error, and the pushforward density $f_{g(X)}$ are built in Expectations (the §4.B section below), all through this same contraction — no new accumulation.

  • Test. test/histograms.jl (recovered $\hat\mu_2 \dots \hat\mu_6$ match the direct sum to $10^{-8}$ and the true Gaussian moments to sampling error); test/histograms2d.jl (2D marginals + covariance); test/mergeability.jl (merge $=$ together); test/allocations.jl (bounded observe! / merge!); test/gpu/suite.jl (CPU-vs-AMD-GPU parity).

The cone — scale!, weighted merge!, hyperstatistics (§10)

  • Object. the summary monoid $(\mathcal{S}, \oplus, 0)$ of the previous entry, equipped also with a nonnegative scalar action $\lambda \cdot T$ (scale! — multiply every count and power-sum by $\lambda \ge 0$), making $\mathcal{S}$ a convex cone: closed under $\oplus$ and under nonnegative scaling, hence under all nonnegative linear combinations $\sum_k \pi_k T_k$ (the weighted merge!(into, from; weight) with weight as $\lambda$, $\texttt{into} \mathrel{+}= \lambda \cdot \texttt{from}$; the default $\lambda = 1$ the plain $\oplus$). Carried on a Float-count variant (count_type = Float64): a weighted count $\lambda n$ is not an integer.

  • Estimand (hyperstatistics; Beck & Cohen 2003). the parameter-mixture (superstatistical) density over a slowly varying parameter $\theta$ with mixing law $\pi(\theta)$,

    \[f_X(x) = \int \pi(\theta) f_\theta(x)\,\mathrm{d}\theta \approx \sum_k \pi_k f_{\theta_k}(x), \qquad \pi_k = \pi(\theta_k) (\theta_{k+1} - \theta_k),\]

    with $f_\theta$ the per-parameter density.

  • Estimator. keep one summary $T_k$ per $\theta_k$ (accumulated independently, $n(T_k)$ samples), form the cone combination $T = \sum_k (\pi_k / n(T_k)) T_k$ by weighted merge!, and read out any smoothing functional $\mathcal{R}_h$ of $T$. Since $\mathcal{R}_h$ normalizes by the total mass $n(T) = \sum_j n_j = \sum_k \pi_k$, this yields $\mathcal{R}_h(T) = (\sum_k \pi_k f_{\theta_k}(x)) / \sum_k \pi_k$ — the mixture (exactly, when the $\pi_k$ sum to one); the $1/n(T_k)$ normalizes each component to a probability measure before mixing, and the conditional / transition estimators carry the local weight $\pi_k f_{\theta_k}(x)$ automatically (ratios of linear functionals). The law $\pi(\theta)$ is chosen post hoc, after the runs, re-weighting without re-accumulating.

  • Cone laws (the spec; the operations have no population estimand). scale! is the monoid action — $\lambda \cdot (\lambda' \cdot T) = (\lambda\lambda') \cdot T$, $1 \cdot T = T$ — and distributes over $\oplus$: $\lambda \cdot (T \oplus T') = (\lambda \cdot T) \oplus (\lambda \cdot T')$; the weighted merge! is $T \oplus (\lambda \cdot T')$. scale! preserves the normalized distribution: the mean and the uncorrected central moments are exact invariants; only the total mass — and through it the Bessel-corrected variance — scales.

  • Exponential forgetting. for a non-stationary stream, scale!(s, λ) with $0 < \lambda < 1$ before folding the next block gives a geometrically down-weighted summary (effective window $1/(1-\lambda)$); the recovered mean is the exact $\lambda$-weighted mean of the blocks.

  • Caveat. $n(T) = \sum_k \pi_k$ ($\approx 1$ for a normalized law), so $T$'s default bandwidth reads $n \approx 1$; pass an explicit $h$ (or pre-scale the weights to a representative sample size). An effective-sample-size rule (Kish $(\sum_i \lambda_i)^2 / \sum_i \lambda_i^2$) is a recorded refinement.

  • Test. test/hyperstatistics.jl — the cone laws (scale! homogeneity / distributivity, weighted merge $=$ scale-then-merge, $\lambda = 1$ $=$ plain merge!, the Float-count guard); the discrete Gaussian-mixture oracle; the $\chi^2$-superstatistics → Student-$\mathrm{t}$ oracle ($X \mid \theta \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1/\theta)$, $\theta \sim \mathrm{Gamma}(\nu/2, 2/\nu)$ $\Rightarrow$ the Student-$\mathrm{t}_\nu$ marginal, recovered into the heavy tail); exact exponential forgetting; the Histogram2D cone with conditional_mean scale-invariance. test/allocations.jl (allocation-free scale! / weighted merge!); test/gpu/suite.jl (CPU-vs-GPU parity, incl. the atomic-float add of Float-count observe!).

The kernel-weighting atom & local-polynomial jet (Kernels._kernel_weight, LocalPolynomial.local_fit)

  • Kernel-weighting atom (_kernel_weight). the single per-bin kernel weight at a query point $x$ and bandwidth $h$, in one of two protocols: symmetric $K_h(c_j - x) = K((c_j - x)/h)/h$ (the $1/h$ folded in, normalized), or asymmetric $K(c_j; x, b)$ (a varying-shape density in the data argument, its own normalization, $b$ its scale). Every smoothing readout consumes this weight — a count sum (density), a ratio (the jet below), a ratio of ratios (conditional_density) — so the two protocols live in exactly one place. Folding the $1/h$ into the weight is $\beta$-invariant for the local-polynomial solve (both the design matrix and the right-hand side scale by $1/h$), so it leaves every symmetric estimate unchanged.
  • Jet (local_fit). the kernel-weighted local-polynomial jet at $x$: the degree-$d$ weighted-least-squares fit of a polynomial in $\tilde c_j - x$ to a binned 1-D measure (locations $\tilde c_j$, design weights $n_j$, response totals $n_j \bar y_j$ with $\bar y_j$ the bin's mean response), returning $\beta = (\beta_0, \dots, \beta_d)$ — $\beta_0$ the value, $\beta_1$ the slope, $2\beta_2$ the curvature. Form the design and response moments $U_p = \sum_j K_h(\tilde c_j - x) n_j (\tilde c_j - x)^p$ ($p = 0 \dots 2d$) and $V_p = \sum_j K_h(\tilde c_j - x) n_j \bar y_j (\tilde c_j - x)^p$ ($p = 0 \dots d$), the kernel weight from _kernel_weight, and solve the $(d+1)\times(d+1)$ Hankel normal equations $[U_{r+s}]_{r,s=0}^d \beta = (V_0, \dots, V_d)$ (a small solve, never an explicit inverse); a degenerate (singular) design returns an all-NaN jet. conditional_mean is $\beta_0$ of one fit; the local-likelihood density reuses the solve as its IRLS inner step. An asymmetric kernel has no jet (it is not a translation kernel), so local_fit admits it at degree = 0 only — the one guard, a clean error above it.
  • Test. test/localpolynomial.jl — on a binned measure whose per-bin response mean is exactly a polynomial $m$, the jet recovers $(m(x), m'(x), m''(x)/2)$ to $10^{-6}$ (degree 2) and $(m(x), m'(x))$ (degree 1); degree 0 is the Nadaraya–Watson ratio $V_0/U_0$; a degenerate design gives an all-NaN jet; the kernel normalization cancels. The $\beta$-invariance of the $1/h$ fold and the asymmetric-degree-0 admission ride on the unchanged density and conditional_mean oracle / rate tests. The kernel constants the weighting depends on ($\int K = 1$, $\mu_2(K)$, $R(K)$) are verified by independent quadrature in test/kernels.jl.

Marginal density (§4.1) — density

  • Estimand. $f_X = \mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_X/\mathrm{d}x$, the Lebesgue density of $X$.
  • Estimator. the binned KDE $\hat f_X(x) = \frac{1}{nh}\sum_j n_j K\Bigl(\frac{x-\tilde c_j}{h}\Bigr)$ ($\int K = 1$, Gaussian $K$) from the bin counts $n_j$ at bin locations $\tilde c_j$ — the within-bin centroid $\tilde c_j = c_j + S_1^{(j)}/n_j$ (linear binning, the default binning = :linear) or the geometric center $\tilde c_j = c_j$ (binning = :simple) — with $h$ from bandwidth (Silverman $h = (4/3)^{1/5}\hat\sigma_X n^{-1/5}$).
  • Consistency claim ($h \to 0$, $nh \to \infty$): $\operatorname{bias}\hat f_X(x) \approx \tfrac{1}{2} h^2 \mu_2(K) f_X''(x)$ and $\operatorname{Var}\hat f_X(x) \approx R(K) f_X(x)/(n h)$; the binning error converges to the exact KDE at $O(\Delta^2)$ in the bin width $\Delta$ for linear binning — the centroid matches the within-bin first moment, $\sum_{i\in j}(X_i - \tilde c_j) = 0$, so the leading $O(\Delta)$ discretization term vanishes — and at $O(\Delta)$ for simple binning; either is dominated by the $O(h^2)$ smoothing bias when $\Delta$ is small.
  • Test. test/oracles.jl (recovers the $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ pdf to $< 3\times10^{-3}$ over $[-3,3]$, integrates to 1). test/density_convergence.jl — a representative analytic-PDF suite (Gaussian, uniform, exponential, Student-$\mathrm{t}$ $\nu=3$ heavy tail, log-normal, bimodal: each recovered to $\sim 10^{-2}$ and integrating to $\approx 1$, the variable bandwidth tracking the $\mathrm{t}$ heavy tail better than the fixed) and the convergence-rate test: the ISE falls at the theoretical $n^{-2q/(2q+1)}$ — slope $\approx -0.80$ for degree 0/1 ($q=2$), the steeper $\approx -0.89$ ($n^{-8/9}$, $q=4$) for degree 2, the Abramson variable bandwidth sharing the degree-0 order; plus the binning-error convergence sub-test — the binned estimate approaches the exact KDE at $O(\Delta^2)$ for the default linear/centroid binning (slope $\approx 2$ in $\Delta$) and $O(\Delta)$ for binning = :simple (slope $\approx 1$).

Local-log-polynomial & adaptive density (§4.1) — density's degree / adaptive

  • Estimand. $f_X$, exactly as for the degree-0 density above; the options change the estimator, not the target.
  • adaptive (Abramson variable bandwidth). A fixed-bandwidth pilot $\tilde f_X$ at $h$ sets per-bin bandwidths $h_j = h (\tilde f_X(c_j)/G)^{-1/2}$ ($G$ the geometric mean of $\tilde f_X$ over the sample), widening the kernel in the sparse tails: $\operatorname{bias} = O(h^4)$ at interior smooth $x$ (Abramson 1982), with far less tail variance than a fixed $h$.
  • $\texttt{degree} \ge 1$ (local-log-polynomial). Fit $\log f_X$ by a degree-$d$ polynomial through a binned local Poisson likelihood — $\log \mathbb{E}[n_j] = \log(n\Delta) + \sum_{p \le d} \beta_p (c_j-x)^p$, weights $K((c_j-x)/h_j)$, by Newton/IRLS — and report $\hat f_X(x) = e^{\hat\beta_0}$ (Loader 1996). Degree 1 is exact for an exponential density (tracks exponential tails), degree 2 for a Gaussian. The two options compose. On a bounded support the degree-0 KDE accepts support = (lower, upper), renormalizing by the kernel mass $\int_{\texttt{lower}}^{\texttt{upper}} K_h(x-v)\,\mathrm{d}v$ inside the support (Jones 1993) to remove the $O(1)$ boundary bias that otherwise halves the estimate at the edge.
  • Test. test/marginaldensity.jl (every grid corner recovers the $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ pdf into the tail and integrates to $\approx 1$; the variable bandwidth resolves the sharp Laplace cusp the fixed KDE oversmooths; degree-1 beats the KDE in a Laplace tail).

Asymmetric kernels (§4.1) — density's gamma_kernel / beta_kernel

  • Estimand. $f_X$ on a bounded or positive support.
  • Estimator. the varying-shape estimate $\hat f_X(x) = \tfrac{1}{n} \sum_j n_j K(\tilde c_j; x, b)$ with $K(\cdot;x,b)$ the modified $\mathrm{Gamma}(\rho(x), b)$ density (gamma kernel, support $[0,\infty)$; Chen 2000) or the modified $\mathrm{Beta}$ density (beta kernel, support $[0,1]$; Chen 1999) — Chen's bias-reduced shapes (interior shape $x/b$, kernel mean $\approx x$, removing the $f_X'$ bias of the naive $x/b+1$ form); the support matches the data, so no mass leaks past the boundary. A reduction over the bin counts (mergeable); default $b = \hat\sigma_X n^{-2/5}$.
  • Consistency claim. bias $O(b)$ uniformly including at the boundary (where a symmetric kernel is $O(h)$ and inconsistent); interior variance $O(n^{-1}b^{-1/2})$; optimal $b \sim n^{-2/5}$, MISE $\sim n^{-4/5}$. Not self-normalizing (an $O(b)$ mass deviation). The modified shape lowers the MISE constant (Chen proves the bias-reduced estimator's MISE is at most the naive estimator's; only the bias-reduced one ships, so the gain is the literature result, not a repo-reproducible number).
  • Test. test/marginaldensity.jl (gamma recovers the exponential at the boundary, beating the symmetric KDE there; beta recovers $\mathrm{Uniform}(0,1)$ at both edges; integrates to $\approx 1$; mergeability; degree/adaptive/support rejected).

Parametric start & normalization (§4.1) — density's start / normalize

  • Estimand. $f_X$, exactly as above; start and normalize change the estimator, not the target.
  • Estimator (start, Hjort & Glad 1995). The semiparametric estimate $\hat f_X(x) = f_{\hat\theta}(x) \widehat{f_X/f_\theta}(x)$, where $f_{\hat\theta}$ is a parametric start fit by method of moments from the recovered $\bar X$, $\hat\sigma_X^2$, and $\widehat{f_X/f_\theta}(x) = \tfrac{1}{nh}\sum_j (n_j/f_{\hat\theta}(c_j)) K((x-c_j)/h)$ is a KDE of the correction $f_X/f_\theta$ (the start's local relative error). Five starts are provided — normal_start, lognormal_start, gamma_start, exponential_start, inverse_gaussian_start — each a density whose parameters are fixed by the first two moments, so $\hat\theta$ is itself a post-hoc functional of the summary and the whole estimate is mergeable ($\hat\theta$ merges through the moments; the reweighted counts add). Degree-0, fixed-$h$, symmetric kernel.
  • Consistency claim. For a fixed start with bounded, twice-differentiable correction $f_X/f_\theta$, consistent for $f_X$ at $\operatorname{bias} = O(h^2)$, $\operatorname{Var} = O(1/(nh))$ (MISE $\sim n^{-4/5}$), but with the leading bias governed by $(f_X/f_\theta)''$ in place of $f_X''$ and the variance $R(K)f_X(x)/(nh)$ unchanged to leading order. When the start is correct $f_X/f_\theta \equiv 1$, $(f_X/f_\theta)'' \equiv 0$: the leading bias vanishes and the estimate collapses to the parametric fit. So the start is a bias reduction that pays in the bias-dominated regime (skewed / heavy-tailed shapes, oversmoothed $h$), not in the large-$n$ limit where the KDE is already nearly unbiased.
  • Estimator (normalize). $\hat f_X / \int \hat f_X\,\mathrm{d}x$, the integral over the summary's range intersected with the estimator's support, by the trapezoidal rule on a fine grid — a deterministic, mergeable functional of the estimate making $\int \hat f_X = 1$ exactly (the asymmetric kernels and a parametric start are not self-normalizing; the symmetric KDE already is, to the grid's $O(\Delta^2)$).
  • Test. test/marginaldensity.jl (a normal start at a wide $h$ recovers the Gaussian peak the plain KDE flattens by $\approx 10\%$, ISE $> 20\times$ smaller; a log-normal start tracks log-normal data $> 3\times$ better than the symmetric KDE, and pointwise into the right tail; normalize drives $\int\hat f_X$ to $1$ for a start and for the gamma kernel; mergeability; the rejected option combinations).

Pointwise standard error (§4.1) — density_standard_error, density_with_standard_error

  • Estimand. the standard error of the density estimator, $\operatorname{SE}(x) = \sqrt{\operatorname{Var}\hat f_X(x)}$ with the finite-sample KDE variance $\operatorname{Var}\hat f_X(x) \approx R(K) f_X(x)/(nh) - f_X(x)^2/n$ (Wand & Jones 1995).
  • Estimator. $\sqrt{R(K) \hat f_X(x)/(nh) - \hat f_X(x)^2/n}$ from the recovered $\hat f_X$, $n$, $h$, and $R(K) = \int K^2$ (by quadrature) — the band $\hat f_X(x) \pm 1.96 \operatorname{SE}(x)$ about $\mathbb{E}\hat f_X$, covering $f_X$ where the $O(h^2)$ smoothing bias is small. It tracks the estimator's options: adaptive uses the local Abramson bandwidth $h(x)$ in place of $h$; support (Jones renormalization) uses the boundary-restricted roughness $R_a(x) = \frac{1}{h}\int_{\text{support}} K((x-v)/h)^2\,\mathrm dv$ and divides by the squared in-support kernel mass $a(x)^2$, inflating the variance near an edge; a parametric start leaves the leading variance unchanged (its $\hat\theta$ uncertainty is an $O(1/n)$ omission). density_with_standard_error returns the estimate and this band from one option specification (the NamedTuple $(\texttt{value}, \texttt{standard\_error})$), so they describe the same estimator by construction. Degree 0/1 and a symmetric kernel (the asymmetric-kernel variance and degree $\ge 2$ are coverage gaps).
  • Test. test/marginaldensity.jl — the SE equals the two-term $\sqrt{R(K)\hat f_X/(nh) - \hat f_X^2/n}$ exactly; a Monte-Carlo check (the predicted SE matches the empirical sampling standard deviation of $\hat f_X(0)$ across independent datasets to $20\%$); adaptive lowers the tail variance and support inflates the edge variance; density_with_standard_error matches the separate calls exactly; the 95\% band covers the $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ truth over $\ge 75\%$ of the interior; vector / empty / error forms.

Bandwidth selection (§4.5) — bandwidth

  • Estimand. the AMISE-optimal bandwidth (AMISE = asymptotic mean integrated squared error) $h_{\mathrm{AMISE}}$ — asymptotically minimising $\operatorname{MISE}(h) = \int \mathbb{E}[(\hat f_h(x) - f_X(x))^2]\,\mathrm{d}x$ (the AMISE is its leading-order large-$n$ expansion) for a kernel $K$ and local-polynomial degree $d$ — the asymptotically optimal smoothing scale, itself $O(n^{-1/(2q+1)})$ (a finite-sample selector, not a fixed population constant).
  • Estimator (rule = :normal, the kernel- and degree-aware normal-reference). $h = C(K,d) \hat\sigma_X n^{-1/(2q+1)}$ with bias order $q = 2$ (degree 0/1) or $q = 4$ (degree 2/3), and constant $C(K,d) = [(R(K)/\mu_2(K)^2) 2^{q+1}\sqrt{\pi}/(2q-1)!!]^{1/(2q+1)}$ at degree 0/1; at degree 2/3 the canonical factor $R(K)/\mu_2(K)^2$ is replaced by its fourth-order equivalent-kernel analog $(4!^2/8) R(K_{(4)})/\mu_4(K_{(4)})^2$ with $K_{(4)}(u) = K(u) (\mu_4(K) - u^2 \mu_2(K))/(\mu_0(K) \mu_4(K) - \mu_2(K)^2)$ (Loader 1996); here $R(K) = \int K^2$ and $\mu_p(K) = \int u^p K(u)\,\mathrm{d}u$ (by quadrature). $\hat\sigma_X$ and $n$ are read from the one summary.
  • Estimator (rule = :sj, the data-driven Sheather–Jones plug-in; degree 0/1). The curvature $\psi_4 = R(f_X'')$ and its $\psi_6$ pilot are estimated from the bin counts, $\hat\psi_r(\tilde h) = (n^2 \tilde h^{r+1})^{-1}\sum_{j,j'} n_j n_{j'} \varphi^{(r)}((c_j - c_{j'})/\tilde h)$ (Gaussian $\varphi$, the $n$ self-pairs removed), and the solve-the-equation bandwidth (Sheather & Jones 1991) is the root of $h = [R(\varphi)/(\mu_2(\varphi)^2 \hat\psi_4(\tilde h) n)]^{1/5}$, the pilot $\tilde h$ proportional to $h^{5/7}$; the eval kernel then multiplies by the canonical-bandwidth ratio $\delta(K)/\delta(\varphi)$ with $\delta(K) = [R(K)/\mu_2(K)^2]^{1/5}$. Both functionals are reductions of the bin counts, hence mergeable; degree $\ge 2$ is a recorded coverage gap. The improved variant (rule = :isj; Botev, Grotowski & Kroese 2010) instead reads $\|f_X^{(r)}\|^2$ off the DCT of the bin counts and solves the AMISE fixed-point equation for $h^2$ — also a mergeable functional of the counts, and robust on strongly multimodal data.
  • Estimator (rule = :aic, the fit-based information criterion; degree 0/1). Minimise $\operatorname{AIC}(h) = -2\sum_j n_j\log\hat f_h(c_j) + 2\operatorname{df}(h)$ of the binned Gaussian KDE over a log-spaced $h$-grid, with the effective degrees of freedom $\operatorname{df}(h) = (\varphi(0)/h)\sum_j n_j/(n\hat f_h(c_j))$ — the kernel smoother's self-influence trace ($\varphi$ the Gaussian); both terms diverge as $h\to0$, giving a stable interior optimum. Mergeable (a functional of the counts); the eval kernel multiplies by $\delta(K)/\delta(\varphi)$. The leave-one-out cross-validation selectors (likelihood-CV, least-squares LSCV) are a recorded coverage gap — binning ties drive their objective to $h\to0$.
  • Estimator (rule = :plugin, the asymmetric-kernel Chen plug-in; gamma_kernel / beta_kernel). The gamma / beta estimators take a scale $b$ (rate $n^{-2/5}$), not $h$; their AMISE (Chen 1999, 2000; the bias-reduced estimator) is $A b^2 + V b^{-1/2}/n$ with $A = \tfrac{1}{4}\int w^2 (f_X'')^2$, $V = (2\sqrt\pi)^{-1}\mathbb{E}[w(X)^{-1/2}]$ and local-scale weight $w(x) = x$ (gamma) or $x(1-x)$ (beta), giving $b^\star = [\mathbb{E}[w(X)^{-1/2}]/(2\sqrt\pi n\int w^2 (f_X'')^2)]^{2/5}$. $\mathbb{E}[w(X)^{-1/2}]$ is an exact count moment; the curvature $\int w^2 (f_X'')^2$ is a grid quadrature of the pilot KDE's $\hat f''_{\tilde h}$ (pilot $\tilde h = 1.3 \min(\hat\sigma_X, \operatorname{IQR}/1.349) n^{-1/9}$ on a robust scale, the variance inflation $R(\varphi'') \mathbb{E}[w^2]/(n \tilde h^5)$ removed), which — unlike the integration-by-parts $\psi_4$ U-statistic — stays valid where $f_X(0) > 0$ (the $w \to 0$ boundary weight suppresses the pilot's edge leakage). A deterministic functional of the counts (mergeable). The symmetric :sj / :isj / :aic do not apply; a fully data-adaptive curvature pilot is a recorded refinement.
  • Consistency claim. with $\hat\sigma_X \to \sigma_X$ and the Gaussian reference $R(f_X^{(q)}) = (2q-1)!!/(2^{q+1}\sqrt{\pi} \sigma_X^{2q+1})$, $C(K,d)$ is the exact AMISE-optimal constant: degree 0/1 gives $h_{\mathrm{AMISE}} \sim n^{-1/5}$ (MISE $\sim n^{-4/5}$), degree 2 the higher-order $h_{\mathrm{AMISE}} \sim n^{-1/9}$ (MISE $\sim n^{-8/9}$); the Gaussian/degree-0 case is Silverman $(4/3)^{1/5}$, the Epanechnikov $\approx 2.34$.
  • Test. test/bandwidth.jl (Gaussian degree 0 reproduces Silverman, degree 1 equals degree 0, the Epanechnikov $h$ is $2.214\times$ the Gaussian, degree 2 matches the closed-form $n^{-1/9}$ rule and exceeds degree 0; coverage-gap and sample-size errors; plus the Sheather–Jones tests — SJ $\approx$ the normal reference on Gaussian data, much tighter on a bimodal mixture, the exact $\delta(K)$ kernel ratio, mergeability, the degree $\ge 2$ gap; and the AIC tests — a stable optimum below the normal reference on Gaussian data, far smaller on a bimodal mixture, the exact $\delta(K)$ ratio, mergeability, and the deliberately-absent CV rules erroring; and the asymmetric Chen plug-in — :normal reproduces the $\hat\sigma_X n^{-2/5}$ rule of thumb, :plugin lowers the ISE ($\ge 1.25\times$ on log-normal, $\ge 1.11\times$ on gamma; $\approx 1.4\text{–}1.7\times$ typically, seed-audited) and stays $\approx$ neutral on the near-exponential boundary case, gamma and beta kernels, mergeability, and the inapplicable :sj/:isj/:aic and $\texttt{degree} \ge 1$ rules erroring; the Improved Sheather–Jones tests (:isj $\approx$ the normal reference on Gaussian, $< 0.5\times$ it on a bimodal mixture, the exact $\delta(K)$ ratio, mergeability); and the Marron–Wand claw stress, where the data-driven :sj / :isj resolve the five spikes the normal reference oversmooths — $\approx$ an order of magnitude lower ISE) and test/oracles.jl (the kernel-aware ratio on the $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ oracle).

Local-linear conditional mean (§4.2) — conditional_mean

  • Estimand. $m(x) = \mathbb{E}[Y \mid X = x] = \int y f_{Y\mid X}(y \mid x)\,\mathrm{d}y$, the $L^2(\mathbb{P}_X)$ projection of the response onto the conditioning variable.

  • Estimator. the local-linear fit $\hat m(x) = (U_2 V_0 - U_1 V_1)/(U_0 U_2 - U_1^2)$ from the kernel-weighted power-sums $U_p = \sum_i K_h(X_i-x)(X_i-x)^p$ and $V_p = \sum_i K_h(X_i-x)(X_i-x)^p Y_i$ — recovered from the joint Histogram2D's per-x-column counts and response totals $\sum Y$ (exact from the per-cell power-sums); the $g = \mathrm{identity}$ case of conditional_expectation.

  • Consistency claim (interior $x$, $h \to 0$, $nh \to \infty$):

    \[\operatorname{bias}\hat m(x) \approx \tfrac{1}{2} h^2 \mu_2(K) m''(x), \qquad \operatorname{Var}\hat m(x) \approx \frac{R(K) \sigma^2(x)}{n h f_X(x)},\]

    giving the optimal rate $\hat m_{h^\star(x)}(x) - m(x) = O_p(n^{-2/5})$ at $h^\star(x) \sim n^{-1/5}$.

  • Test. test/conditionalexpectations.jl — the bivariate-Gaussian oracle $Y = \rho X + \sqrt{1-\rho^2} \varepsilon$ (so $\mathbb{E}[Y\mid X=x] = \rho x$): conditional_mean recovers the slope to $\lesssim 2\times10^{-2}$, with the degree (0/1/2) and kernel options exercised (degree-2 and Epanechnikov both recover the linear mean). test/conditional_convergence.jl — the $n^{-2/5}$ rate: on a curved-mean oracle ($m(x) = \sin x$, so the local-linear fit carries a genuine $O(h^2)$ bias) at $h \propto n^{-1/5}$ the integrated squared error falls at $n^{-4/5}$ (the $n^{-2/5}$ RMSE), the fitted log–log slope $\approx -0.80$.

Conditional variance (§4.2) — conditional_variance

  • Estimand. $\sigma^2(x) = \operatorname{Var}(Y \mid X = x) = \mathbb{E}[Y^2 \mid X = x] - m(x)^2$, with $m(x)$ the conditional mean above.
  • Estimator (method = :residual, the default; Fan & Yao 1998). The two-stage residual-based estimator: fit $\hat m$ at each x-column center $c^x_j$, form the per-column squared-residual total $\sum (Y - \hat m_j)^2 = \sum Y^2 - 2\hat m_j \sum Y + \hat m_j^2 N_j$ ($\hat m_j = \hat m(c^x_j)$, exact from the stored column sums), and take a second local fit of it, $\hat\sigma^2(x) = \widehat{\mathbb{E}}[(Y - \hat m(X))^2 \mid X = x]$. The :plugin alternative is the simple difference $\widehat{\mathbb{E}}[Y^2 \mid X = x] - \hat m(x)^2$; both clamped non-negative.
  • Consistency claim. both are consistent for $\sigma^2(x)$. The plug-in carries the local-polynomial smoothing bias of the curved $\mathbb{E}[Y^2\mid X = x]$ — an $O(h^2)$ term driven by $m''$ and the curvature of $\mathbb{E}[Y^2\mid X = x]$, large where $m$ curves. The residual-based estimator fits the far flatter $\mathbb{E}[(Y - m(X))^2\mid X = x] = \sigma^2(x)$, whose $O(h^2)$ bias $\propto (\sigma^2)''$ is much smaller, so it removes the $m''$-driven bias.
  • Standard error (conditional_variance_standard_error; paired conditional_variance_with_standard_error). The residual approximation $\operatorname{SE}(\hat\sigma^2(x)) = \sqrt{\ell(x) (\mu_4(x) - \sigma^4(x))}$ — since $\hat\sigma^2(x)$ is a conditional mean of the squared residual $(Y-\hat m(x))^2$, it is the conditional_expectation_standard_error of that response, with the 4th central conditional moment $\mu_4(x) = \mathbb{E}[(Y-m(x))^4\mid X = x]$ read off the order-2 $y$-power-sums by the $y$-quadrature of the smooth $(Y-\hat m)^4$ — so the $O(\Delta_y^2)$ quadrature captures the conditional kurtosis with no 4th-moment storage. Leading-order, common to the :residual and :plugin variance methods; tested in test/conditionalexpectations.jl by Monte-Carlo calibration on the Gaussian and a heavy-tailed Student-$\mathrm{t}_5$ response (the heavier $\mu_4$ widens the band), the $n^{-1/2}$ scaling contract, and paired consistency.
  • Test. test/conditionalexpectations.jl — recovers $1-\rho^2$ on the (linear-mean) bivariate-Gaussian oracle to a few percent, and on a heteroscedastic curved-mean oracle ($X\sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$, $m(x)=x^2$, $\sigma^2(x)=0.2+0.1x^2$) the residual estimator recovers $\sigma^2(x)$ (degree 2) and beats the plug-in in the curved tail (the plug-in inflated $>1.5\times$ by the $m''$ curvature); the universal contract via check_point_estimator (scalar $=$ vector, functional of the merged summary, $\mathrm{NaN}$ off-support). Its rate is the same $n^{-2/5}$ local-regression rate (a second local-polynomial fit, of the squared residuals); it shares the conditional_mean slope test (test/conditional_convergence.jl) rather than carrying a separate one.

Conditional expectation of a functional (§4.2) — conditional_expectation

  • Estimand. $\mathbb{E}[g(Y) \mid X = x] = \int g(y) f_{Y\mid X}(y \mid x)\,\mathrm{d}y$, the conditional expectation of a smooth functional of the response ($g = \mathrm{identity}$ the regression function $m(x)$; $g(y) = y^2$ gives $\mathbb{E}[Y^2\mid X = x]$).
  • Estimator. a local-polynomial regression in $x$ (as for conditional_mean) of a per-$x$-column $y$-quadrature response $\sum_i g(Y_i)$ formed from the stored order-2 $y$-power-sums by the §4.B quadrature ($a_0 n_{jl} + a_1 S_{01} + a_2 S_{02}$ for cell $(j, l)$, with $a_p$ the degree-2 interpolant coefficients of $g$ on the $y$-bin); the estimate is $\hat\beta_0$. It composes the two atoms — power-sum quadrature in $y$ then kernel-weighting in $x$ — and reduces to conditional_mean at $g = \mathrm{identity}$ (folded in exactly).
  • Consistency claim. the $x$-side carries conditional_mean's local-regression bias/variance (rate $n^{-2/5}$); the $y$-quadrature is exact for $g$ of degree $\le 2$ in $y$ and $O(\Delta_y^2)$ for a general smooth $g$ (the fixed order-2 $y$-power-sums of Histogram2D).
  • Test. test/conditionalexpectations.jl — the bivariate-Gaussian oracle $\mathbb{E}[\cos Y\mid X = x] = \cos(\rho x) e^{-(1-\rho^2)/2}$ (and $\sin$ likewise) to $\lesssim 2.5\times10^{-2}$; the exact fold $\texttt{conditional\_mean}(x) = \texttt{conditional\_expectation}(\mathrm{identity}, x)$; the :plugin variance equal to $\mathbb{E}[Y^2\mid X = x] - \mathbb{E}[Y\mid X = x]^2$ via the general reader; scalar $=$ vector; the universal contract via check_point_estimator.

Conditional pushforward density (§4.2) — conditional_pushforward_density

  • Estimand. $f_{g(Y)\mid X}(z \mid x) = \sum_{y: g(y)=z} f_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x)/|g'(y)|$, the density of the transformed response $Z = g(Y)$ given $X = x$ (change of variables on the conditional law).
  • Estimator. the conditional KDE of $g(Y)$ written as a conditional expectation, $\hat f_{g(Y)\mid X}(z\mid x) = \widehat{\mathbb{E}}[K_{h_z}(g(Y)-z)\mid X = x]$ — one conditional_expectation of the composed test function $y\mapsto K_{h_z}(g(y)-z)$, no $g^{-1}$ and no explicit Jacobian; a non-monotone $g$ contributes from every preimage, $g'=0$ is an integrable caustic. Two smoothing directions: the $x$-conditioning regression (h_x, kernel_x, degree) and the $z$ smoothing (h_z, kernel_z). Its standard error conditional_pushforward_density_standard_error (with the paired conditional_pushforward_density_with_standard_error) is the two-term $\sqrt{\ell(x) [R(K_z)\hat f_{g(Y)\mid X}/h_z - \hat f_{g(Y)\mid X}^2]}$ — the local-fit leverage $\ell(x)$ times the variance of $K_{h_z}(g(Y) - z)$ (analytic $R(K_z)$, since the order-2 $y$-quadrature cannot resolve $K_{h_z}^2$) — coinciding with conditional_density_standard_error at $g = \mathrm{identity}$, degree = 0.
  • Consistency claim. the standard KDE bias–variance in $z$ (over the $y$-quadrature), with a symmetric $K_z$ giving $\int\hat f_{g(Y)\mid X}\,\mathrm dz = 1$ exactly; needs $g$ smooth on the $y$-bin scale and $h_z \gtrsim |g'| \Delta_y$. At $g = \mathrm{identity}$, degree = 0 it coincides with the double-kernel conditional_density (up to the within-$y$-cell $O(\Delta_y^2)$ correction).
  • Test. test/conditionalexpectations.jl — $Z = e^Y\mid X = x \sim \mathrm{Lognormal}(\rho x, 1-\rho^2)$ recovered pointwise (to $8\%$) with $\int\hat f_{g(Y)\mid X}\,\mathrm dz \approx 1$; scalar $=$ vector; the positive-$h_z$ guard; the degree-0 identity-$g$ coincidence with conditional_density.

Conditional expectation standard error (§4.2) — conditional_expectation_standard_error

  • Estimand. the sampling standard error of $\widehat{\mathbb{E}}[g(Y)\mid X = x]$, $\operatorname{SE} = \sqrt{\ell(x) \operatorname{Var}(g(Y)\mid X = x)}$ with the local-regression leverage $\ell(x) = \sum_j N_j \omega_j(x)^2$ ($\omega_j$ the effective weights of the fit's $\hat\beta_0$) and $\operatorname{Var}(g(Y)\mid X = x) = \mathbb{E}[g^2\mid X = x] - \mathbb{E}[g\mid X = x]^2$.
  • Estimator. $\sqrt{\hat\ell(x) (\widehat{\mathbb{E}}[g^2\mid X = x] - \widehat{\mathbb{E}}[g\mid X = x]^2)}$, the leverage-times-noise-scale readout: $\hat\ell(x) = e_0^\top U^{-1} W U^{-1} e_0$ with $U$ the design Hankel $[U_{r+s}]_{r,s=0}^d$ and $W = [W_{r+s}]_{r,s=0}^d$ the Hankel of the squared-kernel moments $W_p = \sum_j N_j K_h(c_j-x)^2 (c_j-x)^p$ (the LocalPolynomial._leverage primitive — the reference mechanism for the kernel-family SE), times the plug-in conditional variance of $g(Y)$ (two conditional_expectation readouts). It is the exact analog of the marginal expectation_standard_error, $\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(g(X))/n}$, with the global-mean leverage $1/n$ replaced by $\sum_j N_j \omega_j^2$; at $g = \mathrm{identity}$ it is the conditional mean's standard error. The paired conditional_expectation_with_standard_error returns this error with the estimate $\widehat{\mathbb{E}}[g(Y)\mid X = x]$ from one shared fit (the $(\texttt{value}, \texttt{standard\_error})$ NamedTuple).
  • Consistency claim. it estimates the leading-order sampling variance of the local fit (not the $O(h^2)$ smoothing bias); at $g = \mathrm{identity}$ the leading term is $R(K)\sigma^2(x)/(n h f_X(x))$.
  • Test. test/conditionalexpectations.jl — a Monte-Carlo check: the predicted SE (from one dataset) matches the empirical standard deviation of conditional_mean across $J$ independent datasets (to $20\%$ at the density peak, where the finite-$h$ leverage corrections are smallest); the leading-order closed form; positivity, tail growth, off-support NaN, general $g$, scalar $=$ vector.

Response-aware local bandwidth (§4.5) — bandwidth(::Histogram2D, x; rule = :local)

  • Estimand. the AMSE-optimal (asymptotic mean squared error) local bandwidth for the conditional mean, balancing the local-linear squared bias against the variance at each $x$:

    \[h^\star(x) = \biggl[\frac{R(K) \sigma^2(x)}{\mu_2(K)^2 m''(x)^2 f_X(x) n}\biggr]^{1/5}.\]

  • Estimator. plug in the three $x$-local quantities from the one joint summary, each at a pilot bandwidth: $\hat\sigma^2(x)$ (the Fan–Yao conditional_variance), the curvature $\hat m''(x) = 2\hat\beta_2$ (the degree-2 LocalPolynomial jet), and $\hat f_X(x)$ (the x-marginal KDE); $R(K)$, $\mu_2(K)$ by quadrature. The default pilot is the curvature-rate rule of thumb $\hat\sigma_X n^{-1/9}$ (wider than $h^\star(x)$, so the second derivative is stable); where the regression is locally linear ($m'' \to 0$) the optimal $h$ is unbounded, capped at the x-range.

  • Consistency claim. with consistent pilots $\hat\sigma^2(x) \to \sigma^2(x)$, $\hat m'' \to m''$, $\hat f_X \to f_X$, the plug-in is consistent for $h^\star(x) \sim n^{-1/5}$; each pilot enters through a weak ($1/5$-power) factor, so $h^\star(x)$ is robust to pilot error.

  • Test. test/localbandwidth.jl — the quadratic-mean Gaussian oracle ($X\sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$, $Y = X^2 + \sigma\varepsilon$: $m''=2$, $\sigma^2$ constant, $f_X = \varphi$) where $h^\star(x)$ is closed-form: recovered near the centre to $\lesssim 12\%$, positive/finite on support, $\mathrm{NaN}$ off it, scalar $=$ vector, default-pilot sanity, and mergeability.

Conditional & transition density (§4.3) — conditional_density, transition_density

  • Estimand. $f_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x) = f_{X,Y}(x,y)/f_X(x)$, and the two-time transition density $p_\tau(y\mid x) = f_{Y(t+\tau)\mid X(t)}(y\mid x)$ (the same object on a lag-$\tau$ joint).

  • Estimator. the binned double-kernel (Rosenblatt 1969; Hyndman, Bashtannyk & Grunwald 1996), with a conditioning kernel $K_x$ (kernel_x, of bandwidth $h_x$ given by h_x, in $x$) and a response kernel $K_y$ (kernel_y, of bandwidth $h_y$ given by h_y, in $y$); each $\int K = 1$, default gaussian_kernel: from the cell counts $n_{jl}$ at centers $(c^x_j, c^y_l)$ with $N_j = \sum_l n_{jl}$,

    \[\hat f_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x) = \frac{\sum_{j,l} n_{jl} K_{h_x}(c^x_j - x) K_{h_y}(c^y_l - y)}{\sum_j N_j K_{h_x}(c^x_j - x)} ,\]

    computed as $\hat f_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x) = \bigl(\sum_l P_l K_y((c^y_l - y)/h_y)\bigr) / (h_y D)$ for a symmetric $K_y$ (the surviving $1/h_y$ from $K_{h_y}$), with the conditioning-kernel-weighted profile $P_l = \sum_j n_{jl} K_{h_x}(c^x_j - x)$ and $D = \sum_l P_l$. The conditioning-kernel weighting cancels in the ratio (so kernel_x only weights the local window); for a symmetric $K_y$ the $1/h_y$ makes $\int \hat f_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x)\,\mathrm dy = 1$ exactly ($\int K = 1$). For a bounded / positive response an asymmetric $K_y$ = kernel_y (gamma / beta; Chen 1999, 2000), of scale $b$ given by h_y, is instead a density in the data argument, $\hat f_{Y\mid X} = \bigl(\sum_l P_l K_y(c^y_l; y, b)\bigr)/D$ — boundary-bias-free in $y$ but only $\approx$-normalized, made exact by the normalize renormalization.

  • Consistency claim ($h_x, h_y \to 0$, $n h_x h_y \to \infty$): for symmetric kernels the pointwise bias is $\tfrac{1}{2} h_x^2 \mu_2(K_x)\bigl[\partial_{xx} f_{Y\mid X} + 2 \partial_x f_{Y\mid X} \partial_x f_X / f_X\bigr] + \tfrac{1}{2} h_y^2 \mu_2(K_y) \partial_{yy} f_{Y\mid X}$ and the variance $R(K_x)R(K_y) f_{Y\mid X}/(n h_x h_y f_X)$, giving the slower conditional rate $O(n^{-2/3})$ at the optimal $h \sim n^{-1/6}$ (the bias is worst at the conditional mode). An asymmetric $K_y$ carries the boundary-uniform $O(b)$ Chen $y$-bias instead of the $O(h_y^2)$ interior term — the price of consistency at the $y = 0$ edge a symmetric kernel cannot reach.

  • Test. test/conditionaldensity.jl — the bivariate-Gaussian fixture $Y\mid X=x \sim \mathcal{N}(\rho x, 1-\rho^2)$: $\int \hat f_{Y\mid X}\,\mathrm dy \approx 1$ and pointwise recovery to $3\times10^{-2}$; the scalar/vector forms, the transition_density equivalence, off-support NaN, bandwidth checks, and mergeability; plus the asymmetric-response oracle ($X\sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$, $Y\sim\mathrm{Exp}(1)\perp X$ $\Rightarrow$ $f_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x)=e^{-y}$), where the gamma kernel_y recovers the density at the boundary $y\approx0$ that the symmetric Gaussian leaks low, normalize driving $\int\hat f_{Y\mid X}\,\mathrm dy$ to 1. test/oracles.jl — the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck oracle: transition_density recovers the closed-form $p_\tau(y\mid x) = \mathcal{N}(x e^{-\kappa\tau}, \sigma_X^2(1-e^{-2\kappa\tau}))$ to $4\times10^{-2}$ (the $O(h^2)$ mode bias). test/conditional_convergence.jl — the $n^{-2/3}$ rate: on the bivariate-Gaussian oracle, with $h_x = h_y \propto n^{-1/6}$, the ISE in $y$ at $x_0 = 0$ falls with fitted log–log slope $\approx -2/3$ (reaching the 2-D asymptotic regime needs $n \gtrsim 10^5$, so the n-range starts higher than the 1-D density test).

Conditional density standard error (§4.3) — conditional_density_standard_error, conditional_density_with_standard_error

  • Estimand. the pointwise standard error of $\hat f_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x)$, $\operatorname{SE}(y\mid x) = \sqrt{\operatorname{Var}\hat f_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x)}$ with the two-term double-kernel variance $\operatorname{Var}\hat f_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x) \approx R(K_x)/(n h_x f_X(x)) \cdot [R(K_y) f_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x)/h_y - f_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x)^2]$ (Hyndman, Bashtannyk & Grunwald 1996): the sum of the squared $x$-weights times the variance of one response-kernel term.
  • Estimator. $\sqrt{R(K_x)/(h_x D) \cdot [R(K_y) \hat f_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x)/h_y - \hat f_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x)^2]}$ from the recovered $\hat f_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x)$, the roughnesses $R(K) = \int K^2$ (by quadrature), and the $x$-window denominator $D = \sum_j N_j K_{h_x}(c^x_j - x) = n \hat f_X(x)$ the estimator already forms. It is the two-dimensional analog of density_standard_error, the $x$-window effective sample size $n h_x \hat f_X(x)$ replacing $n$. transition_density_standard_error is the same on a lag-$\tau$ joint; conditional_density_with_standard_error returns the $(\texttt{value}, \texttt{standard\_error})$ NamedTuple from one profile. Symmetric kernels (the asymmetric-$K_y$ variance a recorded gap).
  • Consistency claim. it estimates the leading double-kernel sampling variance (not the $O(h^2)$ smoothing bias); the two-term form matches the finite-sample spread, and the relative error $\operatorname{SE}/\hat f_{Y\mid X}$ is $O\bigl((n h_x h_y)^{-1/2}\bigr)$ — the 2-D effective sample size.
  • Test. test/conditionaldensity.jl — the SE equals the two-term closed form (with the true $f_{Y\mid X}$, $f_X$) to $6\%$; a Monte-Carlo check (predicted SE $=$ the empirical spread of $\hat f_{Y\mid X}(0\mid 0)$ across 200 independent datasets to $12\%$); the exact $n^{-1/2}$ copy-merge scaling and the asymptotic $h_y^{-1/2}$ bandwidth scaling; the paired reader and the transition_density aliases match the separate calls; off-support NaN, and the asymmetric-kernel gap throws.

Power-sum quadrature — expectation of a functional (§4.B) — expectation

  • Estimand. the expectation of a smooth functional of $X$, $\mathbb{E}[g(X)] = \int g(x) f_X(x)\,\mathrm dx$.
  • Estimator. the power-sum quadrature $\widehat{\mathbb{E}}[g] = \tfrac{1}{n} \sum_j \sum_{p=0}^{\texttt{order}} a_p^{(j)} S_p^{(j)}$ ($S_0^{(j)} = n_j$), where $a_p^{(j)}$ are the coefficients (in powers of $x - c_j$) of the degree-$\texttt{order}$ polynomial interpolant of $g$ on bin $j$ — a Chebyshev–Lobatto interpolant, its inverse Vandermonde precomputed once (uniform bins), so no derivatives of $g$ are formed. For a monomial $g = x^p$ ($p \le \texttt{order}$) the interpolant is exact and $a_k^{(j)}$ is the binomial $\binom{p}{k} c_j^{p-k}$, so the readout reduces to the shift formula — mean, var, central_moment are this estimator, and share the Expectations._quadrature atom.
  • Consistency claim. exact (to floating-point) for polynomial $g$ of degree $\le \texttt{order}$, independent of the bin width; $O(\Delta^{ \texttt{order}+1})$ for general smooth $g$ (the interpolation remainder). The estimand needs $g$ smooth on the bin scale and the mass inside the range.
  • Test. test/expectations.jl — the Gaussian oracles $\mathbb{E}[\cos X] = e^{-1/2}$, $\mathbb{E}[\sin X] = 0$ (MGF/trig); polynomial exactness ($\widehat{\mathbb{E}}[g] = \tfrac{1}{n}\sum_i g(X_i)$ to $10^{-10}$ for degree $\le \texttt{order}$); the binning error vs the raw empirical mean $< 10^{-6}$ for a smooth $g$; the moment unification ($\mathbb{E}[X^p]$ equals the recovered moment); mergeability; empty $\Rightarrow$ NaN.

Power-sum quadrature — sampling standard error (§4.B) — expectation_standard_error

  • Estimand. the sampling standard error of $\widehat{\mathbb{E}}[g]$, $\operatorname{SE} = \sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(g(X))/n}$ with $\operatorname{Var}(g(X)) = \mathbb{E}[g^2] - \mathbb{E}[g]^2$.
  • Estimator. $\sqrt{(\widehat{\mathbb{E}}[g^2] - \widehat{\mathbb{E}}[g]^2)/n}$ — both moments the same quadrature ($\mathbb{E}[g^2]$ is expectation with the smooth integrand $g^2$). It is the plug-in sampling error of the empirical mean of $g(X)$; the $O(\Delta^{ \texttt{order}+1})$ quadrature bias is separate and not included. The paired expectation_with_standard_error returns this error with the estimate $\widehat{\mathbb{E}}[g]$ from one shared quadrature pass (the $(\texttt{value}, \texttt{standard\_error})$ NamedTuple).
  • Consistency claim. for $g = \mathrm{identity}$ it is exactly $\sqrt{\hat\mu_2/n}$ ($\sqrt{\hat\mu_2}$ the uncorrected standard deviation); in general the plug-in $\widehat{\operatorname{Var}(g(X))}$ is consistent as $\Delta \to 0$.
  • Test. test/expectations.jl — $\operatorname{SE}(\mathrm{identity}) = \sqrt{\hat\mu_2/n}$ to $10^{-10}$; $\operatorname{SE}(\cos)$ matches the raw empirical $\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(\cos X)/n}$; positivity; empty $\Rightarrow$ NaN.

Power-sum quadrature — pushforward density (§4.B) — pushforward_density

  • Estimand. the density of the transformed variable $Z = g(X)$, the pushforward $f_{g(X)}(z) = \sum_{x: g(x)=z} f_X(x)/|g'(x)|$ (change of variables, summed over preimages).
  • Estimator. the kernel density estimate of the pushforward written as an expectation, $\hat f_{g(X)}(z) = \tfrac{1}{n} \sum_i K_h(g(X_i) - z) = \widehat{\mathbb{E}}[K_h(g(X) - z)]$, so it is one expectation of the composed test function $x \mapsto K_h(g(x) - z)$ — no $g^{-1}$ and no explicit Jacobian. A non-monotone $g$ contributes from every preimage automatically; $g' = 0$ is an integrable caustic. For a symmetric $K$ ($\int K = 1$) the surviving $1/h$ gives $\int \hat f_{g(X)}\,\mathrm dz = 1$ exactly. Its standard error pushforward_density_standard_error (with the paired pushforward_density_with_standard_error) is the expectation_standard_error of that same integrand — the two-term $\sqrt{R(K)\hat f_{g(X)}/(nh) - \hat f_{g(X)}^2/n}$, coinciding with density_standard_error at $g = \mathrm{identity}$.
  • Consistency claim. the standard KDE bias–variance in $z$ (bias $\tfrac{1}{2} h^2 \mu_2(K) f_{g(X)}''$, variance $R(K) f_{g(X)}/(nh)$), provided $g$ is smooth on the bin scale and $h \gtrsim |g'| \Delta$ (so $K_h\circ g$ is resolved across a bin).
  • Test. test/expectations.jl — $Z = e^X$ with $X\sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)$ recovers the $\mathrm{Lognormal}(0,1)$ pdf (pointwise to $6\%$, $\int\hat f_{g(X)}\,\mathrm dz \approx 1$), and the non-monotone $Z = X^2$ recovers the $\chi^2_1$ pdf in the bulk (both preimages $\pm\sqrt z$ contributing); scalar $=$ vector, positive-$h$ check, mergeability.
