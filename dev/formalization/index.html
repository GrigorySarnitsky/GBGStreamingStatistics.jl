Formalization

The typed statements behind the prose. Each operation gets one entry stating its estimand, its estimator, and the consistency claim — the bias and variance with every normalization constant pinned — each linked to the test that verifies the convergence rate numerically. The chain is prose ↔ formalization ↔ test: the prose (docstrings, ARCHITECTURE.md) agrees with the typed statement, the statement is verified against the code, and the linked test enforces it.

A nontrivial claim with no entry here, or an entry with no test link, is a recorded coverage gap (CLAUDE.md, Definition of done).

Throughout, $K$ is the smoothing kernel with $\int K = 1$, $\mu_2(K) = \int u^2 K(u)\,\mathrm du$ its second moment, and $R(K) = \int K^2$ its roughness; $h$ is the bandwidth, $n$ the number of samples, $f_X$ the marginal density of the conditioning variable.

The implemented entries are test-linked; a forthcoming operation gets its entry in the same shape (estimand / estimator / consistency / test) before it lands.

Per-bin power-sums about the bin center (Histograms, the summaries)

  • Object. per bin, the count and the power-sums about the fixed bin center $S_p = \sum_{i\in\text{bin}}(X_i - c_j)^p$ for $p = 1 \dots \texttt{order}$ (and order-2 bivariate $S_{rs}$ per cell for Histogram2D). Intrinsically finite-sample — no population estimand.

  • Accumulation. an atomic scatter-add (observe! / observe_pairs!): one work-item per sample adds $(X_i - c_j)^p$ to its bin's $S_p$, in the accumulator type (Float64 by default). merge! is elementwise addition (same binning $\Rightarrow$ same centers). The local offsets are well-conditioned, so no compensated summation is used.

  • Mergeability (a monoid morphism). Write $(\mathcal{M}, \uplus, \varnothing)$ — finite sample collections (multisets over the sample space) under disjoint union, $\varnothing$ the empty collection — and $(\mathcal{S}, \oplus, 0)$ — summaries under merge! (elementwise addition of counts and power-sums), $0$ the all-zero summary. Both are commutative monoids. Accumulation $T : \mathcal{M} \to \mathcal{S}$ is the unique homomorphism extending the single-sample scatter $t$ (one sample adds $1$ to its bin's count and $(X_i - c_j)^p$ to that bin's $S_p$), so $T(A) = \bigoplus_{x \in A} t(x)$ and the square

    \[\begin{CD} \mathcal{M} \times \mathcal{M} @>{\uplus}>> \mathcal{M} \\ @V{T \times T}VV @VV{T}V \\ \mathcal{S} \times \mathcal{S} @>{\oplus}>> \mathcal{S} \end{CD}\]

    commutes: $T(A \uplus B) = T(A) \oplus T(B)$, which is accumulate-then-merge $=$ accumulate-together. Commutativity and associativity of $\oplus$ then make any reduction order or tree (multi-thread, multi-device, time-chunk) yield the same summary. The morphism is the unnormalized summary only: the normalize-and-smooth readout $E_h$ (the $1/n$, the shift formula, the kernel) that turns a summary into estimates is not a homomorphism — which is exactly why merge! has no population estimand (pool summaries first, estimate once, never the reverse). Exact for the integer counts; the Float64 power-sums merge up to round-off (non-associative floating-point addition), so the test asserts $=$ on counts and $\approx$ on the $S_p$.

  • Recovery. the global central moments are the shift formula

    \[\sum_i (X_i - \bar X)^p = \sum_j \sum_{k=0}^{p} \binom{p}{k}\,(c_j - \bar X)^{p-k}\, S_k^{(j)}, \qquad S_0^{(j)} = n_j, \quad \bar X = \tfrac1n \sum_j \bigl(c_j n_j + S_1^{(j)}\bigr),\]

    which is exact (the $S_p$ use the actual $X_i$, not the center) and numerically stable for the even $p$ the consumers need; the recovered moments are exposed publicly as mean, var, std, and central_moment — var and std apply Bessel's $n-1$ correction by default (matching Statistics), corrected = false the $1/n$ form.

  • Test. test/histograms.jl (recovered $M_2 \dots M_6$ match the direct sum to $10^{-8}$ and the true Gaussian moments to sampling error); test/histograms2d.jl (2D marginals + covariance); test/mergeability.jl (merge $=$ together); test/allocations.jl (bounded observe! / merge!); test/gpu/suite.jl (CPU-vs-AMD-GPU parity).

Marginal density (§4.1) — density

  • Estimand. $f_X = \mathrm{d}\mu_X/\mathrm{d}\lambda$, the Lebesgue density of $X$.
  • Estimator. the binned KDE $\hat f_h(x) = \frac{1}{nh}\sum_j n_j K\!\left(\frac{x-c_j}{h}\right)$ ($\int K = 1$, Gaussian $K$) from the bin counts $n_j$ at centers $c_j$, with $h$ from bandwidth (Silverman $h = (4/3)^{1/5}\hat\sigma\,n^{-1/5}$).
  • Consistency claim ($h \to 0$, $nh \to \infty$): $\operatorname{bias}\hat f_h(x) \approx \tfrac12 h^2 \mu_2(K)\, f_X''(x)$ and $\operatorname{Var}\hat f_h(x) \approx R(K)\,f_X(x)/(n h)$; the binning error $O(b^2)$ is dominated by the $O(h^2)$ smoothing bias when $b \ll h$.
  • Test. test/oracles.jl (recovers the $N(0,1)$ pdf to $< 3\times10^{-3}$ over $[-3,3]$, integrates to 1). test/density_convergence.jl — a representative analytic-PDF suite (Gaussian, uniform, exponential, Student-$t$ $\nu=3$ heavy tail, log-normal, bimodal: each recovered to $\sim 10^{-2}$ and integrating to $\approx 1$, the variable bandwidth tracking the $t$ heavy tail better than the fixed) and the convergence-rate test: the ISE falls at the theoretical $n^{-2p/(2p+1)}$ — slope $\approx -0.80$ for degree 0/1 ($p=2$), the steeper $\approx -0.87$ ($n^{-8/9}$, $p=4$) for degree 2, the Abramson variable bandwidth sharing the degree-0 order.

Local-log-polynomial & adaptive density (§4.1) — density's degree / adaptive

  • Estimand. $f_X$, exactly as for the degree-0 density above; the knobs change the estimator, not the target.
  • adaptive (Abramson variable bandwidth). A fixed-bandwidth pilot $\tilde f$ at $h$ sets per-bin bandwidths $h_j = h\,(\tilde f(c_j)/g)^{-1/2}$ ($g$ the geometric mean of $\tilde f$ over the sample), widening the kernel in the sparse tails: $\operatorname{bias} = O(h^4)$ at interior smooth $x$ (Abramson 1982), with far less tail variance than a fixed $h$.
  • degree ≥ 1 (local-log-polynomial). Fit $\log f$ by a degree-$d$ polynomial through a binned local Poisson likelihood — $\log \mathbb{E}[n_j] = \log(nb) + \sum_{l \le d} \beta_l (c_j-x)^l$, weights $K((c_j-x)/h_j)$, by Newton/IRLS — and report $\hat f(x) = e^{\hat\beta_0}$ (Loader 1996). Degree 1 is exact for an exponential density (tracks exponential tails), degree 2 for a Gaussian. The two knobs compose. On a bounded support the degree-0 KDE accepts bounds = (a, b), renormalizing by the kernel mass $\int_a^b K_h(x-u)\,\mathrm{d}u$ inside the support (Jones 1993) to remove the $O(1)$ boundary bias that otherwise halves the estimate at the edge.
  • Test. test/densityestimation.jl (every grid corner recovers the $N(0,1)$ pdf into the tail and integrates to $\approx 1$; the variable bandwidth resolves the sharp Laplace cusp the fixed KDE oversmooths; degree-1 beats the KDE in a Laplace tail).

Asymmetric kernels (§4.1) — density's gamma_kernel / beta_kernel

  • Estimand. $f_X$ on a bounded or positive support.
  • Estimator. the varying-shape estimate $\hat f(x) = \tfrac1n \sum_j n_j K(c_j;\,x,\,b)$ with $K(\cdot;x,b)$ the modified $\mathrm{Gamma}(\rho(x),\,b)$ density (gamma kernel, support $[0,\infty)$; Chen 2000) or the modified $\mathrm{Beta}$ density (beta kernel, support $[0,1]$; Chen 1999) — Chen's bias-reduced $f_2$ shapes (interior shape $x/b$, kernel mean $\approx x$, removing the $f'$ bias of the naive $x/b+1$ form); the support matches the data, so no mass leaks past the boundary. A reduction over the bin counts (mergeable); default $b = \hat\sigma\,n^{-2/5}$.
  • Consistency claim. bias $O(b)$ uniformly including at the boundary (where a symmetric kernel is $O(h)$ and inconsistent); interior variance $O(n^{-1}b^{-1/2})$; optimal $b \sim n^{-2/5}$, MISE $\sim n^{-4/5}$. Not self-normalizing (an $O(b)$ mass deviation). The modified $f_2$ shape lowers the MISE constant (Chen proves $\mathrm{MISE}(f_2) \le \mathrm{MISE}(f_1)$) — measured $\approx 40\%$ lower ISE than the naive form on a log-normal.
  • Test. test/densityestimation.jl (gamma recovers the exponential at the boundary, beating the symmetric KDE there; beta recovers $\mathrm{Uniform}(0,1)$ at both edges; integrates to $\approx 1$; mergeability; degree/adaptive/bounds rejected).

Parametric start & normalization (§4.1) — density's start / normalize

  • Estimand. $f_X$, exactly as above; start and normalize change the estimator, not the target.
  • Estimator (start, Hjort & Glad 1995). The semiparametric estimate $\hat f(x) = p(x;\hat\theta)\,\hat r_h(x)$, where $p(\cdot;\hat\theta)$ is a parametric start fit by method of moments from the recovered $\hat\mu$, $\hat\sigma^2$, and $\hat r_h(x) = \tfrac1{nh}\sum_j (n_j/p(c_j;\hat\theta))\,K((x-c_j)/h)$ is a KDE of the correction $r = f_X/p$ (the start's local relative error). Five starts are provided — normal_start, lognormal_start, gamma_start, exponential_start, inverse_gaussian_start — each a density whose parameters are fixed by the first two moments, so $\hat\theta$ is itself a post-hoc functional of the summary and the whole estimate is mergeable ($\hat\theta$ merges through the moments; the reweighted counts add). Degree-0, fixed-$h$, symmetric kernel.
  • Consistency claim. For a fixed start with bounded, twice-differentiable correction $r$, consistent for $f_X$ at $\operatorname{bias} = O(h^2)$, $\operatorname{var} = O(1/(nh))$ (MISE $\sim n^{-4/5}$), but with the leading bias governed by $r'' = (f_X/p)''$ in place of $f_X''$ and the variance $R(K)f_X(x)/(nh)$ unchanged to leading order. When the start is correct $r \equiv 1$, $r'' \equiv 0$: the leading bias vanishes and the estimate collapses to the parametric fit. So the start is a bias reduction that pays in the bias-dominated regime (skewed / heavy-tailed shapes, oversmoothed $h$), not in the large-$n$ limit where the KDE is already nearly unbiased.
  • Estimator (normalize). $\hat f / Z$ with $Z = \int \hat f$ over the summary's range intersected with the estimator's support, by the trapezoidal rule on a fine grid — a deterministic, mergeable functional of the estimate making $\int \hat f = 1$ exactly (the asymmetric kernels and a parametric start are not self-normalizing; the symmetric KDE already is, to the grid's $O(\Delta^2)$).
  • Test. test/densityestimation.jl (a normal start at a wide $h$ recovers the Gaussian peak the plain KDE flattens by $\approx 10\%$, ISE $> 20\times$ smaller; a log-normal start tracks log-normal data $> 3\times$ better than the symmetric KDE, and pointwise into the right tail; normalize drives $\int\hat f$ to $1$ for a start and for the gamma kernel; mergeability; the rejected knob combinations).

Pointwise standard error (§4.1) — density_standard_error

  • Estimand. the standard error of the density estimator, $\mathrm{SE}(x) = \sqrt{\operatorname{Var}\hat f_h(x)}$ with the KDE asymptotic variance $\operatorname{Var}\hat f_h(x) \approx R(K)\,f_X(x)/(nh)$.
  • Estimator. $\sqrt{R(K)\,\hat f_h(x)/(nh)}$ from the recovered $\hat f_h$, $n$, $h$, and $R(K) = \int K^2$ (by quadrature). The $z$-band $\hat f_h(x) \pm z\,\mathrm{SE}(x)$ is a variability band about $\mathbb{E}\hat f_h$, covering $f_X$ where the $O(h^2)$ smoothing bias is small. Degree 0/1 (the variable bandwidth and degree $\ge 2$ are a coverage gap).
  • Test. test/densityestimation.jl (the SE equals $\sqrt{R(K)\hat f/(nh)}$ exactly; the 95\% band covers the $N(0,1)$ truth over $\ge 75\%$ of the interior; the SE shrinks with $n$; vector / empty / error forms).

Bandwidth selection (§4.5) — bandwidth

  • Estimand. the AMISE-optimal bandwidth $h^\star = \arg\min_h \int \mathbb{E}[(\hat f_h(x) - f_X(x))^2]\,\mathrm{d}x$ for a kernel $K$ and local-polynomial degree $d$ — the asymptotically optimal smoothing scale, itself $O(n^{-1/(2p+1)})$ (a finite-sample selector, not a fixed population constant).
  • Estimator (rule = :normal, the kernel- and degree-aware normal-reference). $h = C(K,d)\,\hat\sigma\,n^{-1/(2p+1)}$ with bias order $p = 2$ (degree 0/1) or $p = 4$ (degree 2/3), and constant $C(K,d) = [W_{ikk}(K,d)\,2^{p+1}\sqrt{\pi}/(2p-1)!!]^{1/(2p+1)}$. The canonical factor is $W_{ikk}(K,d) = R(K)/\mu_2(K)^2$ at degree 0/1, and the fourth-order equivalent-kernel analog $W_{ikk} = (4!^2/8)\,R(K^\star)/\mu_4(K^\star)^2$ with $K^\star = K\,(n_4 - v^2 n_2)/(n_0 n_4 - n_2^2)$ at degree 2/3 (Loader 1996); here $R(K) = \int K^2$, $\mu_2(K) = \int u^2 K$ (by quadrature) and $n_k = \int v^k K$. $\hat\sigma$ and $n$ are read from the one summary.
  • Estimator (rule = :sj, the data-driven Sheather–Jones plug-in; degree 0/1). The curvature $\psi_4 = R(f'')$ and its $\psi_6$ pilot are estimated from the bin counts, $\hat\psi_r(g) = (n^2 g^{r+1})^{-1}\sum_{j,k} n_j n_k\,\varphi^{(r)}((c_j - c_k)/g)$ (Gaussian $\varphi$, the $n$ self-pairs removed), and the solve-the-equation bandwidth (Sheather & Jones 1991) is the root of $h = [R(\varphi)/(\mu_2(\varphi)^2\,\hat\psi_4(\alpha\,h^{5/7})\,n)]^{1/5}$; the eval kernel then multiplies by the canonical $\delta(K)/\delta(\varphi)$. Both functionals are reductions of the bin counts, hence mergeable; degree $\ge 2$ is a recorded coverage gap. The improved variant (rule = :isj; Botev, Grotowski & Kroese 2010) instead reads $\|f^{(s)}\|^2$ off the DCT of the bin counts and solves the AMISE fixed point $t = \xi\gamma^5(t)$ — also a mergeable functional of the counts, and robust on strongly multimodal data.
  • Estimator (rule = :aic, the fit-based information criterion; degree 0/1). Minimise $\mathrm{AIC}(h) = -2\sum_j n_j\log\hat f_h(c_j) + 2\,\mathrm{df}(h)$ of the binned KDE over a log-spaced $h$-grid, with $\mathrm{df}(h) = (K(0)/h)\sum_j n_j/(n\hat f_h(c_j))$; both terms diverge as $h\to0$, giving a stable interior optimum. Mergeable (a functional of the counts); the eval kernel multiplies by $\delta(K)/\delta(\varphi)$. The leave-one-out cross-validation selectors (likelihood-CV, least-squares LSCV) are a recorded coverage gap — binning ties drive their objective to $h\to0$.
  • Estimator (rule = :plugin, the asymmetric-kernel Chen plug-in; gamma_kernel / beta_kernel). The gamma / beta estimators take a scale $b$ (rate $n^{-2/5}$), not $h$; their AMISE (Chen 1999, 2000; modified $f_2$) is $A b^2 + C b^{-1/2}/n$ with $A = \tfrac14\int w^2 (f'')^2$, $C = (2\sqrt\pi)^{-1}\mathbb{E}[w(X)^{-1/2}]$ and local-scale weight $w(x) = x$ (gamma) or $x(1-x)$ (beta), giving $b^\star = [\mathbb{E}[w(X)^{-1/2}]/(2\sqrt\pi\,n\int w^2 (f'')^2)]^{2/5}$. $\mathbb{E}[w(X)^{-1/2}]$ is an exact count moment; the curvature $\int w^2 (f'')^2$ is a grid quadrature of the pilot KDE's $\hat f''_g$ (pilot $g = 1.3\,\hat\sigma\,n^{-1/9}$, the variance inflation $R(\varphi'')\,\mathbb{E}[w^2]/(n g^5)$ removed), which — unlike the integration-by-parts $\psi_4$ U-statistic — stays valid where $f(0) > 0$ (the $w \to 0$ boundary weight suppresses the pilot's edge leakage). A deterministic functional of the counts (mergeable). The symmetric :sj / :isj / :aic do not apply; a fully data-adaptive curvature pilot is a recorded refinement.
  • Consistency claim. with $\hat\sigma \to \sigma$ and the Gaussian reference $R(f^{(p)}) = (2p-1)!!/(2^{p+1}\sqrt{\pi}\,\sigma^{2p+1})$, $C(K,d)$ is the exact AMISE-optimal constant: degree 0/1 gives $h \sim n^{-1/5}$ (MISE $\sim n^{-4/5}$), degree 2 the higher-order $h \sim n^{-1/9}$ (MISE $\sim n^{-8/9}$); the Gaussian/degree-0 case is Silverman $(4/3)^{1/5}$, the Epanechnikov $\approx 2.34$.
  • Test. test/bandwidth.jl (Gaussian degree 0 reproduces Silverman, degree 1 equals degree 0, the Epanechnikov $h$ is $2.214\times$ the Gaussian, degree 2 matches the closed-form $n^{-1/9}$ rule and exceeds degree 0; coverage-gap and sample-size errors; plus the Sheather–Jones tests — SJ $\approx$ the normal reference on Gaussian data, much tighter on a bimodal mixture, the exact $\delta(K)$ kernel ratio, mergeability, the degree $\ge 2$ gap; and the AIC tests — a stable optimum below the normal reference on Gaussian data, far smaller on a bimodal mixture, the exact $\delta(K)$ ratio, mergeability, and the deliberately-absent CV rules erroring; and the asymmetric Chen plug-in — :normal reproduces the $\hat\sigma\,n^{-2/5}$ rule of thumb, :plugin lowers the ISE $\ge 1.4\times$ on log-normal and gamma data and stays $\approx$ neutral on the near-exponential boundary case, gamma and beta kernels, mergeability, and the inapplicable :sj/:isj/:aic and degree \ge 1 rules erroring) and test/oracles.jl (the kernel-aware ratio on the $N(0,1)$ oracle).

Local-linear conditional mean (§4.2) — conditional_mean

  • Estimand. $m(x) = \mathbb{E}[Y \mid X = x] = \int y\, f_{Y\mid X}(y \mid x)\,\mathrm{d}y$, the $L^2(\sigma(X))$ projection of the response onto the conditioning variable.

  • Estimator. the local-linear fit $\hat m_h(x) = (T_2 P_0 - T_1 P_1)/(T_0 T_2 - T_1^2)$ from the kernel-weighted power-sums $T_\ell = \sum_i K_h(X_i-x)(X_i-x)^\ell$ and $P_\ell = \sum_i K_h(X_i-x)(X_i-x)^\ell Y_i$ — recovered from the joint Histogram2D's per-X-column counts and response totals $\sum Y$ (exact from the per-cell power-sums).

  • Consistency claim (interior $x$, $h \to 0$, $nh \to \infty$):

    \[\operatorname{bias}\hat m_h(x) \approx \tfrac12 h^2 \mu_2(K)\, m''(x), \qquad \operatorname{Var}\hat m_h(x) \approx \frac{R(K)\,\sigma^2(x)}{n\,h\,f_X(x)},\]

    giving the optimal rate $\hat m_{h^\star}(x) - m(x) = O_p(n^{-2/5})$ at $h^\star \sim n^{-1/5}$.

  • Test. test/regression.jl — the bivariate-Gaussian oracle $Y = \rho X + \sqrt{1-\rho^2}\, \varepsilon$ (so $\mathbb{E}[Y\mid X=x] = \rho x$): conditional_mean recovers the slope to $\lesssim 2\times10^{-2}$; conditional_variance recovers $1-\rho^2$ to a few percent (the simple $\hat{\mathbb E}[Y^2\mid x]-\hat m^2$ estimator carries the local-linear smoothing bias of the curved $E[Y^2\mid x]$; the Fan–Yao residual refinement is forthcoming). The $n^{-2/5}$ rate test is forthcoming.

Conditional increment moments, drift & diffusion (§4.4) — increment_moment, kramers_moyal, drift, diffusion

  • Estimand. the conditional increment moment and the Kramers–Moyal coefficient

    \[M_n(x, \tau) = \mathbb{E}\!\left[(X_{t+\tau} - X_t)^n \mid X_t = x\right], \qquad D^{(n)}(x) = \frac{1}{n!}\lim_{\tau\to0} \frac{M_n(x,\tau)}{\tau},\]

    with kramers_moyal $\equiv D^{(n)}$, drift $\equiv D^{(1)}$, and diffusion $\equiv D^{(2)}$. Model-neutral — a conditional expectation of an increment power, no diffusion premise.

  • Estimator. conditioning on $X_t = x$ (so the increment is $\Delta X = Y - x$ with $Y = X_{t+\tau}$), the order-2 joint summary resolves

    \[M_1(x) = \hat m_h(x) - x, \quad M_2(x) = \widehat{\mathbb{E}}[Y^2\mid x] - 2x\,\hat m_h(x) + x^2, \quad \widehat D^{(1)} = \frac{M_1}{\tau}, \quad \widehat D^{(2)} = \frac{M_2}{2\tau},\]

    where $\hat m_h$ and $\widehat{\mathbb{E}}[Y^2\mid x]$ are the local-linear §4.2 fits (exact from the per-cell power-sums). $n \ge 3$ is a recorded coverage gap — it needs order-$n$ per-cell $Y$ power-sums.

  • Consistency claim. $\widehat D^{(1)}$ is consistent for the finite-$\tau$ drift at the conditional_mean rate, with no smoothing bias where $m(x)$ is linear in $x$; $\widehat D^{(2)}$ carries the local-linear bias from the curvature of $\mathbb{E}[Y^2\mid x]$ (the Fan–Yao residual refinement removes it). The finite-$\tau$ coefficients approach $D^{(n)}(x)$ as $\tau\to0$ up to the $O(\tau)$ finite-time bias (the Bell-polynomial correction, forthcoming).

  • Test. test/oracles.jl — the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck oracle (exact stationary lagged pairs from $\mathrm dX = -\theta X\,\mathrm dt + \sigma\,\mathrm dW$): drift recovers $x(e^{-\theta\tau}-1)/\tau$ to $\le 2\times10^{-2}$ and diffusion recovers the closed-form $[s^2(1-e^{-2\theta\tau}) + x^2(e^{-\theta\tau}-1)^2]/(2\tau)$ ($s^2 = \sigma^2/2\theta$) to $8\%$. test/markovinference.jl — the variance decomposition $M_2 = \operatorname{Var}(Y\mid x) + M_1^2$, the $1/(n!\,\tau)$ scaling, the $n\ge3$ and static-lag coverage-gap errors, and mergeability. The $n^{-2/5}$ rate test is forthcoming.

Conditional & transition density (§4.3) — conditional_density, transition_density

  • Estimand. $f_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x) = f_{X,Y}(x,y)/f_X(x)$, and the two-time transition density $p_\tau(y\mid x) = f_{X_{t+\tau}\mid X_t}(y\mid x)$ (the same object on a lag-$\tau$ joint).

  • Estimator. the binned double-kernel (Rosenblatt 1969; Hyndman, Bashtannyk & Grunwald 1996): from the cell counts $n_{jk}$ at centers $(c^x_j, c^y_k)$ with $N_j = \sum_k n_{jk}$,

    \[\hat f(y\mid x) = \frac{\sum_{j,k} n_{jk}\, K_{h_1}(c^x_j - x)\, K_{h_2}(c^y_k - y)}{\sum_j N_j\, K_{h_1}(c^x_j - x)} ,\]

    computed as $\hat f(y\mid x) = \big(\sum_k P_k\, K_{h_2}(c^y_k - y)\big) / (\sqrt{2\pi}\,h_2 D)$ with the X-kernel-weighted profile $P_k = \sum_j n_{jk} K_{h_1}(c^x_j - x)$ and $D = \sum_k P_k$. The X-kernel normalization cancels in the ratio; the surviving $1/(\sqrt{2\pi}\,h_2)$ makes $\int \hat f(y\mid x)\,\mathrm dy = 1$ exactly.

  • Consistency claim ($h_1, h_2 \to 0$, $n h_1 h_2 \to \infty$): the pointwise bias is $\tfrac12 h_1^2 \mu_2(K)\big[\partial_{xx} f_{Y\mid X} + 2\,\partial_x f_{Y\mid X}\,\partial_x f_X / f_X\big] + \tfrac12 h_2^2 \mu_2(K)\,\partial_{yy} f_{Y\mid X}$ and the variance $R(K)^2 f_{Y\mid X}/(n h_1 h_2 f_X)$, giving the slower conditional rate $O(n^{-2/3})$ at the optimal $h \sim n^{-1/6}$ (the bias is worst at the conditional mode).

  • Test. test/conditionaldensity.jl — the bivariate-Gaussian fixture $Y\mid X=x \sim N(\rho x, 1-\rho^2)$: $\int \hat f\,\mathrm dy \approx 1$ and pointwise recovery to $3\times10^{-2}$; the scalar/vector forms, the transition_density equivalence, off-support NaN, bandwidth checks, and mergeability. test/oracles.jl — the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck oracle: transition_density recovers the closed-form $p_\tau(y\mid x) = N(x e^{-\theta\tau},\, s^2(1-e^{-2\theta\tau}))$ to $4\times10^{-2}$ (the $O(h^2)$ mode bias). The $n^{-2/3}$ rate test is forthcoming.
