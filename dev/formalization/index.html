Formalization

The typed statements behind the prose. Each operation gets one entry stating its estimand, its estimator, and the consistency claim — the bias and variance with every normalization constant pinned — each linked to the test that verifies the convergence rate numerically. The chain is prose ↔ formalization ↔ test: the prose (docstrings, ARCHITECTURE.md) agrees with the typed statement, the statement is verified against the code, and the linked test enforces it.

A nontrivial claim with no entry here, or an entry with no test link, is a recorded coverage gap (CLAUDE.md, Definition of done).

Throughout, $K$ is the smoothing kernel with $\int K = 1$, $\mu_2(K) = \int u^2 K(u)\,\mathrm du$ its second moment, and $R(K) = \int K^2$ its roughness; $h$ is the bandwidth, $n$ the number of samples, $f_X$ the marginal density of the conditioning variable.

The entries are grouped by the lens of ARCHITECTURE.md §2: the atoms (the power-sums summary, the cone, the kernel-weighting atom & jet) first, then the two readout families — the kernel-weighting readouts (§4.A) and the power-sum quadrature readouts (§4.B). A forthcoming operation gets its entry — in the same shape (estimand / estimator / consistency / test) — when it lands, its estimand recorded in ARCHITECTURE.md §4 meanwhile.

Per-bin power-sums about the bin center (Histograms, the summaries)

  • Object. per bin, the count and the power-sums about the fixed bin center $S_p = \sum_{i\in\text{bin}}(X_i - c_j)^p$ for $p = 1 \dots \texttt{order}$ (and order-2 bivariate $S_{rs}$ per cell for Histogram2D). Intrinsically finite-sample — no population estimand.

  • Accumulation. an atomic scatter-add (observe! / observe_pairs!): one work-item per sample adds $(X_i - c_j)^p$ to its bin's $S_p$, in the accumulator type (Float64 by default). merge! is elementwise addition (same binning $\Rightarrow$ same centers). The local offsets are well-conditioned, so no compensated summation is used.

  • Mergeability (a monoid morphism). Write $(\mathcal{M}, \uplus, \varnothing)$ — finite sample collections (multisets over the sample space) under disjoint union, $\varnothing$ the empty collection — and $(\mathcal{S}, \oplus, 0)$ — summaries under merge! (elementwise addition of counts and power-sums), $0$ the all-zero summary. Both are commutative monoids. Accumulation $T : \mathcal{M} \to \mathcal{S}$ is the unique homomorphism extending the single-sample scatter $t$ (one sample adds $1$ to its bin's count and $(X_i - c_j)^p$ to that bin's $S_p$), so $T(A) = \bigoplus_{x \in A} t(x)$ and the square

    \[\begin{CD} \mathcal{M} \times \mathcal{M} @>{\uplus}>> \mathcal{M} \\ @V{T \times T}VV @VV{T}V \\ \mathcal{S} \times \mathcal{S} @>{\oplus}>> \mathcal{S} \end{CD}\]

    commutes: $T(A \uplus B) = T(A) \oplus T(B)$, which is accumulate-then-merge $=$ accumulate-together. Commutativity and associativity of $\oplus$ then make any reduction order or tree (multi-thread, multi-device, time-chunk) yield the same summary. The morphism is the unnormalized summary only: the normalize-and-smooth readout $E_h$ (the $1/n$, the shift formula, the kernel) that turns a summary into estimates is not a homomorphism — which is exactly why merge! has no population estimand (pool summaries first, estimate once, never the reverse). Exact for the integer counts; the Float64 power-sums merge up to round-off (non-associative floating-point addition), so the test asserts $=$ on counts and $\approx$ on the $S_p$.

  • Recovery. the global central moments are the shift formula

    \[\begin{aligned} \sum_i (X_i - \bar X)^p &= \sum_j \sum_{k=0}^{p} \binom{p}{k}\,(c_j - \bar X)^{p-k}\, S_k^{(j)}, \\ \bar X &= \tfrac1n \sum_j \bigl(c_j n_j + S_1^{(j)}\bigr), \qquad S_0^{(j)} = n_j, \end{aligned}\]

    which is exact (the $S_p$ use the actual $X_i$, not the center) and numerically stable for the even $p$ the consumers need; the recovered moments are exposed publicly as mean, var, std, and central_moment — var and std apply Bessel's $n-1$ correction by default (matching Statistics), corrected = false the $1/n$ form.

  • Quadrature readout (the power-sum atom; §4.B). the same power-sums give any smooth functional by Taylor contraction about the bin centers, $\mathbb{E}[g(X)] = \tfrac1n \sum_j \sum_{p=0}^{\texttt{order}} g^{(p)}(c_j)/p!\,S_p^{(j)} + O(\Delta^{\texttt{order}+1})$ — exact for polynomials of degree $\le \texttt{order}$ (the moment recovery above is the $g = x^p$ case), $O(\Delta^{\texttt{order}+1})$ for general smooth $g$. The general $\mathbb{E}[g(X)]$, its standard error, and the pushforward density $f_{g(X)}$ are built in Expectations (the §4.B section below), all through this same contraction — no new accumulation.

  • Test. test/histograms.jl (recovered $M_2 \dots M_6$ match the direct sum to $10^{-8}$ and the true Gaussian moments to sampling error); test/histograms2d.jl (2D marginals + covariance); test/mergeability.jl (merge $=$ together); test/allocations.jl (bounded observe! / merge!); test/gpu/suite.jl (CPU-vs-AMD-GPU parity).

The cone — scale!, weighted merge!, hyperstatistics (§10)

  • Object. the summary monoid $(\mathcal{S}, \oplus, 0)$ of the previous entry, equipped also with a nonnegative scalar action $w \cdot T$ (scale! — multiply every count and power-sum by $w \ge 0$), making $\mathcal{S}$ a convex cone: closed under $\oplus$ and under nonnegative scaling, hence under all nonnegative linear combinations $\sum_k w_k T_k$ (the weighted merge!(into, from; weight), $\texttt{into} \mathrel{+}= \texttt{weight} \cdot \texttt{from}$; the default $\texttt{weight} = 1$ the plain $\oplus$). Carried on a Float-count variant (count = Float64): a weighted count $w\,n$ is not an integer.

  • Estimand (hyperstatistics; Beck & Cohen 2003). the parameter-mixture (superstatistical) density over a slowly varying parameter $\beta$ with mixing law $f(\beta)$,

    \[P(x) = \int f(\beta)\,P(x \mid \beta)\,\mathrm{d}\beta \;\approx\; \sum_k w_k\, f_k(x), \qquad w_k = f(\beta_k)\,\Delta\beta,\]

    with $f_k = P(\cdot \mid \beta_k)$ the per-parameter density.

  • Estimator. keep one summary $T_k$ per $\beta_k$ (accumulated independently, $N_k$ samples), form the cone combination $M = \sum_k (w_k / N_k)\, T_k$ by weighted merge!, and read out any smoothing functional $E_h$ of $M$. Since $E_h$ normalizes by the total mass $N_M = \sum_j m_j = \sum_k w_k$, this yields $E_h(M) = (\sum_k w_k f_k(x)) / \sum_k w_k$ — the mixture (exactly, when the $w_k$ sum to one); the $1/N_k$ normalizes each component to a probability measure before mixing, and the conditional / transition estimators carry the local weight $w_k f_k(x)$ automatically (ratios of linear functionals). Post hoc: $f(\beta)$ is chosen after the runs, re-weighting without re-accumulating.

  • Cone laws (the spec; the operations have no population estimand). scale! is the monoid action — $a \cdot (b \cdot T) = (ab) \cdot T$, $1 \cdot T = T$ — and distributes over $\oplus$: $w \cdot (A \oplus B) = (w \cdot A) \oplus (w \cdot B)$; the weighted merge! is $A \oplus (w \cdot B)$. scale! preserves the normalized distribution: the mean and the uncorrected central moments are exact invariants; only the total mass — and through it the Bessel-corrected variance — scales.

  • Exponential forgetting. for a non-stationary stream, scale!(s, λ) with $0 < \lambda < 1$ before folding the next block gives a geometrically down-weighted summary (effective window $1/(1-\lambda)$); the recovered mean is the exact $\lambda$-weighted mean of the blocks.

  • Caveat. $N_M = \sum_k w_k$ ($\approx 1$ for a normalized law), so $M$'s default bandwidth reads $n \approx 1$; pass an explicit $h$ (or pre-scale the weights to a representative sample size). An effective-sample-size rule (Kish $(\sum_i w_i)^2 / \sum_i w_i^2$) is a recorded refinement.

  • Test. test/hyperstatistics.jl — the cone laws (scale! homogeneity / distributivity, weighted merge $=$ scale-then-merge, $\texttt{weight} = 1$ $=$ plain merge!, the Float-count guard); the discrete Gaussian-mixture oracle; the χ²-superstatistics → Student-$t$ oracle ($X \mid \beta \sim N(0, 1/\beta)$, $\beta \sim \mathrm{Gamma}(\nu/2, 2/\nu)$ $\Rightarrow$ the Student-$t_\nu$ marginal, recovered into the heavy tail); exact exponential forgetting; the Histogram2D cone with conditional_mean scale-invariance. test/allocations.jl (allocation-free scale! / weighted merge!); test/gpu/suite.jl (CPU-vs-GPU parity, incl. the atomic-float add of Float-count observe!).

The kernel-weighting atom & local-polynomial jet (Kernels._kernel_weight, LocalPolynomial.local_fit)

  • Kernel-weighting atom (_kernel_weight). the single per-bin weight $w_j$ at a query point $x$ and bandwidth $h$, in one of two protocols: symmetric $w_j = K((c_j - x)/h)/h$ (the $1/h$ folded in, normalized), or asymmetric $w_j = K(c_j;\,x,\,h)$ (a varying-shape density in the data argument, its own normalization). Every smoothing readout consumes $w_j$ — a count sum (density), a ratio (the jet below), a ratio of ratios (conditional_density) — so the two protocols live in exactly one place. Folding the $1/h$ into the weight is $\beta$-invariant for the local-polynomial solve (both the design matrix and the right-hand side scale by $1/h$), so it leaves every symmetric estimate unchanged.
  • Jet (local_fit). the kernel-weighted local-polynomial jet at $x$: the degree-$d$ weighted-least-squares fit of a polynomial in $u = \ell_j - x$ to a binned 1-D measure (locations $\ell_j$, design weights $w_j$, response totals $r_j$), returning $\beta = (\beta_0, \dots, \beta_d)$ — $\beta_0$ the value, $\beta_1$ the slope, $2\beta_2$ the curvature. Form the design and response moments $T_\ell = \sum_j w_j\,(\ell_j - x)^\ell$ ($\ell = 0 \dots 2d$) and $P_\ell = \sum_j w_j\,r_j\,(\ell_j - x)^\ell$ ($\ell = 0 \dots d$), the $w_j$ from _kernel_weight, and solve the $(d+1)\times(d+1)$ Hankel normal equations $[\,T_{i+\ell}\,]\,\beta = (P_0, \dots, P_d)$ (a small solve, never an explicit inverse); a degenerate (singular) design returns an all-NaN jet. conditional_mean is $\beta_0$ of one fit; the local-likelihood density reuses the solve as its IRLS inner step. An asymmetric kernel has no jet (it is not a translation kernel), so local_fit admits it at degree = 0 only — the one guard, a clean error above it.
  • Test. test/localpolynomial.jl — on a binned measure whose per-bin response mean is exactly a polynomial $P$, the jet recovers $(P(x),\,P'(x),\,P''(x)/2)$ to $10^{-6}$ (degree 2) and $(P,\,P')$ (degree 1); degree 0 is the Nadaraya–Watson ratio $P_0/T_0$; a degenerate design gives an all-NaN jet; the kernel normalization cancels. The $\beta$-invariance of the $1/h$ fold and the asymmetric-degree-0 admission ride on the unchanged density and conditional_mean oracle / rate tests.

Marginal density (§4.1) — density

  • Estimand. $f_X = \mathrm{d}\mu_X/\mathrm{d}\lambda$, the Lebesgue density of $X$.
  • Estimator. the binned KDE $\hat f_h(x) = \frac{1}{nh}\sum_j n_j K\!\left(\frac{x-\ell_j}{h}\right)$ ($\int K = 1$, Gaussian $K$) from the bin counts $n_j$ at bin locations $\ell_j$ — the within-bin centroid $\ell_j = c_j + S_1^{(j)}/n_j$ (linear binning, the default binning = :linear) or the geometric center $\ell_j = c_j$ (binning = :simple) — with $h$ from bandwidth (Silverman $h = (4/3)^{1/5}\hat\sigma\,n^{-1/5}$).
  • Consistency claim ($h \to 0$, $nh \to \infty$): $\operatorname{bias}\hat f_h(x) \approx \tfrac12 h^2 \mu_2(K)\, f_X''(x)$ and $\operatorname{Var}\hat f_h(x) \approx R(K)\,f_X(x)/(n h)$; the binning error converges to the exact KDE at $O(\Delta^2)$ in the bin width $\Delta$ for linear binning — the centroid matches the within-bin first moment, $\sum_{i\in j}(X_i - \ell_j) = 0$, so the leading $O(\Delta)$ discretization term vanishes — and at $O(\Delta)$ for simple binning; either is dominated by the $O(h^2)$ smoothing bias when $\Delta$ is small.
  • Test. test/oracles.jl (recovers the $N(0,1)$ pdf to $< 3\times10^{-3}$ over $[-3,3]$, integrates to 1). test/density_convergence.jl — a representative analytic-PDF suite (Gaussian, uniform, exponential, Student-$t$ $\nu=3$ heavy tail, log-normal, bimodal: each recovered to $\sim 10^{-2}$ and integrating to $\approx 1$, the variable bandwidth tracking the $t$ heavy tail better than the fixed) and the convergence-rate test: the ISE falls at the theoretical $n^{-2p/(2p+1)}$ — slope $\approx -0.80$ for degree 0/1 ($p=2$), the steeper $\approx -0.89$ ($n^{-8/9}$, $p=4$) for degree 2, the Abramson variable bandwidth sharing the degree-0 order; plus the binning-error convergence sub-test — the binned estimate approaches the exact KDE at $O(\Delta^2)$ for the default linear/centroid binning (slope $\approx 2$ in $\Delta$) and $O(\Delta)$ for binning = :simple (slope $\approx 1$).

Local-log-polynomial & adaptive density (§4.1) — density's degree / adaptive

  • Estimand. $f_X$, exactly as for the degree-0 density above; the options change the estimator, not the target.
  • adaptive (Abramson variable bandwidth). A fixed-bandwidth pilot $\tilde f$ at $h$ sets per-bin bandwidths $h_j = h\,(\tilde f(c_j)/g)^{-1/2}$ ($g$ the geometric mean of $\tilde f$ over the sample), widening the kernel in the sparse tails: $\operatorname{bias} = O(h^4)$ at interior smooth $x$ (Abramson 1982), with far less tail variance than a fixed $h$.
  • degree $\ge 1$ (local-log-polynomial). Fit $\log f$ by a degree-$d$ polynomial through a binned local Poisson likelihood — $\log \mathbb{E}[n_j] = \log(nb) + \sum_{l \le d} \beta_l (c_j-x)^l$, weights $K((c_j-x)/h_j)$, by Newton/IRLS — and report $\hat f(x) = e^{\hat\beta_0}$ (Loader 1996). Degree 1 is exact for an exponential density (tracks exponential tails), degree 2 for a Gaussian. The two options compose. On a bounded support the degree-0 KDE accepts bounds = (a, b), renormalizing by the kernel mass $\int_a^b K_h(x-u)\,\mathrm{d}u$ inside the support (Jones 1993) to remove the $O(1)$ boundary bias that otherwise halves the estimate at the edge.
  • Test. test/marginaldensity.jl (every grid corner recovers the $N(0,1)$ pdf into the tail and integrates to $\approx 1$; the variable bandwidth resolves the sharp Laplace cusp the fixed KDE oversmooths; degree-1 beats the KDE in a Laplace tail).

Asymmetric kernels (§4.1) — density's gamma_kernel / beta_kernel

  • Estimand. $f_X$ on a bounded or positive support.
  • Estimator. the varying-shape estimate $\hat f(x) = \tfrac1n \sum_j n_j K(c_j;\,x,\,b)$ with $K(\cdot;x,b)$ the modified $\mathrm{Gamma}(\rho(x),\,b)$ density (gamma kernel, support $[0,\infty)$; Chen 2000) or the modified $\mathrm{Beta}$ density (beta kernel, support $[0,1]$; Chen 1999) — Chen's bias-reduced $f_2$ shapes (interior shape $x/b$, kernel mean $\approx x$, removing the $f'$ bias of the naive $x/b+1$ form); the support matches the data, so no mass leaks past the boundary. A reduction over the bin counts (mergeable); default $b = \hat\sigma\,n^{-2/5}$.
  • Consistency claim. bias $O(b)$ uniformly including at the boundary (where a symmetric kernel is $O(h)$ and inconsistent); interior variance $O(n^{-1}b^{-1/2})$; optimal $b \sim n^{-2/5}$, MISE $\sim n^{-4/5}$. Not self-normalizing (an $O(b)$ mass deviation). The modified $f_2$ shape lowers the MISE constant (Chen proves $\mathrm{MISE}(f_2) \le \mathrm{MISE}(f_1)$; only $f_2$ ships, so the gain is the literature result, not a repo-reproducible number).
  • Test. test/marginaldensity.jl (gamma recovers the exponential at the boundary, beating the symmetric KDE there; beta recovers $\mathrm{Uniform}(0,1)$ at both edges; integrates to $\approx 1$; mergeability; degree/adaptive/bounds rejected).

Parametric start & normalization (§4.1) — density's start / normalize

  • Estimand. $f_X$, exactly as above; start and normalize change the estimator, not the target.
  • Estimator (start, Hjort & Glad 1995). The semiparametric estimate $\hat f(x) = p(x;\hat\theta)\,\hat r_h(x)$, where $p(\cdot;\hat\theta)$ is a parametric start fit by method of moments from the recovered $\hat\mu$, $\hat\sigma^2$, and $\hat r_h(x) = \tfrac1{nh}\sum_j (n_j/p(c_j;\hat\theta))\,K((x-c_j)/h)$ is a KDE of the correction $r = f_X/p$ (the start's local relative error). Five starts are provided — normal_start, lognormal_start, gamma_start, exponential_start, inverse_gaussian_start — each a density whose parameters are fixed by the first two moments, so $\hat\theta$ is itself a post-hoc functional of the summary and the whole estimate is mergeable ($\hat\theta$ merges through the moments; the reweighted counts add). Degree-0, fixed-$h$, symmetric kernel.
  • Consistency claim. For a fixed start with bounded, twice-differentiable correction $r$, consistent for $f_X$ at $\operatorname{bias} = O(h^2)$, $\operatorname{var} = O(1/(nh))$ (MISE $\sim n^{-4/5}$), but with the leading bias governed by $r'' = (f_X/p)''$ in place of $f_X''$ and the variance $R(K)f_X(x)/(nh)$ unchanged to leading order. When the start is correct $r \equiv 1$, $r'' \equiv 0$: the leading bias vanishes and the estimate collapses to the parametric fit. So the start is a bias reduction that pays in the bias-dominated regime (skewed / heavy-tailed shapes, oversmoothed $h$), not in the large-$n$ limit where the KDE is already nearly unbiased.
  • Estimator (normalize). $\hat f / Z$ with $Z = \int \hat f$ over the summary's range intersected with the estimator's support, by the trapezoidal rule on a fine grid — a deterministic, mergeable functional of the estimate making $\int \hat f = 1$ exactly (the asymmetric kernels and a parametric start are not self-normalizing; the symmetric KDE already is, to the grid's $O(\Delta^2)$).
  • Test. test/marginaldensity.jl (a normal start at a wide $h$ recovers the Gaussian peak the plain KDE flattens by $\approx 10\%$, ISE $> 20\times$ smaller; a log-normal start tracks log-normal data $> 3\times$ better than the symmetric KDE, and pointwise into the right tail; normalize drives $\int\hat f$ to $1$ for a start and for the gamma kernel; mergeability; the rejected option combinations).

Pointwise standard error (§4.1) — density_standard_error

  • Estimand. the standard error of the density estimator, $\mathrm{SE}(x) = \sqrt{\operatorname{Var}\hat f_h(x)}$ with the KDE asymptotic variance $\operatorname{Var}\hat f_h(x) \approx R(K)\,f_X(x)/(nh)$.
  • Estimator. $\sqrt{R(K)\,\hat f_h(x)/(nh)}$ from the recovered $\hat f_h$, $n$, $h$, and $R(K) = \int K^2$ (by quadrature). The $z$-band $\hat f_h(x) \pm z\,\mathrm{SE}(x)$ is a variability band about $\mathbb{E}\hat f_h$, covering $f_X$ where the $O(h^2)$ smoothing bias is small. Degree 0/1 (the variable bandwidth and degree $\ge 2$ are a coverage gap).
  • Test. test/marginaldensity.jl (the SE equals $\sqrt{R(K)\hat f/(nh)}$ exactly; the 95\% band covers the $N(0,1)$ truth over $\ge 75\%$ of the interior; the SE shrinks with $n$; vector / empty / error forms).

Bandwidth selection (§4.5) — bandwidth

  • Estimand. the AMISE-optimal bandwidth $h^\star = \arg\min_h \int \mathbb{E}[(\hat f_h(x) - f_X(x))^2]\,\mathrm{d}x$ for a kernel $K$ and local-polynomial degree $d$ — the asymptotically optimal smoothing scale, itself $O(n^{-1/(2p+1)})$ (a finite-sample selector, not a fixed population constant).
  • Estimator (rule = :normal, the kernel- and degree-aware normal-reference). $h = C(K,d)\,\hat\sigma\,n^{-1/(2p+1)}$ with bias order $p = 2$ (degree 0/1) or $p = 4$ (degree 2/3), and constant $C(K,d) = [W_{ikk}(K,d)\,2^{p+1}\sqrt{\pi}/(2p-1)!!]^{1/(2p+1)}$. The canonical factor is $W_{ikk}(K,d) = R(K)/\mu_2(K)^2$ at degree 0/1, and the fourth-order equivalent-kernel analog $W_{ikk} = (4!^2/8)\,R(K^\star)/\mu_4(K^\star)^2$ with $K^\star = K\,(n_4 - v^2 n_2)/(n_0 n_4 - n_2^2)$ at degree 2/3 (Loader 1996); here $R(K) = \int K^2$, $\mu_2(K) = \int u^2 K$ (by quadrature) and $n_k = \int v^k K$. $\hat\sigma$ and $n$ are read from the one summary.
  • Estimator (rule = :sj, the data-driven Sheather–Jones plug-in; degree 0/1). The curvature $\psi_4 = R(f'')$ and its $\psi_6$ pilot are estimated from the bin counts, $\hat\psi_r(g) = (n^2 g^{r+1})^{-1}\sum_{j,k} n_j n_k\,\varphi^{(r)}((c_j - c_k)/g)$ (Gaussian $\varphi$, the $n$ self-pairs removed), and the solve-the-equation bandwidth (Sheather & Jones 1991) is the root of $h = [R(\varphi)/(\mu_2(\varphi)^2\,\hat\psi_4(\alpha\,h^{5/7})\,n)]^{1/5}$; the eval kernel then multiplies by the canonical-bandwidth ratio $\delta(K)/\delta(\varphi)$ with $\delta(K) = [R(K)/\mu_2(K)^2]^{1/5}$. Both functionals are reductions of the bin counts, hence mergeable; degree $\ge 2$ is a recorded coverage gap. The improved variant (rule = :isj; Botev, Grotowski & Kroese 2010) instead reads $\|f^{(s)}\|^2$ off the DCT of the bin counts and solves the AMISE fixed point $t = \xi\gamma^5(t)$ — also a mergeable functional of the counts, and robust on strongly multimodal data.
  • Estimator (rule = :aic, the fit-based information criterion; degree 0/1). Minimise $\mathrm{AIC}(h) = -2\sum_j n_j\log\hat f_h(c_j) + 2\,\mathrm{df}(h)$ of the binned Gaussian KDE over a log-spaced $h$-grid, with $\mathrm{df}(h) = (\varphi(0)/h)\sum_j n_j/(n\hat f_h(c_j))$ ($\varphi$ the Gaussian); both terms diverge as $h\to0$, giving a stable interior optimum. Mergeable (a functional of the counts); the eval kernel multiplies by $\delta(K)/\delta(\varphi)$. The leave-one-out cross-validation selectors (likelihood-CV, least-squares LSCV) are a recorded coverage gap — binning ties drive their objective to $h\to0$.
  • Estimator (rule = :plugin, the asymmetric-kernel Chen plug-in; gamma_kernel / beta_kernel). The gamma / beta estimators take a scale $b$ (rate $n^{-2/5}$), not $h$; their AMISE (Chen 1999, 2000; modified $f_2$) is $A b^2 + C b^{-1/2}/n$ with $A = \tfrac14\int w^2 (f'')^2$, $C = (2\sqrt\pi)^{-1}\mathbb{E}[w(X)^{-1/2}]$ and local-scale weight $w(x) = x$ (gamma) or $x(1-x)$ (beta), giving $b^\star = [\mathbb{E}[w(X)^{-1/2}]/(2\sqrt\pi\,n\int w^2 (f'')^2)]^{2/5}$. $\mathbb{E}[w(X)^{-1/2}]$ is an exact count moment; the curvature $\int w^2 (f'')^2$ is a grid quadrature of the pilot KDE's $\hat f''_g$ (pilot $g = 1.3\,s\,n^{-1/9}$ at the robust scale $s = \min(\hat\sigma,\,\mathrm{IQR}/1.349)$, the variance inflation $R(\varphi'')\,\mathbb{E}[w^2]/(n g^5)$ removed), which — unlike the integration-by-parts $\psi_4$ U-statistic — stays valid where $f(0) > 0$ (the $w \to 0$ boundary weight suppresses the pilot's edge leakage). A deterministic functional of the counts (mergeable). The symmetric :sj / :isj / :aic do not apply; a fully data-adaptive curvature pilot is a recorded refinement.
  • Consistency claim. with $\hat\sigma \to \sigma$ and the Gaussian reference $R(f^{(p)}) = (2p-1)!!/(2^{p+1}\sqrt{\pi}\,\sigma^{2p+1})$, $C(K,d)$ is the exact AMISE-optimal constant: degree 0/1 gives $h \sim n^{-1/5}$ (MISE $\sim n^{-4/5}$), degree 2 the higher-order $h \sim n^{-1/9}$ (MISE $\sim n^{-8/9}$); the Gaussian/degree-0 case is Silverman $(4/3)^{1/5}$, the Epanechnikov $\approx 2.34$.
  • Test. test/bandwidth.jl (Gaussian degree 0 reproduces Silverman, degree 1 equals degree 0, the Epanechnikov $h$ is $2.214\times$ the Gaussian, degree 2 matches the closed-form $n^{-1/9}$ rule and exceeds degree 0; coverage-gap and sample-size errors; plus the Sheather–Jones tests — SJ $\approx$ the normal reference on Gaussian data, much tighter on a bimodal mixture, the exact $\delta(K)$ kernel ratio, mergeability, the degree $\ge 2$ gap; and the AIC tests — a stable optimum below the normal reference on Gaussian data, far smaller on a bimodal mixture, the exact $\delta(K)$ ratio, mergeability, and the deliberately-absent CV rules erroring; and the asymmetric Chen plug-in — :normal reproduces the $\hat\sigma\,n^{-2/5}$ rule of thumb, :plugin lowers the ISE ($\ge 1.25\times$ on log-normal, $\ge 1.11\times$ on gamma; $\approx 1.4\text{–}1.7\times$ typically, seed-audited) and stays $\approx$ neutral on the near-exponential boundary case, gamma and beta kernels, mergeability, and the inapplicable :sj/:isj/:aic and degree $\ge 1$ rules erroring; the Improved Sheather–Jones tests (:isj $\approx$ the normal reference on Gaussian, $< 0.5\times$ it on a bimodal mixture, the exact $\delta(K)$ ratio, mergeability); and the Marron–Wand claw stress, where the data-driven :sj / :isj resolve the five spikes the normal reference oversmooths — $\approx$ an order of magnitude lower ISE) and test/oracles.jl (the kernel-aware ratio on the $N(0,1)$ oracle).

Local-linear conditional mean (§4.2) — conditional_mean

  • Estimand. $m(x) = \mathbb{E}[Y \mid X = x] = \int y\, f_{Y\mid X}(y \mid x)\,\mathrm{d}y$, the $L^2(\sigma(X))$ projection of the response onto the conditioning variable.

  • Estimator. the local-linear fit $\hat m_h(x) = (T_2 P_0 - T_1 P_1)/(T_0 T_2 - T_1^2)$ from the kernel-weighted power-sums $T_\ell = \sum_i K_h(X_i-x)(X_i-x)^\ell$ and $P_\ell = \sum_i K_h(X_i-x)(X_i-x)^\ell Y_i$ — recovered from the joint Histogram2D's per-X-column counts and response totals $\sum Y$ (exact from the per-cell power-sums).

  • Consistency claim (interior $x$, $h \to 0$, $nh \to \infty$):

    \[\operatorname{bias}\hat m_h(x) \approx \tfrac12 h^2 \mu_2(K)\, m''(x), \qquad \operatorname{Var}\hat m_h(x) \approx \frac{R(K)\,\sigma^2(x)}{n\,h\,f_X(x)},\]

    giving the optimal rate $\hat m_{h^\star}(x) - m(x) = O_p(n^{-2/5})$ at $h^\star \sim n^{-1/5}$.

  • Test. test/regression.jl — the bivariate-Gaussian oracle $Y = \rho X + \sqrt{1-\rho^2}\, \varepsilon$ (so $\mathbb{E}[Y\mid X=x] = \rho x$): conditional_mean recovers the slope to $\lesssim 2\times10^{-2}$, with the degree (0/1/2) and kernel options exercised (degree-2 and Epanechnikov both recover the linear mean). test/conditional_convergence.jl — the $n^{-2/5}$ rate: on a curved-mean oracle ($m(x) = \sin x$, so the local-linear fit carries a genuine $O(h^2)$ bias) at $h \propto n^{-1/5}$ the integrated squared error falls at $n^{-4/5}$ (the $n^{-2/5}$ RMSE), the fitted log–log slope $\approx -0.80$.

Conditional variance (§4.2) — conditional_variance

  • Estimand. $\sigma^2(x) = \operatorname{Var}(Y \mid X = x) = \mathbb{E}[Y^2 \mid X = x] - m(x)^2$, with $m(x)$ the conditional mean above.
  • Estimator (method = :residual, the default; Fan & Yao 1998). The two-stage residual-based estimator: fit $\hat m$ at each X-column center $c^x_j$, form the per-column squared-residual total $\sum (Y - \hat m_j)^2 = \sum Y^2 - 2\hat m_j \sum Y + \hat m_j^2 N$ ($\hat m_j = \hat m(c^x_j)$, exact from the stored column sums), and take a second local fit of it, $\hat\sigma^2(x) = \widehat{\mathbb{E}}[(Y - \hat m(X))^2 \mid x]$. The :plugin alternative is the simple difference $\widehat{\mathbb{E}}[Y^2 \mid x] - \hat m_h(x)^2$; both clamped non-negative.
  • Consistency claim. both are consistent for $\sigma^2(x)$. The plug-in carries the local-polynomial smoothing bias of the curved $\mathbb{E}[Y^2\mid x]$ — an $O(h^2)$ term driven by $m''$ and the curvature of $\mathbb{E}[Y^2\mid x]$, large where $m$ curves. The residual-based estimator fits the far flatter $\mathbb{E}[(Y - m)^2\mid x] = \sigma^2(x)$, whose $O(h^2)$ bias $\propto (\sigma^2)''$ is much smaller, so it removes the $m''$-driven bias.
  • Test. test/regression.jl — recovers $1-\rho^2$ on the (linear-mean) bivariate-Gaussian oracle to a few percent, and on a heteroscedastic curved-mean oracle ($X\sim N(0,1)$, $m(x)=x^2$, $\sigma^2(x)=0.2+0.1x^2$) the residual estimator recovers $\sigma^2(x)$ (degree 2) and beats the plug-in in the curved tail (the plug-in inflated $>1.5\times$ by the $m''$ curvature); the universal contract via check_point_estimator (scalar $=$ vector, functional of the merged summary, $\mathrm{NaN}$ off-support). Its rate is the same $n^{-2/5}$ local-regression rate (a second local-polynomial fit, of the squared residuals); it shares the conditional_mean slope test (test/conditional_convergence.jl) rather than carrying a separate one.

Response-aware local bandwidth (§4.5) — bandwidth(::Histogram2D, x; rule = :local)

  • Estimand. the AMISE-optimal local bandwidth for the conditional mean, balancing the local-linear squared bias against the variance at each $x$:

    \[h^\star(x) = \left[\frac{R(K)\,\sigma^2(x)}{\mu_2(K)^2\,m''(x)^2\,f_X(x)\,n}\right]^{1/5}.\]

  • Estimator. plug in the three $x$-local quantities from the one joint summary, each at a pilot bandwidth: $\hat\sigma^2(x)$ (the Fan–Yao conditional_variance), the curvature $\hat m''(x) = 2\beta_2$ (the degree-2 LocalPolynomial jet), and $\hat f_X(x)$ (the X-marginal KDE); $R(K)$, $\mu_2(K)$ by quadrature. The default pilot is the curvature-rate rule of thumb $\hat\sigma_X\,n^{-1/9}$ (wider than $h^\star$, so the second derivative is stable); where the regression is locally linear ($m'' \to 0$) the optimal $h$ is unbounded, capped at the X-range.

  • Consistency claim. with consistent pilots $\hat\sigma^2 \to \sigma^2$, $\hat m'' \to m''$, $\hat f_X \to f_X$, the plug-in is consistent for $h^\star(x) \sim n^{-1/5}$; each pilot enters through a weak ($1/5$-power) factor, so $h^\star$ is robust to pilot error.

  • Test. test/localbandwidth.jl — the quadratic-mean Gaussian oracle ($X\sim N(0,1)$, $Y = X^2 + \sigma\varepsilon$: $m''=2$, $\sigma^2$ constant, $f_X = \varphi$) where $h^\star(x)$ is closed-form: recovered near the centre to $\lesssim 12\%$, positive/finite on support, $\mathrm{NaN}$ off it, scalar $=$ vector, default-pilot sanity, and mergeability.

Conditional & transition density (§4.3) — conditional_density, transition_density

  • Estimand. $f_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x) = f_{X,Y}(x,y)/f_X(x)$, and the two-time transition density $p_\tau(y\mid x) = f_{X_{t+\tau}\mid X_t}(y\mid x)$ (the same object on a lag-$\tau$ joint).

  • Estimator. the binned double-kernel (Rosenblatt 1969; Hyndman, Bashtannyk & Grunwald 1996), with a conditioning kernel $K_1 =$ kernelx (bandwidth $h_1 =$ hx, in $x$) and a response kernel $K_2 =$ kernely (bandwidth $h_2 =$ hy, in $y$); each $\int K = 1$, default gaussian_kernel: from the cell counts $n_{jk}$ at centers $(c^x_j, c^y_k)$ with $N_j = \sum_k n_{jk}$,

    \[\hat f(y\mid x) = \frac{\sum_{j,k} n_{jk}\, K_{h_1}(c^x_j - x)\, K_{h_2}(c^y_k - y)}{\sum_j N_j\, K_{h_1}(c^x_j - x)} ,\]

    computed as $\hat f(y\mid x) = \big(\sum_k P_k\, K((c^y_k - y)/h_2)\big) / (h_2 D)$ for a symmetric $K_2$ (the surviving $1/h_2$ from $K_{h_2}$), with the conditioning-kernel-weighted profile $P_k = \sum_j n_{jk} K_{h_1}(c^x_j - x)$ and $D = \sum_k P_k$. The conditioning-kernel weighting cancels in the ratio (so kernelx only weights the local window); for a symmetric $K_2$ the $1/h_2$ makes $\int \hat f(y\mid x)\,\mathrm dy = 1$ exactly ($\int K = 1$). For a bounded / positive response an asymmetric $K_2$ = kernely (gamma / beta; Chen 1999, 2000) is instead a density in the data argument, $\hat f = \big(\sum_k P_k\, K_2(c^y_k;\, y, h_2)\big)/D$ — boundary-bias-free in $y$ but only $\approx$-normalized, made exact by the normalize renormalization.

  • Consistency claim ($h_1, h_2 \to 0$, $n h_1 h_2 \to \infty$): for symmetric kernels the pointwise bias is $\tfrac12 h_1^2 \mu_2(K)\big[\partial_{xx} f_{Y\mid X} + 2\,\partial_x f_{Y\mid X}\,\partial_x f_X / f_X\big] + \tfrac12 h_2^2 \mu_2(K)\,\partial_{yy} f_{Y\mid X}$ and the variance $R(K)^2 f_{Y\mid X}/(n h_1 h_2 f_X)$, giving the slower conditional rate $O(n^{-2/3})$ at the optimal $h \sim n^{-1/6}$ (the bias is worst at the conditional mode). An asymmetric $K_2$ carries the boundary-uniform $O(h_2)$ Chen $y$-bias instead of the $O(h_2^2)$ interior term — the price of consistency at the $y = 0$ edge a symmetric kernel cannot reach.

  • Test. test/conditionaldensity.jl — the bivariate-Gaussian fixture $Y\mid X=x \sim N(\rho x, 1-\rho^2)$: $\int \hat f\,\mathrm dy \approx 1$ and pointwise recovery to $3\times10^{-2}$; the scalar/vector forms, the transition_density equivalence, off-support NaN, bandwidth checks, and mergeability; plus the asymmetric-response oracle ($X\sim N(0,1)$, $Y\sim\mathrm{Exp}(1)\perp X$ $\Rightarrow$ $f(y\mid x)=e^{-y}$), where the gamma kernely recovers the density at the boundary $y\approx0$ that the symmetric Gaussian leaks low, normalize driving $\int\hat f\,\mathrm dy$ to 1. test/oracles.jl — the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck oracle: transition_density recovers the closed-form $p_\tau(y\mid x) = N(x e^{-\theta\tau},\, s^2(1-e^{-2\theta\tau}))$ to $4\times10^{-2}$ (the $O(h^2)$ mode bias). test/conditional_convergence.jl — the $n^{-2/3}$ rate: on the bivariate-Gaussian oracle, with $h_1 = h_2 \propto n^{-1/6}$, the ISE in $y$ at $x_0 = 0$ falls with fitted log–log slope $\approx -2/3$ (reaching the 2-D asymptotic regime needs $n \gtrsim 10^5$, so the n-range starts higher than the 1-D density test).

Power-sum quadrature — expectation of a functional (§4.B) — expectation

  • Estimand. the expectation of a smooth functional of $X$, $\mathbb{E}[g(X)] = \int g(x)\, f_X(x)\,\mathrm dx$.
  • Estimator. the power-sum quadrature $\hat{\mathbb{E}}[g] = \tfrac1n \sum_j \sum_{p=0}^{\texttt{order}} a_p^{(j)}\, S_p^{(j)}$ ($S_0^{(j)} = n_j$), where $a_p^{(j)}$ are the coefficients (in $u = x - c_j$) of the degree-$\texttt{order}$ polynomial interpolant of $g$ on bin $j$ — a Chebyshev–Lobatto interpolant, its inverse Vandermonde precomputed once (uniform bins), so no derivatives of $g$ are formed. For a monomial $g = x^m$ ($m \le \texttt{order}$) the interpolant is exact and $a_p^{(j)}$ is the binomial $\binom{m}{p} c_j^{m-p}$, so the readout reduces to the shift formula — mean, var, central_moment are this estimator, and share the Histograms._quadrature atom.
  • Consistency claim. exact (to floating-point) for polynomial $g$ of degree $\le \texttt{order}$, independent of the bin width; $O(\Delta^{\,\texttt{order}+1})$ for general smooth $g$ (the interpolation remainder). The estimand needs $g$ smooth on the bin scale and the mass inside the range.
  • Test. test/expectations.jl — the Gaussian oracles $\mathbb{E}[\cos X] = e^{-1/2}$, $\mathbb{E}[\sin X] = 0$ (MGF/trig); polynomial exactness ($\hat{\mathbb{E}}[g] = \tfrac1n\sum_i g(X_i)$ to $10^{-10}$ for degree $\le \texttt{order}$); the binning error vs the raw empirical mean $< 10^{-6}$ for a smooth $g$; the moment unification ($\mathbb{E}[x^p]$ equals the recovered moment); mergeability; empty $\Rightarrow$ NaN.

Power-sum quadrature — sampling standard error (§4.B) — expectation_standard_error

  • Estimand. the sampling standard error of $\hat{\mathbb{E}}[g]$, $\mathrm{SE} = \sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(g(X))/n}$ with $\operatorname{Var}(g(X)) = \mathbb{E}[g^2] - \mathbb{E}[g]^2$.
  • Estimator. $\sqrt{(\hat{\mathbb{E}}[g^2] - \hat{\mathbb{E}}[g]^2)/n}$ — both moments the same quadrature ($\mathbb{E}[g^2]$ is expectation with the smooth integrand $g^2$). It is the plug-in sampling error of the empirical mean of $g(X)$; the $O(\Delta^{\,\texttt{order}+1})$ quadrature bias is separate and not included.
  • Consistency claim. for $g = \mathrm{identity}$ it is exactly $s/\sqrt n$ ($s$ the uncorrected standard deviation); in general the plug-in $\widehat{\operatorname{Var}(g(X))}$ is consistent as $\Delta \to 0$.
  • Test. test/expectations.jl — $\mathrm{SE}(\mathrm{identity}) = \operatorname{std}_{\text{unc}}/\sqrt n$ to $10^{-10}$; $\mathrm{SE}(\cos)$ matches the raw empirical $\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(\cos X)/n}$; positivity; empty $\Rightarrow$ NaN.

Power-sum quadrature — pushforward density (§4.B) — pushforward_density

  • Estimand. the density of the transformed variable $Y = g(X)$, the pushforward $f_{g(X)}(y) = \sum_{x:\,g(x)=y} f_X(x)/|g'(x)|$ (change of variables, summed over preimages).
  • Estimator. the kernel density estimate of the pushforward written as an expectation, $\hat f_{g(X)}(y) = \tfrac1n \sum_i K_h(g(X_i) - y) = \hat{\mathbb{E}}[K_h(g(X) - y)]$, so it is one expectation of the composed test function $x \mapsto K_h(g(x) - y)$ — no $g^{-1}$ and no explicit Jacobian. A non-monotone $g$ contributes from every preimage automatically; $g' = 0$ is an integrable caustic. For a symmetric $K$ ($\int K = 1$) the surviving $1/h$ gives $\int \hat f\,\mathrm dy = 1$ exactly.
  • Consistency claim. the standard KDE bias–variance in $y$ (bias $\tfrac12 h^2 \mu_2(K) f_{g(X)}''$, variance $R(K) f_{g(X)}/(nh)$), provided $g$ is smooth on the bin scale and $h \gtrsim |g'|\,\Delta$ (so $K_h\circ g$ is resolved across a bin).
  • Test. test/expectations.jl — $Y = e^X$ with $X\sim N(0,1)$ recovers the Lognormal$(0,1)$ pdf (pointwise to $6\%$, $\int\hat f\,\mathrm dy \approx 1$), and the non-monotone $Y = X^2$ recovers the $\chi^2_1$ pdf in the bulk (both preimages $\pm\sqrt y$ contributing); scalar $=$ vector, positive-$h$ check, mergeability.
