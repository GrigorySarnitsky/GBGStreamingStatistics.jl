Formalization

The typed statements behind the prose. Each operation gets one entry stating its estimand, its estimator, and the consistency claim — the bias and variance with every normalization constant pinned — each linked to the test that verifies the convergence rate numerically. The chain is prose ↔ formalization ↔ test: the prose (docstrings, ARCHITECTURE.md) agrees with the typed statement, the statement is verified against the code, and the linked test enforces it.

A nontrivial claim with no entry here, or an entry with no test link, is a recorded coverage gap (CLAUDE.md, Definition of done).

Throughout, $K$ is the smoothing kernel with $\int K = 1$, $\mu_2(K) = \int u^2 K(u)\,\mathrm du$ its second moment, and $R(K) = \int K^2$ its roughness; $h$ is the bandwidth, $n$ the number of samples, $f_X$ the marginal density of the conditioning variable.

The implemented entries are test-linked; the conditional entry is the format for what lands next.

Per-bin central moments — the Pébay recursion (Accumulators, Histogram1D)

  • Object. per bin, the count, mean, and unnormalized central moments $M_p = \sum_{i \in \text{bin}}(X_i - \bar X)^p$ for $p = 2 \dots$ order (intrinsically finite-sample — no population estimand).
  • Recursion. the single-sample update (_observe_moment!) and the parallel merge (_merge_moment!) are the Pébay (2008) formulas, accumulated in Float64; the single-sample update is the $n_B = 1$ case of the merge, so streaming accumulation and merge! are the same arithmetic.
  • Correctness. the recursion is exact — it computes the same $M_p$ as a direct two-pass sum, to round-off — and the merge gives accumulate-then-merge $=$ accumulate-together.
  • Test. test/histograms.jl (recovered $M_2 \dots M_6$ match the direct sum to $10^{-8}$ and the true Gaussian moments to sampling error); test/mergeability.jl (merge $=$ together); test/allocations.jl (observe!/merge! allocate 0 bytes).

Marginal density (§4.1) — density

  • Estimand. $f_X = \mathrm{d}\mu_X/\mathrm{d}\lambda$, the Lebesgue density of $X$.
  • Estimator. the binned KDE $\hat f_h(x) = \frac{1}{nh}\sum_j n_j K\!\left(\frac{x-c_j}{h}\right)$ ($\int K = 1$, Gaussian $K$) from the bin counts $n_j$ at centers $c_j$, with $h$ from bandwidth (Silverman $h = (4/3)^{1/5}\hat\sigma\,n^{-1/5}$).
  • Consistency claim ($h \to 0$, $nh \to \infty$): $\operatorname{bias}\hat f_h(x) \approx \tfrac12 h^2 \mu_2(K)\, f_X''(x)$ and $\operatorname{Var}\hat f_h(x) \approx R(K)\,f_X(x)/(n h)$; the binning error $O(b^2)$ is dominated by the $O(h^2)$ smoothing bias when $b \ll h$.
  • Test. test/oracles.jl (recovers the $N(0,1)$ pdf to $< 3\times10^{-3}$ over $[-3,3]$, integrates to 1; the Silverman/Scott bandwidths match their formulas).

Local-linear conditional mean (§4.2) — conditional_mean

  • Estimand. $m(x) = \mathbb{E}[Y \mid X = x] = \int y\, f_{Y\mid X}(y \mid x)\,\mathrm{d}y$, the $L^2(\sigma(X))$ projection of the response onto the conditioning variable.

  • Estimator. the local-linear fit $\hat m_h(x) = (T_2 P_0 - T_1 P_1)/(T_0 T_2 - T_1^2)$ from the kernel-weighted power-sums $T_\ell = \sum_i K_h(X_i-x)(X_i-x)^\ell$ and $P_\ell = \sum_i K_h(X_i-x)(X_i-x)^\ell Y_i$, read from the per-bin power-sums of the summary.

  • Consistency claim (interior $x$, $h \to 0$, $nh \to \infty$):

    \[\operatorname{bias}\hat m_h(x) \approx \tfrac12 h^2 \mu_2(K)\, m''(x), \qquad \operatorname{Var}\hat m_h(x) \approx \frac{R(K)\,\sigma^2(x)}{n\,h\,f_X(x)},\]

    giving the optimal rate $\hat m_{h^\star}(x) - m(x) = O_p(n^{-2/5})$ at $h^\star \sim n^{-1/5}$.

  • Test. (forthcoming with the estimator: an OU oracle, and a rate test that the error falls as $n^{-2/5}$.)
