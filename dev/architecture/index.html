GBGStreamingStatistics — Architecture and Rationale

This document explains why GBGStreamingStatistics is built the way it is, in the style of its sibling GBGPeriodicFields (PF). It is self-contained: every symbol — a math symbol or a code identifier — is introduced in plain language at its first use, and it reads top to bottom. All mathematics is rendered LaTeX in standard probability notation. The naming rationale is in §9; deliberate absences are stated as invariants in §6; open decisions are flagged [OPEN].

0. Provenance

The driving requirement is conditional statistics of the highest quality with no storage of the underlying state and no recompute-from-raw reruns — which forces the central architectural choice of §2.

The package is designed to be shared across at least three consumers — turbulence (Lagrangian tracer statistics: conditional averages, transition densities, Kramers–Moyal coefficients), cloud microphysics (droplet growth conditioned on size and supersaturation), and molecular dynamics (the van Hove and intermediate scattering functions are two-time conditional densities) — so it is domain-neutral from the first commit.

1. Purpose and scope

A backend-agnostic library for online nonparametric statistics: it estimates marginal densities, conditional expectations, conditional and two-time transition densities, and standard functionals of these, from a stream of samples that is never stored. Each sample is observed once, folded into a fixed-size summary, and discarded.

The relationship to GBGPeriodicFields is exact and deliberate:

PF is all mathematics, no physics. This package is all statistics, no physics.

PF provides field operations a solver consumes; it carries no concept of time, no particle, no tracer (those are the consumer's). This package provides estimators a solver consumes; it carries no concept of velocity, droplet, trajectory, or turbulence — and, one level deeper, no probabilistic model: the data is not assumed stationary, Markov, diffusive, or parametric. Those are consumer hypotheses the package's diagnostics test, never premises its estimators require.

Boundary — samples in, statistics out. The package never sees a grid, an FFT, a tracer, or a droplet. It consumes a stream of values (scalars / vectors, optionally lagged pairs tagged by a lag $\tau$) and returns estimators. The sampling and transport layer — interpolating off an Eulerian field, reading particle states, walking parcel trajectories — stays in each consumer. The instant a grid or tracer concept crosses this line, reuse across the three domains dies.

Execution target, inherited from PF: a single node, shared-memory only (CPU multithreading and a single GPU); distributed / multi-GPU is out of scope. Development is GPU-first but the code is backend-agnostic (CPU Array, CUDA, ROCm) through KernelAbstractions — §5.

2. The central principle: the architecture follows established statistics

Every abstraction is the code image of a standard object of probability and mathematical statistics, and design decisions are settled by estimation theory, not implementation convenience — the direct analog of PF's "architecture follows established mathematics." As in PF, the recurring lesson is that an absence is a precise statement (§6).

The spine: empirical measure → summary → post-hoc-smoothed estimator

Let $X_1, X_2, \dots$ be the observed stream. The object that carries all information is the empirical measure

\[\hat\mu_n = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \delta_{X_i},\]

where $\delta_x$ is the unit point mass at $x$ and $n$ the number of samples seen. We do not store the $n$ point masses. We store a finite-dimensional summary $T(\hat\mu_n)$ — a functional of the unnormalized empirical measure $\sum_i \delta_{X_i} = n\hat\mu_n$ (the additive object; the $1/n$ normalization is applied post hoc) — that is

  1. mergeable — $T$ is an additive monoid (in fact a cone — also scalable by scale!; §10) over the sample stream: the unnormalized summary of a batch is a running sum, so two batches' summaries combine by merge! and accumulate-then-merge equals accumulate-together (to round-off). This is just additivity of measures, and it is what makes multi-device reduction and time-chunk stitching free (§5, §6);
  2. rich enough that every reported quantity is recoverable from it post hoc, with no second pass over the data.

Every reported quantity is then an estimator — a functional

\[\hat\theta = S_h\bigl(T(\hat\mu_n)\bigr)\]

of that summary, where $S_h$ is a smoothing operator (a choice of kernel, bandwidth $h$, and any secondary smoothing). The load-bearing decision:

The summary $T$ is accumulated online and is estimand-agnostic; the smoothing $S_h$ is applied entirely post hoc. Accumulation knows nothing of bandwidth, kernel, or which response channel — which response variable, each with its own summary — will be asked for. This separation is the architectural invariant of the package — the analog of PF's representation/operator split — and it is exactly what delivers the user's requirement: no state storage (only $T$ survives a step) and no reruns (every $h$, every channel, every secondary smoothing is computed from the one stored $T$).

The algebra of measures — a structural lens

The spine has a compact algebraic reading, offered as a structural lens, not a second motivation: the package is and remains mathematical statistics grounded in practice — its targets are consistency, the $n^{-2/5}$ convergence rates, the analytic oracles, honest bandwidth selection. The lens only names why the spine takes the shape it does (established structure read back onto the code, as in PF), and settles nothing the statistics does not already settle.

Read the summaries as living in the cone of (unnormalized) measures: a commutative monoid under merge!, closed under nonnegative scaling by scale! (the cone of §10). The binning map $T$ is then linear, $T(\mu + \nu) = T\mu + T\nu$ — a cone homomorphism — and accumulation is the monoid homomorphism from the multiset of samples (the free commutative monoid; observe! folds a batch, merge! unions two summaries). This is why merge! is elementwise addition and accumulate-then-merge equals accumulate-together: not a convenience, but linearity of $T$.

The estimands sit one level up. Every reported quantity — the normalized density $f_X$, the conditional mean $m(x) = \mathbb{E}[Y \mid X = x]$, the conditional density $f_{Y\mid X}$ — is homogeneous of degree zero in the measure: rescale $\mu \mapsto \lambda\mu$ and it is unchanged, so it lives not on the cone but on its projectivization, the probability measures. The scale!-invariance §10 verifies (a conditional_mean unmoved by reweighting) is this degree-zero homogeneity. And because the readout $S_h$ is degree zero while the cone operations are linear, $S_h$ is not a cone homomorphism — the estimand-agnostic-accumulation / post-hoc-smoothing split restated: pooling must happen on the cone (linear, mergeable), and the degree-zero readout must stay post hoc. That is why "no reruns" forces the smoothing post hoc.

The lens also fixes the shape of the estimators: the universal object is the summary-as-measure, and every estimand is a degree-zero readout of it that falls into one of two families, each with one shared code atom — kernel-weighting (the local / smoothing readouts, through the Kernels._kernel_weight atom, with LocalPolynomial.local_fit the jet readout on it — the jet being the fit's local Taylor coefficients) and power-sum quadrature (the global / moment readouts, the Taylor contraction of the stored $S_p$). §4.A and §4.B exposit the two families. The jet local_fit is the kernel-weighting family's primitive, not the universal one — $\mathbb{E}[g(X)]$ is a readout with no jet at all — and the package's one nonlinearity (the density's log link and normalization) lives in the readout, exactly where the cone homomorphism breaks.

Why a fine histogram is the canonical $T$ (and the honest caveat)

The canonical summary is a finely binned empirical measure — a histogram, the fine-grained measure that post-hoc smoothing (a choice of bandwidth $h$) later coarse-grains into each reported estimate — optionally carrying per-bin moments (below). For nonparametric estimation there is, strictly, no finite sufficient statistic; the histogram is lossy. The justification is quantitative and sets the bin count: with bin width $b$ and smoothing bandwidth $h$, the binning error is $O(b^2)$ while the irreducible smoothing bias is $O(h^2)$, so choosing $b \ll h$ (in practice $\gtrsim 10^3$ bins over $\pm 5\sigma$, so $b \approx \sigma/100 \ll h$) makes the summary approximately sufficient: it loses nothing at the target accuracy (the WARPing / binned-KDE / average-shifted-histogram result, Hall & Wand 1996; Scott 2015). We therefore say "approximately-sufficient summary," never "sufficient statistic" — the latter would be the category overclaim PF's correctness policy exists to catch.

Estimand families (the operation taxonomy)

Mirroring PF's operation families (vertical algebra / differential / reductions / transfer), the package's families are organized by estimand — the population quantity targeted. Each is detailed in §4:

  • Marginal density $f_X$ — from a 1-D histogram.
  • Conditional expectation $m(x) = \mathbb{E}[Y \mid X = x]$ and conditional variance — from per-bin power-sums.
  • Conditional / transition density $f_{Y\mid X}$, $p_\tau(y \mid x)$ — from a 2-D (joint) histogram, per lag.
  • Conditional increment moments and their functionals (archived, §4.4) — Kramers–Moyal coefficients, drift/diffusion, Pawula / Q diagnostics — domain-neutral functionals of the above; the SDE reading stays consumer-side.
  • Bandwidth selection (cross-cutting) — global and the response-aware local $h^\star(x)$ (both built).
  • [OPEN] a streaming projection road (basis projection à la Stochastic Force Inference (SFI) / Underdamped Langevin Inference (ULI)) as an alternative accumulator.

The histogram is the summary; the ECF is deferred (likely never needed)

The empirical measure could also be carried in a Fourier representation — the empirical characteristic function $\hat\varphi_n(s) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_i e^{\mathrm{i} s X_i}$ — but the histogram is the sole summary. Tail estimation (the heavy-tailed acceleration / $(\Delta X)^6$ channels) is robust only in the histogram, where the ECF inverse rings; the ECF would earn its place only for a strictly near-Gaussian marginal, which the consumers do not require. It is therefore deferred and, per the user, most likely never built.

This does not forgo the FFT — and the distinction is the crux. Storing the ECF as a sufficient statistic (an accumulation choice) is a different thing from using the FFT to smooth the histogram (a post-hoc computation choice). The former is dropped outright; the latter is the endorsed post-hoc optimization (§4.5), deferred (recorded, not built): smoothing a binned density or regression is a convolution of the bin counts with the kernel, which the FFT does in $O(B\log B)$ for $B$ bins and — decisively — amortizes over bandwidth (transform the counts once; each bandwidth $h$ is then a multiply by the kernel's transform). It is a pure optimization — the direct summation is already exact and dwarfed by the accumulation (§5) — so it waits for a consumer whose $h^\star(x)$ bandwidth grid makes it bite. So FFT-smoothing is the endorsed (if deferred) computation; the ECF summary is rejected as a stored representation.

Naming conventions

Following PF's mathematics-first naming:

  • Estimands / estimated quantities take the quantity's noun: density, conditional_mean, conditional_variance, conditional_density, transition_density, expectation.
  • Operations on the summary that are actions take the verb: observe! (fold a sample batch in), merge! (combine summaries), scale! (reweight the cone).
  • In-place forms append ! uniformly (observe!, merge!, scale!).
  • Standard named statistical objects would keep their standard name (a histogram, a characteristic_function) — a forward policy with no current instance; note the local-polynomial fits are one conditional_mean with a degree option, not separate nadaraya_watson / local_linear bindings.

Documentation policy — estimand first, estimator second

The mirror of PF's physical-meaning-first rule. Because every operation estimates a population quantity from data:

A docstring (and every doc-page entry) for an estimator states the estimand — the population quantity, in standard probability notation, with its defining formula — before the estimator that approximates it from the accumulated summary.

When an object is intrinsically finite-sample (a bandwidth selector, a merge!), say so plainly rather than forcing a population form. The full convention — the conditional_mean reference example, the symbol-binding, and the separate correctness/normalization check — lives with the documentation conventions (§7).

3. The accumulated summary — the universal lower half

These types are the load-bearing, domain-agnostic core; they are pure numerics over a stream and transfer verbatim to every consumer.

  • Histogram1D — a binned empirical measure of a conditioning variable $X$: linear (WARPing) bins over a fixed range (e.g. $\pm 5\sigma$, $\gtrsim 10^3$ bins), each bin carrying its count and per-bin power-sums about the bin center $S_p = \sum_{i\in\text{bin}}(X_i - c_j)^p$ for $p = 1 \dots P$ (default $P = 4$; raise $P$ for the heavy-tailed channels — a high marginal central moment such as $M_6$ needs $P = 6$, and its sampling variance reaches the 12th moment; the conditional increment moments of the Pawula quotient $Q$ (the fourth increment moment over thrice the squared second, §11) instead need the Histogram2D order-raise, §11). Because the offset $X_i - c_j$ is small within a bin ($\lesssim b/2 \approx \sigma/200$), the local power-sums are well-conditioned: they are accumulated by a plain atomic scatter-add — single-pass, numerically stable, with no compensated summation needed. The global central moments are recovered post hoc by the shift formula (the binscatter / WARPing local-moment construction; Scott 2015; Cattaneo et al. 2024), exactly (the power-sums use the actual $X_i$, not the center) and stably for the even high-order moments the consumers need.
  • Bins are exactly uniform — by design. The binning map is a single multiply-and-floor, i = floor((x - lower) * scale) + 1 with one scale = nbins / (upper - lower) — $O(1)$, branch-free, and allocation-free, the GPU atomic-scatter hot path (§5). This requires equal-width bins; arbitrary edges would force a per-sample search ($O(\log \texttt{nbins})$) and break the kernel. So the constructor takes the explicit (lower, upper, nbins), which states the uniform contract, rather than a vector or range of edges (which would merely happen to be uniform — and floating-point rounding need not even guarantee that). Non-uniform binning is out of scope.
  • Histogram2D — a joint binned measure of $(X, Y)$, one per lag $\tau$, for conditional and transition densities; per-cell counts and bivariate power-sums $S_{rs}$ about the cell center.
  • HistogramND [OPEN] — the $n$-D generalization, deferred until a consumer needs conditioning on more than one variable (the PF rule against speculative structure).
  • per-bin power-sums are the one summary scheme above, in a univariate form ($S_p$ of $X$, for the marginal moments and density) and a bivariate form ($S_{rs} = \sum_{i\in\text{bin}}(X_i - c^x)^{r}(Y_i - c^y)^{s}$ about the cell center, the local sufficient statistic for local-polynomial regression; Cattaneo et al. 2024). All bandwidths, kernels, and moments are recovered from them post hoc.
  • the mergeable-monoid (cone) interface — merge! on every summary is elementwise addition (same binning ⇒ same bin centers ⇒ counts and power-sums add); the weighted merge!(into, from; weight) and scale! extend it to the cone (§10). Tested: accumulate-then-merge $=$ accumulate-together, and the cone laws.
  • the backend-agnostic scatter-add primitive is observe! / observe_pairs! — one KernelAbstractions + Atomix kernel (one work-item per sample) that atomically scatters the count and the power-sums into the preallocated summary, the direct analog of PF's _bin_by_shell / anterpolate!. Allocation-free up to the kernel launch; verified identical on CPU and the AMD GPU under allowscalar(false) (§5). Storing power-sums about the bin center is precisely what keeps this a plain atomic add — no privatization, no workspace.

Numeric precision — accumulate wider than the data. Samples often arrive in Float32 (the GPU-first, low-memory consumers), but the summaries do not accumulate in Float32:

  • Counts are integers (UInt64 merged). Float32 represents integers exactly only up to $2^{24} = 16{,}777{,}216$ — beyond which adding one sample is silently lost — yet a single $256^3$ snapshot is exactly $2^{24}$ points, and a run accumulates $10^{10}$+ into the peak (modal) bins. Float32 counting is disqualified outright.
  • Power-sum accumulators default to Float64. The local sums are well-conditioned, but the global moment recovery (the shift formula combines them with large bin-center offsets) and the high-order channels still want full precision; Float32 would discard the ~7 digits the Float32 input carries, through the reduction over $n$. The power-sums accumulate in Float64 by default, regardless of input type.

Accumulator precision is a type parameter (PF-style element-type genericity) but its default is Float64, counts integer — accumulate-wider-than-the-data, the deliberate opposite of PF's preserve-the-field's-eltype (a reduction should out-precision its inputs; a transform should not). It costs almost nothing: the histogram is small (a few $\times10^3$ bins $\times$ a few Float64 power-sums — kilobytes per channel, versus a gigabyte field), and on the GPU the Float64 arithmetic is hidden behind the memory- and atomic-bound accumulation (§5), not FP64 throughput.

Two-time samples / lag handling — settled: the package bins pairs; the consumer produces them. For a two-time quantity like $m_\tau(u) = \mathbb{E}[Y_{t+\tau}\mid X_t = u]$ the conditioning value $X(t)$ and the response $Y(t+\tau)$ must be paired per source — two separate marginal histograms cannot recover the joint (for the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck (OU) oracle both marginals are the same stationary Gaussian; the entire $\tau$-dependence lives in the pair). The one histogram entry is made at the response step, into the bin of $X(t)$. The package surface for this is just the memoryless observe_pairs!(stat, x_past, y_now) — it bins a batch of pre-formed pairs into the per-lag summary (the lag is the summary's Histogram2D(…; lag) label, not an argument here; §4.2 / §4.3). (A pre-quantization path — a public binning map plus an index-taking observe_pairs! that folds a compact buffer of x-bin indices directly, lossless because the conditioning readers use only the x-cell and never its sub-bin offset — is deferred; the bin_index helper is archived until it lands.)

Everything that produces the pairs is the consumer's, being sample-source policy a domain-neutral package must not hold: the snapshot buffer of past $X(t)$ (held until the response arrives — the irreducible $O(M)$-per-lag state for $M$ sources, optionally stored as compact UInt16 bin indices when memory is tight); which sources to sample; which lags to form (e.g. log-spaced); and the origin cadence / buffer depth (a memory-vs-variance budget). Pairing is by source identity — for a stably-ordered set just element-wise array alignment (no id tag), an explicit id only if the ordering can change (re-meshing, sorting, birth/death).

What the package keeps is the per-lag histograms — the accumulated power-sums, the small output ($L \times$ bins $\times$ moments, a few MB), never the $O(ML)$ snapshot buffer (the expensive object, consumer-side). For the nonstationary case the running origin $t$ and lag $\tau$ are explicit labels on the summary set ($p_{t,\tau}$), never assumed away. (If the ring-and-pair boilerplate proves worth sharing, it can be a non-domain-aware utility offered alongside the summary — never something the package's vocabulary depends on.)

4. Estimand families (estimators — the exportable upper half)

Each estimand is written estimand first (per §2), grouped by the two readout families of the lens: §4.A kernel-weighting (the local / smoothing readouts through Kernels._kernel_weight — the marginal density §4.1, the conditional mean and variance §4.2, the conditional / transition density §4.3, and bandwidth selection §4.5) and §4.B power-sum quadrature (the global moment readouts off the stored $S_p$ — mean / var / central_moment, and expectation $\mathbb{E}[g(X)]$ / pushforward_density, all built). The §4.4 Kramers–Moyal / SDE-reading functionals are archived (in git history at commit 37006b5, restore when a consumer needs them and Histogram2D raises its per-cell order); §4.6 is an [OPEN] alternative.

4.A Kernel-weighting readouts

The local / smoothing readouts: each a degree-zero readout of the kernel-weighted measure through the Kernels._kernel_weight atom (§2), written estimand-first.

4.1 Marginal density $f_X$

Estimand. $f_X = \mathrm{d}\mu_X/\mathrm{d}\lambda$, the Lebesgue density: $P(X \in A) = \int_A f_X\,\mathrm{d}\lambda$. Estimator (binned KDE / WARPing): $\hat f_h(x) = \frac{1}{nh}\sum_j n_j K((x-\ell_j)/h)$ ($\int K = 1$) from counts $n_j$ at the within-bin centroid $\ell_j = c_j + S_1^{(j)}/n_j$ (binning = :linear, the default — matching the bin's first moment, so the discretization is $O(\Delta^2)$ in the bin width $\Delta$; $\ell_j = c_j$ is binning = :simple, $O(\Delta)$), the centroid free from the stored $S_1$. Bandwidth from §4.5; the kernel is pluggable. The tail and shape refinements are all post-hoc on the one summary — the adaptive variable bandwidth (cutting tail variance), the local-log-polynomial degree (cutting tail bias), a bounded support (support, or an asymmetric boundary kernel), a parametric start, and exact normalize — with density_standard_error the pointwise variability band; FORMALIZATION.md and the API page carry each one's estimand and constants. The axes are conceptually independent (each cures a different defect of the plain KDE), but not every pair combines: the table after this paragraph records which pairs do, which are refused as redundant, and which are refused only because the combination is not built. Caveats: the fixed range $[\texttt{lower}, \texttt{upper})$ drops events beyond it (cover the data — $\approx \pm 5\sigma$ for light tails, wider for heavy ones, with proportionally more bins to keep $b \ll h$), and uniform bins under-resolve power-law tails (the variable bandwidth is the preferred fix — it stays post-hoc, mergeable, and estimand-agnostic, which non-uniform bins would not).

4.2 Conditional expectation $m(x)$ and variance $\sigma^2(x)$

Estimand. $m(x) = \mathbb{E}[Y\mid X=x]$ (the $L^2(\sigma(X))$ projection; §2) and $\sigma^2(x) = \mathrm{Var}(Y\mid X=x) = \mathbb{E}[Y^2\mid X=x] - m(x)^2$. Estimator (local-linear; Fan & Gijbels 1996): from the joint Histogram2D's kernel-weighted power-sums $T_\ell = \sum_i K_h(X_i-x)(X_i-x)^\ell$ and $P_\ell = \sum_i K_h(X_i-x)(X_i-x)^\ell Y_i$,

\[\hat m_h(x) = \frac{T_2 P_0 - T_1 P_1}{T_0 T_2 - T_1^{2}}\]

— the $\beta_0$ of one LocalPolynomial.local_fit (§4.A atom); local-linear removes the design-density bias $2 m'(x) f'(x)/f(x)$ that the degree-0 Nadaraya–Watson $P_0/T_0$ carries. The variance is the bias-reduced residual-based Fan & Yao (1998) estimator (method = :residual, the default — a second local fit of the squared residuals $\widehat{\mathbb{E}}[(Y-\hat m)^2\mid x]$, removing the curved-$\mathbb{E}[Y^2\mid x]$ smoothing bias the :plugin $\widehat{\mathbb{E}}[Y^2\mid x] - \hat m^2$ carries). A one-step robust local M-estimator for heavy-tailed channels (Fan & Jiang 2000) is forthcoming.

The general reader conditional_expectation ($\mathbb{E}[g(Y)\mid x]$) subsumes both — a per-$X$-column $y$-quadrature of $g$ (the §4.B atom) regressed in $x$ by the same local_fit, so it joins the two families — with conditional_mean its $g = \mathrm{identity}$ case and the conditional pushforward density conditional_pushforward_density its transformed-response reader (one conditional_expectation of a composed kernel, no $g^{-1}$). Each carries a leverage-based conditional_expectation_standard_error, $\sqrt{\ell(x) \mathrm{Var}(g(Y)\mid x)}$, the conditional analog of the marginal expectation_standard_error. FORMALIZATION.md pins the constants and rates.

4.3 Conditional and transition density

Estimand. $f_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x) = f_{X,Y}(x,y)/f_X(x)$, and the two-time transition density $p_\tau(y\mid x) = f_{X_{t+\tau}\mid X_t}(y\mid x)$ (nonstationary: $p_{t,\tau}$). Estimator (double-kernel; Rosenblatt 1969; Hyndman, Bashtannyk & Grunwald 1996):

\[\hat f(y\mid x) = \frac{\sum_i K_{h_1}(X_i-x) K_{h_2}(Y_i-y)}{\sum_i K_{h_1}(X_i-x)},\]

with a separate conditioning kernel kernelx (in $x$) and response kernel kernely (in $y$). For a bounded / positive response — a droplet radius as $Y$ — kernely may be asymmetric (gamma / beta; Chen 1999, 2000), boundary-bias-free in $y$, with normalize restoring $\int\hat f\,\mathrm dy = 1$ — the conditional analog of the marginal density's asymmetric kernels. The conditioning kernel cancels in the $f_{X,Y}/f_X$ ratio, so it stays symmetric (a bounded conditioning variable needs none — the local-linear conditional_mean is boundary-adaptive, §4.2).

4.5 Bandwidth selection (cross-cutting)

Estimand. The estimand is the AMISE-optimal bandwidth (AMISE = asymptotic mean integrated squared error), the $h$ asymptotically minimising $\mathrm{MISE}(h) = \int \mathbb{E}[(\hat f_h - f_X)^2]$ for a kernel $K$ and local-polynomial degree $d$. Estimator. For a marginal the package uses the kernel- and degree-aware normal-reference rule $h = C(K,d) \hat\sigma n^{-1/(2p+1)}$ (bias order $p = 2$ at degree 0/1, $p = 4$ at degree 2/3; the kernel enters through one canonical factor $[R(K)/\mu_2(K)^2]^{1/5}$ by quadrature — Silverman $(4/3)^{1/5}$ the Gaussian/degree-0 case, Epanechnikov $\approx 2.34$), plus the data-driven Sheather–Jones :sj and DCT-based :isj (Botev, Grotowski & Kroese 2010) plug-ins, the fit-based :aic, and the asymmetric-kernel Chen :plugin (scale $b \sim n^{-2/5}$) — each a mergeable functional of the counts (the eval kernel a clean $\delta(K)$ rescale); leave-one-out CV is a recorded gap (binned ties drive $h \to 0$). The load-bearing one for conditional moments is the response-aware local plug-in bandwidth(::Histogram2D, x; rule = :local),

\[h^\star(x) = \biggl[\frac{R(K) \sigma^2(x)}{\mu_2(K)^2 m''(x)^2 f_X(x) n}\biggr]^{1/5},\]

which depends on the response's variance $\sigma^2(x)$ and curvature $m''(x)$ — so each response channel gets its own $h^\star$ (a single shared bandwidth is provably suboptimal), all three of $\sigma^2(x)$, $m''(x)$, $f_X(x)$ recovered from the one summary, post hoc. (Performance: every smoother here is a convolution of bin arrays with the kernel, so an FFT computes it in $O(B\log B)$ amortized over the bandwidth grid the $h^\star(x)$ sweep needs — Silverman 1982; Wand 1994 — a deferred optimization (recorded, not built), and in any case dwarfed by the per-sample accumulation hot loop, §5.)

4.B Power-sum quadrature readouts

The global (non-local) readouts: a smooth functional of $X$ read straight off the stored power-sums — no kernel, no query point. Within a bin, $S_p^{(j)} = \sum_{i\in\text{bin}}(X_i - c_j)^p$ is the within-bin $p$-th moment, so Taylor-expanding any smooth $g$ about the center,

\[\mathbb{E}[g(X)] = \frac{1}{n} \sum_j \sum_{p=0}^{\texttt{order}} \frac{g^{(p)}(c_j)}{p!} S_p^{(j)} + O\bigl(\Delta^{ \texttt{order}+1}\bigr),\]

exact for polynomials of degree $\le \texttt{order}$ (the remainder terminates) — this is the recovered mean / var / central_moment (§3, the shift formula, the $g = x^p$ case) — and $O(\Delta^{ \texttt{order}+1})$ for general smooth $g$. Built in Expectations:

  • expectation ($\mathbb{E}[g(X)]$) for a general smooth g. The per-bin coefficients $g^{(p)}(c_j)/p!$ are read off a degree-$\texttt{order}$ polynomial interpolant of $g$ on each bin — no automatic differentiation and no new dependency (uniform bins ⇒ one Chebyshev–Vandermonde inverse serves every bin), which reduces exactly to the shift formula on the monomials. Both expectation and the recovered moments route through the one shared contraction atom Expectations._quadrature (the moments supplying closed-form coefficients, expectation interpolation coefficients) — the literal realization of the two-family lens.
  • expectation_standard_error, $\sqrt{(\mathbb{E}[g^2] - \mathbb{E}[g]^2)/n}$ — the sampling error of $\widehat{\mathbb{E}}[g]$, itself a second quadrature readout ($\mathbb{E}[g^2]$ is expectation with the smooth integrand $g^2$).
  • pushforward_density ($f_{g(X)}$), the density of $Y = g(X)$. Rather than form $g^{-1}$ or a Jacobian, it is written as the expectation of the composed kernel test function, $\hat f_{g(X)}(y) = \tfrac{1}{n}\sum_i K_h(g(X_i) - y) = \widehat{\mathbb{E}}[K_h(g(X) - y)]$ — so it is one more expectation call. A non-monotone $g$ is handled automatically (every preimage contributes), a flat spot $g'=0$ shows up as the expected (integrable) caustic, and a symmetric $K$ makes $\int \hat f\,\mathrm dy = 1$ exactly.

Limits: $g$ smooth on the bin scale, mass inside the range, and — for the pushforward — the response bandwidth $h \gtrsim |g'| \Delta$ so $K_h\circ g$ is resolved across a bin.

4.6 Projection road [OPEN, alternative]

An alternative to bin-and-smooth for the conditional-functional / SDE-inference channels (the archived §4.4 road, or general regression fields): estimate by projecting onto a function basis with running inner-product accumulators (the Gram matrix and projection vector themselves a mergeable summary) — Stochastic Force Inference (Frishman & Ronceray 2020) and the underdamped ULI (Brückner, Ronceray & Broedersz 2020), fitting on $(X, \dot X)$ data. Shares the lower-half monoid foundation; deferred until a consumer wants parametric-in-basis fields.

5. Backend-agnosticism and the hot-loop contract

Inherited wholesale from PF (its invariants 6 and 13):

  • Storage is generic AbstractArray; no scalar indexing of device arrays in package methods; allocate via similar.
  • Accumulation is allocation-free. observe!(summary, batch) allocates only a bounded amount (the kernel launch) on a preallocated summary — never an $O(n)$ or $O(\text{bins})$ temporary, which on a GPU hot loop would escape the host GC and wedge the run. Enforced by an @allocated audit on the CPU (where a materialized array's bytes are visible), the direct analog of PF's test/allocations.jl.
  • One portable kernel (KernelAbstractions + Atomix) does the atomic scatter-add binning, on CPU and any GPU backend; the vendor stacks (CUDA/AMDGPU) stay test-only dependencies.
  • Mergeable across devices — merge! is the multi-device / multi-chunk reduction (§2), here just elementwise addition. The monoid laws are the spec; tested for associativity and accumulate-then- merge equality.
  • Contention, not FP64, is the hot-loop enemy. The conditioning variable is peaked (Gaussian-ish), so the modal bins are atomic hot spots. The power-sums-about-the-bin-center choice (§3) makes each update a plain atomic add — integer-exact for counts, round-off for the Float64 power-sums — so global atomics handle even heavy contention directly (the proven PF anterpolate! pattern, verified on the AMD GPU). Privatization (per-block local histograms in fast memory, merged hierarchically via the monoid) stays available as a future optimization should a channel's modal-bin contention dominate, but is not needed for correctness. This per-sample accumulation over $N^3\times$steps is the package's true performance-critical path; the post-hoc smoothing (§4.5) is second-order.

Getting backend-portability and mergeability right from the first commit is the one piece of "design for reuse now" that pays — both are expensive to retrofit and are the reason the package survives contact with three different execution models.

6. Invariants — deliberate absences are statements

The PF discipline: a missing capability encodes a decision. The candidate invariants:

  1. Accumulation is estimand-agnostic; smoothing is post hoc. No summary stores a bandwidth, kernel, or channel choice. An API that bakes a bandwidth into accumulation is the banned analog of PF's spectral product-as-*. (This is the invariant that makes "no reruns" true.)
  2. Samples in, statistics out — no domain, no model. Domain-neutral vocabulary (sample / variate / response / lag / conditioning value), never particle / tracer / droplet / velocity. No stationarity, Markov, diffusion, or parametric assumption is a premise; such structure is only ever tested. (The analog of PF's "no time, no particles.")
  3. Everything is a mergeable monoid — in fact a cone. Every summary supports merge!; the unnormalized summary is additive over the stream, and scalable (scale!), so the summaries form a convex cone (equal-weight merge! the built special case; the weighted merge! and scale! the cone operations — §10 hyperstatistics / forgetting). No non-mergeable accumulator is admitted.
  4. The hot path is allocation-free and backend-agnostic (§5).
  5. The summary is rich enough to recover every target post hoc. A target that would need a finer or different summary is a recorded coverage gap, not silently approximated — the "no reruns" promise made falsifiable.
  6. Accumulate wider than the data. Counts are integer; moment accumulators default to Float64 even on Float32 input (§3). A Float32 count saturates ($2^{24}$); a Float32 high-order moment loses the data's own precision through the reduction. (The deliberate opposite of PF's preserve-the-eltype, for the opposite reason: this is a reduction, not a transform.)

Deliberate absences (consumer-side, by scope). Bulk scalar time-correlation functions $C(\tau) = \langle X(t) X(t+\tau)\rangle$ are not provided: a Histogram2D per lag would store the whole joint density to extract one cross-moment ($O(T n_x n_y)$ state for $O(T)$ scalars), and a scalar correlation is cheaply streamed by a dedicated lagged product-sum or FFT. The Histogram2D-per-lag path is for the conditional / two-time density objects — $p_\tau(y \mid x)$, $D^{(n)}(x)$, the van Hove $G(r,t)$ — that genuinely need the joint, over a typically sparse set of lags; the consumer owns the scalar-correlation fast path.

7. Documentation, correctness, and testing conventions

Mirrors PF; the deltas are noted.

  • Estimand-first docstrings (§2) — state the estimand (the population quantity, in standard notation, with its formula) before the estimator. conditional_mean is the reference: lead with $m(x) = \mathbb{E}[Y \mid X = x] = \int y f_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x)\,\mathrm{d}y$ (the $L^2$ projection), then the local-linear $\hat m_h$. Self-contained pages, rendered LaTeX in standard notation, every code object bound to its math symbol on first mention, in apposition (density ($f_X$), conditional_mean ($m(x)$), transition_density ($p_\tau(y\mid x)$)).
  • Every estimator reports its standard error. An estimator approximates a population quantity from a finite stream, so it is incomplete without the sampling error of that approximation: every population-quantity estimator ships a companion *_standard_error (and the one-call paired *_with_standard_error) — a mergeable functional of the same summary, held to the check_standard_error contract. The exemptions are exactly estimand-first's: an intrinsically finite-sample object with no population estimand (bandwidth, a selector; merge! / scale!).
  • A FORMALIZATION.md analog. PF records a typed statement per operation, expansion-checked against the code and test-linked. Here the typed entry states the estimand, the estimator, and the consistency claim — its bias and variance, e.g. $\mathrm{bias} \hat m_h(x) \approx \tfrac{1}{2} h^2 \mu_2(K) m''(x)$ and $\mathrm{Var} \hat m_h(x) \approx \frac{R(K) \sigma^2(x)}{n h f_X(x)}$ for local-linear regression — every normalization constant pinned, each linked to the test that verifies the convergence rate numerically.
  • Testing against analytic oracles — the statistics analog of PF's analytic round-trips: estimators are checked against distributions with closed forms (Gaussian marginals; an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process, whose drift, diffusion, and transition density $p_\tau(y\mid x)$ are known exactly — the natural end-to-end oracle for §4.3–4.4), plus consistency / rate tests (error falls at the theoretical $n^{-2/5}$ as $n$ grows), mergeability tests, the @allocated audit, and GPU-vs-CPU parity under allowscalar(false).
  • The documentation checks are the shared tooling's — the rulebook of GBGCodingTools, run in every docs build, and its documentation review, read by a statistician and a software architect. What the package adds is the consistency and normalization of every estimator against its estimand — each estimator consistent for its estimand, every normalization exact (the $1/n$, $1/(nh)$, $\int K = 1$, $\mu_2(K)$, $R(K)$); a stray $1/h$ or kernel-moment factor is the easy slip, separate from formatting.

11. Decision log

Append-only; mirrors PF's decision log. The settled decisions of §9–§10 are recorded here as the first entries so later sessions extend rather than re-litigate them (the overlap with the body is intentional — do not dedupe it).

  • Package name — GBGStreamingStatistics (§9). Operationally precise; avoids OnlineStats.jl confusion. Alternatives GBGEmpiricalMeasures / GBGSufficientStatistics weighed and declined.

  • Numeric precision — integer counts, Float64 moment accumulators by default (§3, invariant 6), type-parameterized; Float32 input promoted at accumulation.

  • ECF summary — deferred, likely never built (§2). FFT-smoothing of the histogram is a separate, deferred post-hoc optimization (§4.5) — a computation choice, not a stored representation.

  • Lag handling — the package bins pre-formed pairs (observe_pairs!); the pre-quantization / index-ingestion path (bin_index) is deferred (archived); the snapshot buffer and all lag / sampling / cadence policy stay consumer-side (§3).

  • Summary storage — power-sums about the bin center, not Pébay-about-the-running-mean (§3). Changed during the GPU pass. The original design accumulated per-bin central moments by the Pébay (2008) running-mean recursion with Kahan compensation. A Pébay update is not atomic, so the GPU hot loop would have needed per-block privatization (private histograms merged hierarchically) — a pattern the sibling PF uses nowhere. Switching to power-sums $S_p = \sum (X_i - c_j)^p$ about the fixed bin center makes observe! a plain atomic scatter-add (PF's proven anterpolate! pattern): allocation-free, one portable kernel identical on CPU and the AMD GPU, merge! reduced to elementwise addition, and the marginal moments unified with the regression power-sums. The offset $X_i - c_j$ is tiny, so the local sums are well-conditioned (Kahan no longer needed); global central moments are recovered post hoc by the shift formula — exact, and stable for the even high-order marginal moments the consumers need (odd moments keep weaker relative precision, an accepted trade). Verified by the ROCm GPU parity suite.

  • Kramers–Moyal §4.4 — drift and diffusion first, as conditional increment moments (§4.4). [Superseded — these functionals were built and OU-tested, then archived; see the archival entries below.] The first slice implements the increment moments and the finite-$\tau$ Kramers–Moyal coefficients drift $D^{(1)} = M_1/\tau$ and diffusion $D^{(2)} = M_2/(2\tau)$ directly from the order-2 Histogram2D, via $M_1(x) = \hat m_h(x) - x$ and $M_2(x) = \widehat{\mathbb{E}}[Y^2\mid x] - 2x \hat m_h(x) + x^2$ — reusing the §4.2 local-linear primitives (no new accumulator), reported as the plain finite-$\tau$ ratio $M_n/(n! \tau)$. The all-orders Bell-polynomial finite-time correction (Rydin Gorjão et al. 2021) and the $\tau\to0$ limit stay forthcoming / consumer-side. $n \ge 3$ — the higher Kramers–Moyal coefficients and pawula_quotient (whose $Q = \mu_4/(3\mu_2^2)$ needs $M_4$) — is a recorded coverage gap (it needs the joint summary raised to order-$n$ per-cell $Y$ power-sums, deferred per invariant 5); markov_test composes the now-available §4.3 transition density. OU-oracle-tested: drift recovers the finite-$\tau$ value $x(e^{-\theta\tau}-1)/\tau$ exactly (no smoothing bias — the OU conditional mean is linear — $\to -\theta x$ as $\tau \to 0$), diffusion $\to \sigma^2/2$ carrying the local-linear bias of the curved $\mathbb{E}[Y^2\mid x]$.

  • Conditional / transition density §4.3 — binned double-kernel from the joint counts (§4.3). The Rosenblatt double-kernel estimator (§4.3) is evaluated from the Histogram2D cell counts (no power-sums needed): at the conditioning value $x$ the joint is reduced to an $x$-kernel-weighted Y-profile $P_k = \sum_j n_{jk} K_{h_1}(c^x_j - x)$ with $D = \sum_k P_k$, and then $\hat f(y\mid x) = \sum_k P_k K_{h_2}(c^y_k - y) / (h_2 D)$ for a symmetric kernel $K$ (any $\int K = 1$, default Gaussian). The $x$-kernel weighting cancels in the conditional ratio — only the $1/h_2$ survives, and $\int K = 1$ gives $\int \hat f(y\mid x)\,\mathrm dy = 1$ exactly. transition_density is the same computation on a lag-$\tau$ joint (the two-time reading). The two bandwidths $h_1$ (hx, conditioning) and $h_2$ (hy, response) and the kernel are supplied; the conditional MISE rate is the slower $O(n^{-2/3})$. OU-oracle-tested against the closed-form Gaussian $p_\tau(y\mid x)$.

  • Bandwidth §4.5 — the marginal bandwidth made kernel- and degree-aware (§4.5). Generalised the global rule. The first slice returned only Silverman $(4/3)^{1/5}\hat\sigma n^{-1/5}$ (Gaussian, degree 0; rules :silverman/:scott). A survey of the field (locfit, KernSmooth, ks, statsmodels) confirmed the AMISE-optimal $h$ depends on the kernel only through the canonical factor $\delta(K) = [R(K)/\mu_2(K)^2]^{1/5}$ and on the local-polynomial degree through both the rate ($n^{-1/5}$ at degree 0/1, $n^{-1/9}$ at degree 2) and the equivalent-kernel constant — exactly locfit's Wikk(K, d). bandwidth(summary; rule = :normal, kernel, degree) now returns $C(K,d) \hat\sigma n^{-1/(2p+1)}$ ($R(K)$, $\mu_2(K)$ by quadrature, so any kernel works) and is also density's default h, so a degree-2 or Epanechnikov call is matched automatically. The kernel-as-standard-deviation shortcut (base-R, KDEpy, KernelDensity.jl — kernel-blind, not AMISE-exact) was weighed and declined. The data-driven Sheather–Jones plug-in (rule = :sj, degree 0/1) is now added — the $\psi_4$/$\psi_6$ curvature functionals as mergeable Hermite-weighted reductions of the bin counts, the solve-the-equation root-find (base-R bw.SJ ste), the eval kernel a clean $\delta(K)$ rescale (the pilot stays Gaussian, kernel-independent) — much tighter than the normal reference on a bimodal sample. The fit-based AIC follows (next entry).

  • Bandwidth §4.5 — fit-based AIC added; leave-one-out CV is a deliberate absence (§4.5). The survey flagged Loader's preferred route as fit-based selection over an $h$-grid. Implemented as rule = :aic: minimise the binned-KDE AIC $-2\sum_j n_j\log\hat f_h(c_j) + 2\mathrm{df}(h)$ ($\mathrm{df}$ the effective degrees of freedom, the smoother's self-influence trace) over the grid — a mergeable functional of the counts, the eval kernel a $\delta(K)$ rescale. But the cross-validation selectors (likelihood-CV and least-squares LSCV) are not built: they are leave-one-out, and a binned summary has heavy ties (≈10²–10³ samples per bin) whose same-bin term drives the CV objective to $h \to 0$ (verified — LSCV's $(1/2 + S/n)/(nh\sqrt\pi)$ numerator goes negative, so it diverges to $-\infty$ as $h \to 0$). base-R bw.ucv survives only via coarse bins, a constrained search range, and a boundary warning. AIC needs no leave-one-out — both its log-likelihood and df-penalty diverge as $h \to 0$ — so its optimum is a stable interior point. CV recorded as a coverage gap (a within-bin-spread correction from the power-sums could rescue it); :sj, :isj, and :aic are the data-driven selectors.

  • Bandwidth §4.5 — Improved Sheather–Jones (rule = :isj) added (§4.5). The classic :sj plug-in estimates the curvature $\psi_4$ from a normal-reference pilot, which can mislead on strongly multimodal data (the Marron–Wand claw). Botev, Grotowski & Kroese (2010) instead read the curvature norms $\|f^{(s)}\|^2$ off the DCT of the bin counts and solve the AMISE fixed point — a fully nonparametric pilot. Added as rule = :isj: a mergeable functional of the counts' cosine transform, the eval kernel the same $\delta(K)$ rescale, robust where :sj's pilot misleads.

  • Density §4.1 — parametric start and exact normalization added (§4.1). A second pass over the downloaded reference software (studying source, not docs) found two capabilities worth mirroring: the parametric start (Hjort & Glad 1995; kdensity's headline feature) and the exact renormalization (kdensity's normalized). The start is the semiparametric $\hat f = p(\cdot;\hat\theta)\cdot\widehat{(f/p)}$ — a moment-fitted model times a KDE of its correction — with five moment-fittable starts (normal_start, lognormal_start, gamma_start, exponential_start, inverse_gaussian_start); it stays in-discipline because $\hat\theta$ is a functional of the recovered moments, so the whole estimate is mergeable, and it is a bias reduction (the leading bias is driven by $(f/p)''$), valuable for the skewed positive shapes the package targets (droplet radii). normalize=true divides any estimate by its integral over the support so $\int\hat f = 1$ exactly — the finishing step for the non-self-normalizing asymmetric kernels and starts. Both are post-hoc degree-0 options; start does not compose with $\texttt{degree} \ge 1$, adaptive, support, or the asymmetric kernels (the local-likelihood-with-start generalization is a recorded gap). The cross-validated start bandwidth (KernelEstimator.jl) and the Hjort–Jones locally parametric generalization were weighed and deferred. The same source pass also corrected the gamma/beta kernels from Chen's naive $f_1$ to the bias-reduced $f_2$ (a fix, committed separately).

  • Bandwidth §4.5 — Chen plug-in for the asymmetric kernels; AIC, U-statistic, and gamma-reference routes rejected (§4.5). The asymmetric (gamma / beta) kernels shipped only the $\hat\sigma n^{-2/5}$ rule of thumb; a data-driven selector was wanted because the optimal scale is $0.27\text{–}1.6\times$ it across densities ($> 2\times$ ISE on log-normal — the droplet regime). Three routes were tried and rejected before the plug-in: the binned AIC (as for the symmetric kernels) is monotonic in $b$ — the gamma self-influence $\sim 1/\sqrt{xb}$ makes the df penalty too weak against the flat binned likelihood of dense data, so it oversmooths without bound; the $\psi_4$ U-statistic curvature (the $:sj$ machinery) assumes $f$ vanishes at the boundary (integration by parts) and goes negative on the half-line where $f(0) > 0$ (e.g. the exponential); a gamma-reference plug-in is pathological for shape $k \le 1$ (a non-integrable $f''$ boundary singularity, $b^\star \to 0$). The shipped rule = :plugin is the Chen plug-in with the curvature $\int w^2 (f'')^2$ (weight $w = x$ / $x(1-x)$) estimated by grid quadrature of the pilot $\hat f''$ — boundary-valid because $w \to 0$ there, and always real. The $f''$ pilot is a normal-reference scale $1.3 \hat\sigma n^{-1/9}$ (constant calibrated for robustness, as Silverman's 1.06 is): never materially worse than the rule of thumb, $1.4\text{–}1.7\times$ lower ISE on skewed data. A fully-adaptive (solve-the-equation) curvature pilot is a recorded refinement.

  • Hyperstatistics — the cone: scale! + weighted merge! over a Float-count variant (§10). Built. The unnormalised summary is scalable, so the summaries form a convex cone, not merely an additive monoid. Realized as scale!(summary, weight) (the scalar action — every count and power-sum $\times$ weight) and the weighted merge!(into, from; weight) ($\texttt{into} \mathrel{+}= \texttt{weight} \cdot \texttt{from}$; $\texttt{weight} = 1$ recovers the plain monoid add), on a Float-count variant (count_type = Float64; the default UInt64 counts cannot hold a fractional $w n$, so scale! and a weighted merge ArgumentError on them). The summary storage needed almost no change — observe!'s atomic scatter already adds one(eltype(counts)), so a Float count routes through the atomic-float add (CPU and GPU), and merge! was already an elementwise broadcast. Estimand: the hyperstatistical parameter-mixture density $P(x) = \int f(\beta) P(x\mid\beta)\,\mathrm{d}\beta \approx \sum_k w_k f_k(x) = \sum_k (w_k/N_k) T_k$ (superstatistics; Beck & Cohen 2003) — keep one summary $T_k$ per $\beta_k$, choose the mixing law $f(\beta)$ post hoc, re-weight without re-accumulating; and exponential forgetting (scale!(s, λ) before the next block). Oracle-tested by the χ²-superstatistics → Student-$t$ result (a Gamma-weighted Gaussian superposition has the heavy $t$ tail), a discrete Gaussian mixture, the cone laws, exact forgetting, the Histogram2D cone, and GPU parity (test/hyperstatistics.jl, FORMALIZATION.md). One caveat recorded: the mixture's total mass is $\sum_k w_k$ ($\approx 1$ for a normalized law), so its default bandwidth reads $n \approx 1$ — pass an explicit $h$ (or rescale the weights to a representative sample size). An effective-sample-size bandwidth for weighted summaries (Kish $(\sum_i w_i)^2 / \sum_i w_i^2$) is a possible refinement, not built.

  • Linear (centroid) binning — the default for density (§4.1). Built. The binned KDE places each bin's mass at a representative location $\ell_j$. The geometric center $\ell_j = c_j$ (simple binning) leaves a nonzero within-bin first moment, an $O(\Delta)$ discretization of the exact KDE; placing the mass at the recovered within-bin centroid $\ell_j = c_j + S_1^{(j)}/n_j$ zeroes that first moment ($\sum_{i\in j}(X_i - \ell_j) = 0$), so the leading term cancels and the discretization error drops to $O(\Delta^2)$ — the centroid analog of Hall–Wand linear binning. The first power-sum $S_1$ is already stored, so the refinement is free, and it is the new default (binning = :linear; binning = :simple keeps the center). One change threads it everywhere: every density path reads a centers vector, so the KDE, the local-log fit, the parametric start, and the asymmetric kernels all inherit the centroid at no extra cost. Oracle-tested by the binning-error convergence sub-test (test/density_convergence.jl: a log–log slope $\approx 2$ in $\Delta$ for :linear against $\approx 1$ for :simple, the linear estimate an order of magnitude tighter at a fine grid). A higher-order correction using the stored $S_2$ is conceivable but not built.

  • Estimator architecture — the linear jet-smoother is the primitive, local likelihood a consumer; and the measure-algebra lens (§2, §4). A framing decision, landed in the code. Two settlements taken together. (1) The lens. The spine is given an algebraic reading (§2): the summaries are the cone of measures, $T$ is the linear cone homomorphism (so merge! is addition), and every reported estimand is homogeneous of degree zero in the measure, descending to the projectivization (the probability measures) — exactly the scale!-invariance §10 tests, and exactly why the post-hoc readout is not a homomorphism. Adopted as a structural lens only; the motivation stays mathematical statistics grounded in practice (consistency, rates, oracles). (2) The primitive. Two abstractions were weighed for sharing code between density and conditional_mean: a local generalized linear model (local likelihood — density at degree $\ge 1$ is a local Poisson/log fit, conditional_mean a local Gaussian/identity fit; Loader 1996, Fan & Gijbels 1996), versus a linear local-polynomial jet-smoother (smooth the binned measure, return the local Taylor coefficients $(\beta_0, \beta_1, \dots)$; conditioning a ratio of two such). They are not competitors: a local GLM is fit by iteratively reweighted least squares, so each Newton step is itself a kernel-weighted local-polynomial solve, and the identity-link case (regression) converges in one step to the plain solve. The GLM is therefore built from the linear solve, not parallel to it. So the linear jet-smoother is the irreducible primitive (local_fit), and the GLM is one of three thin consumers: a link-cap (the density's $e^{\beta_0}$, the IRLS loop at degree $\ge 1$), a ratio (conditioning — conditional_density, the disintegration of the joint), and a jet component (the derivative functionals drift, diffusion, and $h^\star(x)$). This keeps the lone nonlinearity on one call site and the hot, allocation-free, GPU-portable path linear. Landed (Stages 1–3): the shared LocalPolynomial.local_fit (the jet-smoother, returning $(\beta_0, \beta_1, \beta_2)$) now backs conditional_mean (its $\beta_0$), the local-likelihood density (the IRLS inner solve), and the increment moments — replacing the formerly duplicated ConditionalExpectations._local_linear (degree-1, intercept only) and the bespoke Newton step in MarginalDensity._local_log. On it: conditional_mean / conditional_variance gained the degree (0/1/2) and kernel options; conditional_variance the bias-reduced Fan & Yao default (method = :residual); and the response-aware local bandwidth $h^\star(x)$ = bandwidth(::Histogram2D, x; rule = :local), which reads the curvature $m''(x) = 2\beta_2$ of a degree-2 fit together with $\sigma^2(x)$ and $f_X(x)$. It landed as the substrate of that feature, not as a standalone refactor (convenience is not a justification, §6). Settled by estimation theory (iteratively reweighted least squares is the linear solve iterated; the smoother is linear on the cone), not implementation convenience.

  • Kernels — the asymmetric kernels consolidated into the foundation module (§4.1, §4.3). The varying-shape boundary kernels (gamma_kernel, beta_kernel, the AsymmetricKernel type; Chen 1999,

    1. were moved from MarginalDensity to Kernels, joining the symmetric gaussian_kernel /

    epanechnikov_kernel. They had stayed in MarginalDensity only because, when Kernels was first extracted, density was their sole consumer — a historical artifact, not a design boundary. A kernel is a kernel: an asymmetric boundary kernel is a smoothing kernel for bounded / positive support, so it belongs in the shared foundation, where any density estimator — marginal or conditional — can reach it. This gives the kernel argument one documented home (no cross-module split) and enabled the now-built asymmetric-kernel conditional density (§4.3: a bounded response — e.g. a droplet radius as $Y$ — takes a boundary-free $y$-kernel via conditional_density(…; kernely = gamma_kernel, normalize = true), the same first-order boundary-bias fix the marginal density already has). It does not motivate asymmetric conditioning in conditional_mean: local-linear regression is boundary-adaptive (Fan & Gijbels 1996), already correcting the $O(h)$ boundary bias to $O(h^2)$ with a symmetric kernel, so an asymmetric conditioning kernel would be a boundary-variance refinement only, not a correctness fix — a recorded possibility, not built (convenience / parity is not a justification, §6).

  • Kernels / LocalPolynomial — the kernel-weighting atom _kernel_weight; the lens corrected to two readout families (§2, §4). The symmetric/asymmetric kernel protocols, previously re-coded inline at four sites (local_fit, the density KDE, its asymmetric branch, the conditional density), are unified in one Kernels._kernel_weight (the $1/h$ folded in, so every readout — count sum, Nadaraya–Watson ratio, jet — is uniform; the fold is $\beta$-invariant for the local-polynomial solve, so symmetric results are bit-unchanged). local_fit carries the one guard: an asymmetric kernel has no jet, so it is admitted at $\texttt{degree} = 0$ only (a clean ArgumentError, not the former MethodError). This made degree-0 conditional_mean accept asymmetric kernels uniformly — building the degree-0 conditioning-side asymmetric kernel the entry above had left "not built". Honest finding: the Chen gamma/beta kernels are a density boundary tool; their asymmetry interacts with the regression design density, so asymmetric-kernel Nadaraya–Watson does not generally improve the conditional mean (degree-1 local-linear — boundary-adaptive, unbiased for a linear mean — is the regression tool). The capability is admitted for uniformity of the abstraction, not as the recommended regression estimator; the code is uniform, the guidance separate (in conditional_mean). The episode also corrected the lens: local_fit is the kernel-weighting family's primitive, not the universal one — $\mathbb{E}[g(X)]$ is a power-sum-quadrature readout with no jet, the second family. Settled by the measure-algebra lens, not convenience.

  • IncrementMoments §4.4 — archived (§4). The SDE-reading drift / diffusion / Kramers–Moyal functionals (working, OU-tested; Pawula / Markov were stubs) moved to archive/ to focus the package on its universal statistical core — they are thin functionals of conditional_mean, the most physics-flavored, least-complete, most consumer-side part, and the identity is sharper without them. Restore when a consumer needs them and Histogram2D raises its per-cell order for $n \ge 3$.

  • FFT-amortized smoothing — deferred (recorded, not built) (§4.5). Every reported quantity is already computed exactly by direct summation over the few $\times 10^3$ bins, and that cost is dwarfed by the per-sample accumulation (§5), so the FFT is a pure amortization win — decisive only once a consumer sweeps a grid of bandwidths (the local $h^\star(x)$ road). Recorded here and in §4.5, to be built when that cost bites. FFTW is retained — the ISJ bandwidth (rule = :isj) uses its DCT today; the unused AbstractFFTs direct dependency (its sole intended consumer was this deferred smoothing) was dropped, per the rule against speculative structure (§6).

  • Power-sum quadrature readouts §4.B — built (expectation, expectation_standard_error, pushforward_density). The second readout family lands: a smooth functional $\mathbb{E}[g(X)]$ of the binned measure, its sampling standard error, and the pushforward density of $g(X)$. All three route through the one shared contraction atom Expectations._quadrature, into which mean / var / central_moment were folded as the $g = x^p$ case (guarded by the unchanged exactness tests) — the literal realization of the two-family lens. Decision — the boundary between the two families is that they consume different sufficient statistics: kernel-weighting (§4.A) reads counts + bin locations, weights by $K_h$, and localizes/solves (a function of a query point, needs a bandwidth); power-sum quadrature (§4.B) contracts the within-bin power-sums $S_p$ against a test function's local coefficients (a scalar, no bandwidth). The summary stores both counts and power-sums precisely because there are two readout modes. This settles the tempting over-unification: the kernel estimators are not trivial cases of $\mathbb{E}[g(X)]$ (they are ratios / local solves of joint moments; only the bare degree-0 fixed-$h$ KDE is formally $\mathbb{E}[K_h(\cdot - x_0)]$), so a single universal map would collapse to the vacuous "every estimator is a functional of the empirical measure" — the mirror of the corrected "the jet is the primitive" lens. Two atoms, each estimator a readout of one. g's coefficients come from a dependency-free local polynomial interpolant; the pushforward is expectation of $K_h\circ g$.

  • ConditionalExpectations §4.2 — general $\mathbb{E}[g(Y)\mid x]$ + conditional pushforward, built. The general conditional_expectation (with conditional_mean folded in as $g = \mathrm{identity}$, and conditional_pushforward_density ($f_{g(Y)\mid X}$)) makes the conditional side symmetric with the marginal Expectations. Refines the two-family boundary: conditional functionals compose the atoms — a per-$X$-column power-sum quadrature in $y$ (§4.B) fed as the response to a kernel-weighted regression in $x$ (§4.A). So "each estimator is a readout of one atom" (the §4.B entry above) softens to "readouts are built from the atoms, sometimes composing them" — conditional_mean always did (its trivial $\sum Y$ response is the $y$-quadrature of the identity); the general $g$ just makes the inner $y$-readout non-trivial. The order-2 $Y$-power-sums cap a general $g$ at $O(\Delta_y^2)$.

  • The argument audit (2026-10-15). Every exported method's arguments and defaults reviewed under the rules the owner adopted from the sibling packages (a keyword shows its real default; nothing only for a genuine absence; one name per concept and one concept per name; no placeholder replaced on entry). Done, each its own commit: the histogram constructors' accumulator_type / count_type; density's bounds → support (the start's and the kernel's name for the same tuple); the asymmetric kernel's local_scale_weight (the cone's weight is a different quantity) and pilot_bandwidth; the conditional estimators' degree defaulting to 0 under an asymmetric kernel, where 1 was refused; and density's h = nothing, replaced by its measured equal bandwidth(summary; kernel, degree), which moved the package's argument order to kernel → degree → bandwidth everywhere (a default sees only what precedes it). Not done, with reasons: the local plug-in's pilot_bandwidth = nothing stays a computed default (a public pilot function for one default is not worth its surface; to reconsider); h keeps carrying the asymmetric kernels' scale $b$ (one smoothing parameter per kernel, the kernel owning its parametrization, as kde(…; bandwidth, kernel) does elsewhere); and the density variants stay keywords rather than one estimator value — the axes are independent corrections, and the refused pairs are redundancies or undeveloped estimators, now tabulated in §4.1, not a reason to redesign.
