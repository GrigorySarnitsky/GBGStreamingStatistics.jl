GBGStreamingStatistics — Architecture and Rationale

This document explains why GBGStreamingStatistics is built the way it is, in the style of its sibling GBGPeriodicFields (PF). It is self-contained: every symbol — a math symbol or a code identifier — is introduced in plain language at its first use, and it reads top to bottom. All mathematics is rendered LaTeX in standard probability notation. The naming rationale is in §9; deliberate absences are stated as invariants in §6; open decisions are flagged [OPEN].

0. Provenance

The driving requirement is conditional statistics of the highest quality with no storage of the underlying state and no recompute-from-raw reruns — which forces the central architectural choice of §2.

The package is designed to be shared across at least three consumers — turbulence (Lagrangian tracer statistics: conditional averages, transition densities, Kramers–Moyal coefficients), cloud microphysics (droplet growth conditioned on size and supersaturation), and molecular dynamics (the van Hove and intermediate scattering functions are two-time conditional densities) — so it is domain-neutral from the first commit.

1. Purpose and scope

A backend-agnostic library for online nonparametric statistics: it estimates marginal densities, conditional expectations, conditional and two-time transition densities, and standard functionals of these, from a stream of samples that is never stored. Each sample is observed once, folded into a fixed-size summary, and discarded.

The relationship to GBGPeriodicFields is exact and deliberate:

PF is all mathematics, no physics. This package is all statistics, no physics.

PF provides field operations a solver consumes; it carries no concept of time, no particle, no tracer (those are the consumer's). This package provides estimators a solver consumes; it carries no concept of velocity, droplet, trajectory, or turbulence — and, one level deeper, no probabilistic model: the data is not assumed stationary, Markov, diffusive, or parametric. Those are consumer hypotheses the package's diagnostics test, never premises its estimators require.

Boundary — samples in, statistics out. The package never sees a grid, an FFT, a tracer, or a droplet. It consumes a stream of values (scalars / vectors, optionally lagged pairs tagged by a lag $\tau$) and returns estimators. The sampling and transport layer — interpolating off an Eulerian field, reading particle states, walking parcel trajectories — stays in each consumer. The instant a grid or tracer concept crosses this line, reuse across the three domains dies.

Execution target, inherited from PF: a single node, shared-memory only (CPU multithreading and a single GPU); distributed / multi-GPU is out of scope. Development is GPU-first but the code is backend-agnostic (CPU Array, CUDA, ROCm) through KernelAbstractions — §5.

2. The central principle: the architecture follows established statistics

Every abstraction is the code image of a standard object of probability and mathematical statistics, and design decisions are settled by estimation theory, not implementation convenience — the direct analog of PF's "architecture follows established mathematics." As in PF, the recurring lesson is that an absence is a precise statement (§6).

The spine: empirical measure → summary → post-hoc-smoothed estimator

Let $X_1, X_2, \dots$ be the observed stream. The object that carries all information is the empirical measure

\[\hat\mu_n \;=\; \frac1n \sum_{i=1}^{n} \delta_{X_i},\]

where $\delta_x$ is the unit point mass at $x$ and $n$ the number of samples seen. We do not store the $n$ point masses. We store a finite-dimensional summary $T(\hat\mu_n)$ that is

  1. mergeable — $T$ is an additive monoid over the sample stream: the unnormalized summary of a batch is a running sum, so two batches' summaries combine by merge! and accumulate-then-merge equals accumulate-together (to round-off). This is just additivity of measures, and it is what makes multi-device reduction and time-chunk stitching free (§5, §6);
  2. rich enough that every reported quantity is recoverable from it post hoc, with no second pass over the data.

Every reported quantity is then an estimator — a functional

\[\hat\theta \;=\; S_h\!\big(T(\hat\mu_n)\big)\]

of that summary, where $S_h$ is a smoothing operator (a choice of kernel, bandwidth $h$, and any secondary smoothing). The load-bearing decision:

The summary $T$ is accumulated online and is estimand-agnostic; the smoothing $S_h$ is applied entirely post hoc. Accumulation knows nothing of bandwidth, kernel, or which response channel will be asked for. This separation is the architectural invariant of the package — the analog of PF's representation/operator split — and it is exactly what delivers the user's requirement: no state storage (only $T$ survives a step) and no reruns (every $h$, every channel, every secondary smoothing is computed from the one stored $T$).

Why a fine histogram is the canonical $T$ (and the honest caveat)

The canonical summary is a finely binned empirical measure — a histogram, the fine-grained measure that post-hoc smoothing (a choice of bandwidth $h$) later coarse-grains into each reported estimate — optionally carrying per-bin moments (below). For nonparametric estimation there is, strictly, no finite sufficient statistic; the histogram is lossy. The justification is quantitative and sets the bin count: with bin width $b$ and smoothing bandwidth $h$, the binning error is $O(b^2)$ while the irreducible smoothing bias is $O(h^2)$, so choosing $b \ll h$ (in practice $\gtrsim 10^3$ bins over $\pm 5\sigma$, so $b \approx \sigma/100 \ll h$) makes the summary approximately sufficient: it loses nothing at the target accuracy (the WARPing / binned-KDE / average-shifted-histogram result, Hall & Wand 1996; Scott 2015). We therefore say "approximately-sufficient summary," never "sufficient statistic" — the latter would be the category overclaim PF's correctness policy exists to catch.

Estimand families (the operation taxonomy)

Mirroring PF's operation families (vertical algebra / differential / reductions / transfer), the package's families are organized by estimand — the population quantity targeted. Each is detailed in §4:

  • Marginal density $f_X$ — from a 1-D histogram.
  • Conditional expectation $m(x) = \mathbb{E}[Y \mid X = x]$ and conditional variance — from per-bin power-sums.
  • Conditional / transition density $f_{Y\mid X}$, $p_\tau(y \mid x)$ — from a 2-D (joint) histogram, per lag.
  • Conditional increment moments and their functionals — Kramers–Moyal coefficients, drift/diffusion, Pawula / Q diagnostics — domain-neutral functionals of the above; the SDE reading stays consumer-side.
  • Bandwidth selection (cross-cutting) — global and response-aware local.
  • [OPEN] a streaming projection road (basis projection à la Stochastic Force Inference / ULI) as an alternative accumulator.

The histogram is the summary; the ECF is deferred (likely never needed)

The empirical measure could also be carried in a Fourier representation — the empirical characteristic function $\hat\varphi_n(s) = \frac1n\sum_i e^{\mathrm{i} s X_i}$ — but the histogram is the sole summary. Tail estimation (the heavy-tailed acceleration / $(\Delta X)^6$ channels) is robust only in the histogram, where the ECF inverse rings; the ECF would earn its place only for a strictly near-Gaussian marginal, which the consumers do not require. It is therefore deferred and, per the user, most likely never built.

This does not forgo the FFT — and the distinction is the crux. Storing the ECF as a sufficient statistic (an accumulation choice) is a different thing from using the FFT to smooth the histogram (a post-hoc computation choice). The former is dropped; the latter is kept as a major performance lever (§4.5): smoothing a binned density or regression is a convolution of the bin counts with the kernel, which the FFT does in $O(B\log B)$ for $B$ bins and — decisively — amortizes over bandwidth (transform the counts once; each bandwidth $h$ is then a multiply by the kernel's transform). So FFT-smoothing is recommended; the ECF summary is not.

Naming conventions

Following PF's mathematics-first naming:

  • Estimands / estimated quantities take the quantity's noun: density, conditional_mean, conditional_variance, conditional_density, transition_density, drift, diffusion.
  • Operations on the summary that are actions take the verb: observe! (fold a sample batch in), merge! (combine summaries), estimate/smooth.
  • In-place forms append ! uniformly (observe!, merge!).
  • Standard named statistical objects keep their standard name (histogram, characteristic_function, the eponymous nadaraya_watson, local_linear).

Documentation policy — estimand first, estimator second

This is the user's explicit requirement and the exact mirror of PF's "physical-space meaning first — even for spectral-representation operations." In PF, because a field is a real object and spectral is only a representation, a docstring must state what the operation does to the real object in physical space — with the formula — before the spectral form it actually computes. Here, because every operation estimates a population quantity from data:

A docstring (and every doc-page entry) for an estimator must state the estimand — the population quantity, in standard probability notation, with its defining formula — before the estimator that approximates it from the accumulated summary.

conditional_mean is the intended reference implementation. It must lead with the estimand

\[m(x) \;=\; \mathbb{E}[Y \mid X = x] \;=\; \int y\, f_{Y\mid X}(y \mid x)\,\mathrm{d}y\]

— named as the $L^2$ projection of $Y$ onto $\sigma(X)$, the regression function — and only then give the local-linear estimator $\hat m_h(x)$ computed from the per-bin power-sums. State the estimand whenever a clean population definition exists; when an object is intrinsically finite-sample (a bandwidth selector, a merge!), say so plainly rather than forcing a population form — the analog of PF allowing "this is the symbol of $-\nabla^2$, not a kernel" when no clean physical form exists.

As in PF, the symbol is bound to the code on first mention: density $\equiv f_X$, conditional_mean $\equiv m(x)$, transition_density $\equiv p_\tau(y\mid x)$, drift $\equiv D^{(1)}$, diffusion $\equiv D^{(2)}$.

Correctness is its own check (PF's separate-from-formatting rule). Verify the estimator is actually consistent for the stated estimand under the stated assumptions, and that every normalization is exact: the $1/n$, the $1/(nh)$, $\int K = 1$, the kernel moments $\mu_2(K) = \int u^2 K(u)\,\mathrm{d}u$ and roughness $R(K) = \int K^2$, the bias/variance constants. A stray $1/h$ or a kernel-moment factor is the easy slip here — the statistics analog of PF's stray $1/N$ or Parseval factor.

3. The accumulated summary — the universal lower half

These types are the load-bearing, domain-agnostic core; they are pure numerics over a stream and transfer verbatim to every consumer.

  • Histogram1D — a binned empirical measure of a conditioning variable $X$: linear (WARPing) bins over a fixed range (default $\pm 5\sigma$, $\gtrsim 10^3$ bins), each bin carrying its count and per-bin power-sums about the bin center $S_p = \sum_{i\in\text{bin}}(X_i - c_j)^p$ for $p = 1 \dots P$ (default $P = 4$; raise $P$ for the heavy-tailed channels — the turbulence Q-criterion needs $M_6$, whose sampling variance reaches the 12th moment). Because the offset $X_i - c_j$ is small within a bin ($\lesssim b/2 \approx \sigma/200$), the local power-sums are well-conditioned: they are accumulated by a plain atomic scatter-add — single-pass and numerically stable, with no compensated summation needed. The global central moments are recovered post hoc by the shift formula (the binscatter / WARPing local-moment construction; Scott 2015; Cattaneo et al. 2024), exactly (the power-sums use the actual $X_i$, not the center) and stably for the even high-order moments the consumers need.
  • Bins are exactly uniform — by design. The binning map is a single multiply-and-floor, i = floor((x - lower) * scale) + 1 with one scale = nbins / (upper - lower) — O(1), branch-free, and allocation-free, the GPU atomic-scatter hot path (§5). This requires equal-width bins; arbitrary edges would force a per-sample search (O(log nbins)) and break the kernel. So the constructor takes the explicit (lower, upper, nbins), which states the uniform contract, rather than a vector or range of edges (which would merely happen to be uniform — and floating-point rounding need not even guarantee that). Non-uniform binning is out of scope.
  • Histogram2D — a joint binned measure of $(X, Y)$, one per lag $\tau$, for conditional and transition densities; per-cell counts and bivariate power-sums $S_{rs}$ about the cell center.
  • HistogramND [OPEN] — the $n$-D generalization, deferred until a consumer needs conditioning on more than one variable (the PF rule against speculative structure).
  • per-bin power-sums are the one summary scheme above, in a univariate form ($S_p$ of $X$, for the marginal moments and density) and a bivariate form ($S_{rs} = \sum_{i\in\text{bin}}(X_i - c^x)^{r}(Y_i - c^y)^{s}$ about the cell center, the local sufficient statistic for local-polynomial regression; Cattaneo et al. 2024). All bandwidths, kernels, and moments are recovered from them post hoc.
  • the mergeable-monoid interface — merge! on every summary is elementwise addition (same binning ⇒ same bin centers ⇒ counts and power-sums add). Tested: accumulate-then-merge $=$ accumulate-together.
  • the backend-agnostic scatter-add primitive is observe! / observe_pairs! — one KernelAbstractions + Atomix kernel (one work-item per sample) that atomically scatters the count and the power-sums into the preallocated summary, the direct analog of PF's _bin_by_shell / anterpolate!. Allocation-free up to the kernel launch; verified identical on CPU and the AMD GPU under allowscalar(false) (§5). Storing power-sums about the bin center is precisely what keeps this a plain atomic add — no privatization, no workspace.

Numeric precision — accumulate wider than the data. Samples often arrive in Float32 (the GPU-first, low-memory consumers), but the summaries do not accumulate in Float32:

  • Counts are integers (UInt64 merged). Float32 represents integers exactly only up to $2^{24} = 16{,}777{,}216$ — beyond which adding one sample is silently lost — yet a single $256^3$ snapshot is exactly $2^{24}$ points, and a run accumulates $10^{10}$+ into the peak (modal) bins. Float32 counting is disqualified outright.
  • Power-sum accumulators default to Float64. The local sums are well-conditioned, but the global moment recovery (the shift formula combines them with large bin-center offsets) and the high-order channels still want full precision; Float32 would discard the ~7 digits the Float32 input carries, through the reduction over $n$. The power-sums accumulate in Float64 by default, regardless of input type.

Accumulator precision is a type parameter (PF-style element-type genericity) but its default is Float64, counts integer — accumulate-wider-than-the-data, the deliberate opposite of PF's preserve-the-field's-eltype (a reduction should out-precision its inputs; a transform should not). It costs almost nothing: the histogram is small (a few $\times10^3$ bins $\times$ a few Float64 power-sums — kilobytes per channel, versus a gigabyte field), and on the GPU the Float64 arithmetic is hidden behind the memory- and atomic-bound accumulation (§5), not FP64 throughput.

Two-time samples / lag handling — settled: the package bins pairs; the consumer produces them. For a two-time quantity like $m_\tau(u) = \langle Y_{t+\tau}\mid X_t = u\rangle$ the conditioning value $X(t)$ and the response $Y(t+\tau)$ must be paired per source — two separate marginal histograms cannot recover the joint (for the OU oracle both marginals are the same stationary Gaussian; the entire $\tau$-dependence lives in the pair). The one histogram entry is made at the response step, into the bin of $X(t)$. The package surface for this is just:

  • the memoryless observe_pairs!(stat, x_past, y_now; lag) — bins a batch of pre-formed, lag-labelled pairs into the per-lag summary (§4.2 / §4.3); and
  • the binning map bin_index(stat, x) — exposed so a caller can pre-quantize.

Everything that produces the pairs is the consumer's, being sample-source policy a domain-neutral package must not hold: the snapshot buffer of past $X(t)$ (held until the response arrives — the irreducible $O(M)$-per-lag state for $M$ sources, optionally stored as compact UInt16 bin indices via bin_index when memory is tight); which sources to sample; which lags to form (e.g. log-spaced); and the origin cadence / buffer depth (a memory-vs-variance budget). Pairing is by source identity — for a stably-ordered set just element-wise array alignment (no id tag), an explicit id only if the ordering can change (re-meshing, sorting, birth/death).

What the package keeps is the per-lag histograms — the accumulated power-sums, the small output ($L \times$ bins $\times$ moments, a few MB), never the $O(ML)$ snapshot buffer (the expensive object, consumer-side). For the nonstationary case the running origin $t$ and lag $\tau$ are explicit labels on the summary set ($p_{t,\tau}$), never assumed away. (If the ring-and-pair boilerplate proves worth sharing, it can be a non-domain-aware utility offered alongside the summary — never something the package's vocabulary depends on.)

4. Estimand families (estimators — the exportable upper half)

Each entry is written estimand first (per §2), demonstrating the convention.

4.1 Marginal density $f_X$

Estimand. $f_X = \mathrm{d}\mu_X/\mathrm{d}\lambda$, the Lebesgue density: $P(X \in A) = \int_A f_X\,\mathrm{d}\lambda$. Estimator (binned KDE / WARPing): from bin counts $n_j$ at centers $c_j$,

\[\hat f_h(x) \;=\; \frac{1}{n\,h}\sum_{j} n_j\, K\!\Big(\frac{x - c_j}{h}\Big), \qquad \int K = 1,\]

the binned approximation to $\frac{1}{nh}\sum_i K(\frac{x-X_i}{h})$. Bandwidth from §4.5.

Kernels. $K$ is pluggable (any $\int K = 1$); two ship — gaussian_kernel (default) and epanechnikov_kernel (the MSE-optimal $\tfrac34(1-u^2)$ on $|u| \le 1$). bandwidth (§4.5) is Gaussian-tuned, so a non-Gaussian kernel wants a matched $h$ (Epanechnikov's AMISE-optimal $h$ is $\approx 2.21\times$ the Gaussian's).

Tail-aware density [OPEN]. Fixed-$h$ KDE is poor in sparse tails. Two variants reuse this summary: an adaptive (variable-bandwidth) density (Abramson's $h_i \propto \hat f(x_i)^{-1/2}$, widening the kernel where data is thin) and a local-log-polynomial density (a local-linear fit to $\log f$) that captures the exponential / stretched-exponential decay a flat KDE smears — the intermittency regime. Two consumer caveats: the fixed range $[\texttt{lower}, \texttt{upper})$ drops events beyond it (set it wide, $\approx \pm 10\text{–}15\sigma$, to keep the tail), and uniform bins under-resolve power-law tails (bin a transform such as $\log|X|$ and change variables back; the $O(1)$ GPU scatter does not log-bin).

4.2 Conditional expectation $m(x)$ and variance $\sigma^2(x)$

Estimand. $m(x) = \mathbb{E}[Y\mid X=x]$ (the $L^2(\sigma(X))$ projection; §2 reference) and $\sigma^2(x) = \mathrm{Var}(Y\mid X=x) = \mathbb{E}[Y^2\mid X=x] - m(x)^2$. Estimator (local-linear; Fan & Gijbels), with $K_h(u) = h^{-1}K(u/h)$ the bandwidth-scaled kernel: from the per-bin power-sums form the kernel-weighted sums $T_\ell(x) = \sum_i K_h(X_i-x)(X_i-x)^\ell$ and $P_\ell(x) = \sum_i K_h(X_i-x)(X_i-x)^\ell Y_i$, then

\[\hat m_h(x) \;=\; \frac{T_2 P_0 - T_1 P_1}{T_0 T_2 - T_1^{2}}.\]

Nadaraya–Watson is the local-constant (degree-0) fit $\hat m_h = P_0/T_0$ (kept for reference, but local-linear is the default — it removes the design-density bias term $2 m'(x) f'(x)/f(x)$). Conditional variance by the same fit applied to $Y^2$ (Fan & Yao 1998). For heavy-tailed channels (e.g. $(\Delta X)^6$) a one-step robust local M-estimator (Fan & Jiang 2000) replaces the least-squares fit.

4.3 Conditional and transition density

Estimand. $f_{Y\mid X}(y\mid x) = f_{X,Y}(x,y)/f_X(x)$; the two-time transition density $p_\tau(y\mid x) = f_{X_{t+\tau}\mid X_t}(y\mid x)$ (nonstationary: $p_{t,\tau}$). Estimator (double-kernel; Rosenblatt 1969; Hyndman, Bashtannyk & Grunwald 1996): from the joint histogram,

\[\hat f(y\mid x) \;=\; \frac{\sum_i K_{h_1}(X_i-x)\,K_{h_2}(Y_i-y)}{\sum_i K_{h_1}(X_i-x)}.\]

4.4 Conditional increment moments and their functionals

Estimand. The conditional increment moment $M_n(x,\tau) = \mathbb{E}[(X_{t+\tau}-X_t)^n \mid X_t = x]$ — a pure statistical object (a conditional expectation of a power of an increment), estimated by §4.2 applied to the response $(\Delta X)^n$. Standard functionals (named, but model-neutral): the Kramers–Moyal coefficient $D^{(n)}(x) = \lim_{\tau\to 0}\frac{1}{n!\,\tau}M_n(x,\tau)$, with the drift $D^{(1)}$ and diffusion $D^{(2)}$; the finite-$\tau$ bias removed by the all-orders Bell-polynomial correction (Rydin Gorjão et al. 2021) that inverts the $\tau$-expansion rather than extrapolating $\tau\to 0$; the Pawula / Q diagnostic $Q(x) = M_6(x,\tau)/(5\,M_4(x,\tau)^2)$.

Model-neutrality is the invariant here. These functions are defined and estimated without assuming the data is a diffusion. That the conditional moments are the coefficients of a Fokker–Planck operator, that Pawula's theorem makes $D^{(n\ge 3)}$ vanish, that $D^{(1)}$ "is" drift — these are the consumer's interpretation, exactly as "energy" is the consumer's reading of PF's inner(u,u). The package supplies the estimators and the tests of the hypothesis (the Pawula ratio, a $\chi^2$ Markov / Chapman–Kolmogorov test); the consumer asserts the model.

4.5 Bandwidth selection (cross-cutting)

Estimand. the MSE-optimal bandwidth for the requested channel. Global rules (Silverman; Sheather–Jones 1991) for marginals; the load-bearing one for conditional moments is the response-aware local plug-in that answered the user's original question:

\[h^\star(x) \;=\; \left[\frac{R(K)\,\sigma^2(x)}{\mu_2(K)^2\,m''(x)^2\,f_X(x)\,n}\right]^{1/5}.\]

It depends on the response's conditional variance $\sigma^2(x)$ and regression curvature $m''(x)$, so each response channel ($\Delta X, \dots, (\Delta X)^6$, the acceleration $A$) gets its own $h^\star$ — a single shared bandwidth is provably suboptimal. This is why accumulation must be estimand-agnostic and rich (per-bin moments to high order): $\sigma^2(x)$, $m''(x)$, and $f_X(x)$ are all read back from the one stored summary, per channel, post hoc.

Fast smoothing (FFT). [performance] Because the data is binned on a regular grid, every smoother here — the binned KDE (§4.1) and the local-polynomial sums $T_\ell, P_\ell$ (§4.2) — is a convolution of bin arrays with the kernel. The FFT computes it in $O(B\log B)$ (Silverman 1982; Wand 1994; Fan & Marron 1994 for the binned local-poly form), and the decisive gain is amortization over bandwidth: transform the bin counts once, then each $h$ — and the bandwidth grid the local $h^\star(x)$ sweep needs — is a multiply by the kernel's transform ($e^{-h^2\omega^2/2}$ for the Gaussian) and an inverse FFT, no re-convolution. On the GPU the transforms are tiny ($B\sim10^3$) and batched across all channels / lags / $t$-windows in one plan. Lead with direct compact-kernel convolution (simple, exact); switch to batched FFT when the sweep is large; a recursive Gaussian (Deriche) is the $O(B)$-per-channel alternative for the fixed-bandwidth Gaussian. Priority: this smoothing is post-hoc on a small grid and is dwarfed — by $\sim10^3$ in total work — by the per-sample accumulation over $N^3\times$steps (§5), so the dominant performance lever is the accumulation hot loop, not the smoother. This is the FFT use the deferred ECF summary (§2) does not preclude.

4.6 Projection road [OPEN, alternative]

An alternative to bin-and-smooth: estimate drift/diffusion by projecting onto a function basis using running inner-product accumulators (the Gram matrix and projection vector are themselves a mergeable summary) — Stochastic Force Inference (Frishman & Ronceray 2020) and the underdamped ULI (Brückner et al. 2020), which fits on $(X, \dot X)$ data exactly as tracer sampling provides. Shares the lower-half monoid foundation. Include if a consumer wants parametric-in-basis drift fields; otherwise defer.

5. Backend-agnosticism and the hot-loop contract

Inherited wholesale from PF (its invariants 6 and 13):

  • Storage is generic AbstractArray; no scalar indexing of device arrays in package methods; allocate via similar.
  • Accumulation is allocation-free. observe!(summary, batch) allocates only a bounded amount (the kernel launch) on a preallocated summary — never an $O(n)$ or $O(\text{bins})$ temporary, which on a GPU hot loop would escape the host GC and wedge the run. Enforced by an @allocated audit on the CPU (where a materialized array's bytes are visible), the direct analog of PF's test/allocations.jl.
  • One portable kernel (KernelAbstractions + Atomix) does the atomic scatter-add binning, on CPU and any GPU backend; the vendor stacks (CUDA/AMDGPU) stay test-only dependencies.
  • Mergeable across devices — merge! is the multi-device / multi-chunk reduction (§2), here just elementwise addition. The monoid laws are the spec; tested for associativity and accumulate-then- merge equality.
  • Contention, not FP64, is the hot-loop enemy. The conditioning variable is peaked (Gaussian-ish), so the modal bins are atomic hot spots. The power-sums-about-the-bin-center choice (§3) makes each update a plain atomic add — integer-exact for counts, round-off for the Float64 power-sums — so global atomics handle even heavy contention directly (the proven PF anterpolate! pattern, verified on the AMD GPU). Privatization (per-block local histograms in fast memory, merged hierarchically via the monoid) stays available as a future optimization should a channel's modal-bin contention dominate, but is not needed for correctness. This per-sample accumulation over $N^3\times$steps is the package's true performance-critical path; the post-hoc smoothing (§4.5) is second-order.

Getting backend-portability and mergeability right from the first commit is the one piece of "design for reuse now" that pays — both are expensive to retrofit and are the reason the package survives contact with three different execution models.

6. Invariants — deliberate absences are statements

The PF discipline: a missing capability encodes a decision. The candidate invariants:

  1. Accumulation is estimand-agnostic; smoothing is post hoc. No summary stores a bandwidth, kernel, or channel choice. An API that bakes a bandwidth into accumulation is the banned analog of PF's spectral product-as-*. (This is the invariant that makes "no reruns" true.)
  2. Samples in, statistics out — no domain, no model. Domain-neutral vocabulary (sample / variate / response / lag / conditioning value), never particle / tracer / droplet / velocity. No stationarity, Markov, diffusion, or parametric assumption is a premise; such structure is only ever tested. (The analog of PF's "no time, no particles.")
  3. Everything is a mergeable monoid. Every summary supports merge!; the unnormalized summary is additive over the stream. No non-mergeable accumulator is admitted.
  4. The hot path is allocation-free and backend-agnostic (§5).
  5. The summary is rich enough to recover every target post hoc. A target that would need a finer or different summary is a recorded coverage gap, not silently approximated — the "no reruns" promise made falsifiable.
  6. Accumulate wider than the data. Counts are integer; moment accumulators default to Float64 even on Float32 input (§3). A Float32 count saturates ($2^{24}$); a Float32 high-order moment loses the data's own precision through the reduction. (The deliberate opposite of PF's preserve-the-eltype, for the opposite reason: this is a reduction, not a transform.)

Deliberate absences (consumer-side, by scope). Bulk scalar time-correlation functions $C(\tau) = \langle X(t)\,X(t+\tau)\rangle$ are not provided: a Histogram2D per lag would store the whole joint density to extract one cross-moment ($O(T\,n_x n_y)$ state for $O(T)$ scalars), and a scalar correlation is cheaply streamed by a dedicated lagged product-sum or FFT. The Histogram2D-per-lag path is for the conditional / two-time density objects — $p_\tau(y \mid x)$, $D^{(n)}(x)$, the van Hove $G(r,t)$ — that genuinely need the joint, over a typically sparse set of lags; the consumer owns the scalar-correlation fast path.

7. Documentation, correctness, and testing conventions

Mirrors PF; the deltas are noted.

  • Estimand-first docstrings (§2), self-contained pages, rendered LaTeX in standard notation, every code object bound to its math symbol on first mention.
  • A FORMALIZATION.md analog. PF records a typed statement per operation, expansion-checked against the code and test-linked. Here the typed entry states the estimand, the estimator, and the consistency claim — its bias and variance, e.g. $\mathrm{bias}\,\hat m_h(x) \approx \tfrac12 h^2 \mu_2(K)\,m''(x)$ and $\mathrm{Var}\,\hat m_h(x) \approx \frac{R(K)\,\sigma^2(x)}{n\,h\,f_X(x)}$ for local-linear regression — every normalization constant pinned, each linked to the test that verifies the convergence rate numerically.
  • Testing against analytic oracles — the statistics analog of PF's analytic round-trips: estimators are checked against distributions with closed forms (Gaussian marginals; an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process, whose drift, diffusion, and transition density $p_\tau(y\mid x)$ are known exactly — the natural end-to-end oracle for §4.3–4.4), plus consistency / rate tests (error falls at the theoretical $n^{-2/5}$ as $n$ grows), mergeability tests, the @allocated audit, and GPU-vs-CPU parity under allowscalar(false).
  • A check-docs battery — adapt PF's .claude/commands/check-docs.md: the same matrix of (Correct / Self-contained / Notation / Formatted / Current / Cited) × (every surface), with Correct extended to consistency and normalization of every estimator against its estimand.

Internal split, made explicit: Accumulators + Histograms are the universal lower half (every domain shares them verbatim, and they hold the hard, write-once numerics — GPU atomic high-order moment recursions); DensityEstimation through Bandwidth are the estimator upper half (turbulence-leaning but genuinely cross-domain — MD's van Hove function is ConditionalDensity with a different sampler). Keeping the halves separable lets a consumer that only wants marginal PDFs avoid the SDE machinery.

9. Naming the package

Settled: GBGStreamingStatistics (confirmed by the user). Operationally precise (online/streaming is the defining constraint), no overclaim, legible to all three consumer domains, parallel to PF's descriptive compound. The alternatives weighed, for the record:

  • GBGEmpiricalMeasures — the most PF-like choice, naming the mathematical object the package is about (as "PeriodicFields" names fields), and honest (the empirical measure and its summaries are the object). Risk: undersells the regression/conditional scope to a casual reader.
  • GBGSufficientStatistics — names the architectural principle. Risk: a mild category overclaim (there is no finite sufficient statistic in nonparametrics — §2's caveat), which PF's correctness policy would itself flag.

GBGOnlineStatistics is avoided to prevent confusion with the existing OnlineStats.jl (whose mergeable streaming-moment design is, however, worth mirroring — §10).

10. Open questions

  • Name — settled: GBGStreamingStatistics (§9).
  • Numeric precision — settled: integer counts, Float64 moment accumulators by default (type-parameterized), Float32 input promoted at accumulation (§3, invariant 6).
  • ECF summary — settled: deferred, likely never built (§2). FFT-smoothing of the histogram is kept regardless (§4.5) — a separate, post-hoc computation, not a stored representation.
  • Lag handling — settled: the package bins pre-formed pairs (observe_pairs!) and exposes the bin_index map; the snapshot buffer and all lag / sampling / cadence policy are consumer-side (§3).
  • [OPEN] Projection road — include SFI/ULI from the start or defer (§4.6)?
  • [OPEN] HistogramND — wait for a multi-conditioning consumer (§3)?
  • [OPEN] Prior art to mirror — study OnlineStats.jl for its mergeable streaming-moment design and merge semantics before writing Accumulators (it lacks the GPU-resident path and the conditional/SDE estimators, but its composition model is a good CPU-side template).
  • [OPEN] Vector-valued conditioning / responses — reuse PF's FiberVector/FiberTensor fiber algebra, or stay scalar-channel and let the consumer loop components? (Lean scalar-channel: the estimators are per-component, and a fiber dependency would re-import structure the package doesn't otherwise need.)
  • [OPEN] Weighted merge — ensembles and hyperstatistics. merge! is equal-weight addition (the monoid), which already makes ensemble pooling free: each run / device / time-chunk accumulates its own summary and they add (§2). The unnormalised summary is also scalable — multiply every count and power-sum by a nonnegative weight and it is the valid summary of a reweighted measure — so it lives in a cone (closed under nonnegative linear combinations), not merely an additive monoid. That extra freedom is exactly hyperstatistics (superstatistics; Beck & Cohen 2003): a parameter-weighted superposition $P(x) = \sum_k w_k f_k(x)$ equals $\sum_k (w_k / N_k)\, T_k$ over per-parameter summaries $T_k$. Planned as a weighted merge!(into, from; weight) over a Float-count summary variant (a weighted count $w\,n$ is not an integer); the conditional / transition estimators then carry the correct local mixing weight $w_k f_k(x)$ automatically, being ratios of linear functionals of the summary. Recorded, not built — and it fits the post-hoc rule: keep one summary per $\beta$ and choose $f(\beta)$ after the runs, re-weighting without re-accumulating, just as bandwidth is a post-hoc knob.

11. Decision log

Append-only; mirrors PF's decision log. The settled decisions of §9–§10 are recorded here as the first entries so later sessions extend rather than re-litigate them (the overlap with the body is intentional — do not dedupe it).

  • Package name — GBGStreamingStatistics (§9). Operationally precise; avoids OnlineStats.jl confusion. Alternatives GBGEmpiricalMeasures / GBGSufficientStatistics weighed and declined.

  • Numeric precision — integer counts, Float64 moment accumulators by default (§3, invariant 6), type-parameterized; Float32 input promoted at accumulation.

  • ECF summary — deferred, likely never built (§2). FFT-smoothing of the histogram is kept (§4.5) — a post-hoc computation, not a stored representation.

  • Lag handling — the package bins pre-formed pairs (observe_pairs!) and exposes bin_index; the snapshot buffer and all lag / sampling / cadence policy stay consumer-side (§3).

  • Summary storage — power-sums about the bin center, not Pébay-about-the-running-mean (§3). Changed during the GPU pass. The original design accumulated per-bin central moments by the Pébay (2008) running-mean recursion with Kahan compensation. A Pébay update is not atomic, so the GPU hot loop would have needed per-block privatization (private histograms merged hierarchically) — a pattern the sibling PF uses nowhere. Switching to power-sums $S_p = \sum (X_i - c_j)^p$ about the fixed bin center makes observe! a plain atomic scatter-add (PF's proven anterpolate! pattern): allocation-free, one portable kernel identical on CPU and the AMD GPU, merge! reduced to elementwise addition, and the marginal moments unified with the regression power-sums. The offset $X_i - c_j$ is tiny, so the local sums are well-conditioned (Kahan no longer needed); global central moments are recovered post hoc by the shift formula — exact, and stable for the even high-order moments ($M_4$, $M_6$) the Q-criterion needs (odd moments keep weaker relative precision, an accepted trade). Verified by the ROCm GPU parity suite.

  • Kramers–Moyal §4.4 — drift and diffusion first, as conditional increment moments (§4.4). The first slice implements the increment moments and the finite-$\tau$ Kramers–Moyal coefficients drift $D^{(1)} = M_1/\tau$ and diffusion $D^{(2)} = M_2/(2\tau)$ directly from the order-2 Histogram2D, via $M_1(x) = \hat m_h(x) - x$ and $M_2(x) = \widehat{\mathbb{E}}[Y^2\mid x] - 2x\,\hat m_h(x) + x^2$ — reusing the §4.2 local-linear primitives (no new accumulator), reported as the plain finite-$\tau$ ratio $M_n/(n!\,\tau)$. The all-orders Bell-polynomial finite-time correction (Rydin Gorjão et al. 2021) and the $\tau\to0$ limit stay forthcoming / consumer-side. $n \ge 3$ — the Pawula $M_4, M_6$ and pawula_quotient — is a recorded coverage gap (it needs the joint summary raised to order-$n$ per-cell $Y$ power-sums, deferred per invariant 5); markov_test waits on the §4.3 transition density. OU-oracle-tested: drift recovers the finite-$\tau$ value $x(e^{-\theta\tau}-1)/\tau$ exactly (no smoothing bias — the OU conditional mean is linear — $\to -\theta x$ as $\tau \to 0$), diffusion $\to \sigma^2/2$ carrying the local-linear bias of the curved $\mathbb{E}[Y^2\mid x]$.

  • Conditional / transition density §4.3 — binned double-kernel from the joint counts (§4.3). The Rosenblatt double-kernel estimator (§4.3) is evaluated from the Histogram2D cell counts (no power-sums needed): at the conditioning value $x$ the joint is reduced to an $x$-kernel-weighted Y-profile $P_k = \sum_j n_{jk} K_{h_1}(c^x_j - x)$ with $D = \sum_k P_k$, and then $\hat f(y\mid x) = \sum_k P_k K_{h_2}(c^y_k - y) / (\sqrt{2\pi}\,h_2 D)$. The $x$-kernel normalization cancels in the conditional ratio — only the $1/(\sqrt{2\pi}\,h_2)$ survives, giving $\int \hat f(y\mid x)\,\mathrm dy = 1$ exactly. transition_density is the same computation on a lag-$\tau$ joint (the two-time reading). The two bandwidths $h_1$ (hx, conditioning) and $h_2$ (hy, response) are supplied; the conditional MISE rate is the slower $O(n^{-2/3})$. OU-oracle-tested against the closed-form Gaussian $p_\tau(y\mid x)$.

Open decisions still flagged [OPEN] in §10 (the projection road, HistogramND, vector-valued conditioning, prior-art mirroring) graduate to entries here when they are settled.
