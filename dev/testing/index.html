Testing

How the tests are built and the per-operation coverage contract. The statistics analog of PF's analytic round-trips: estimators are checked against distributions with closed forms.

Layers

  • Analytic oracles. Estimators are verified against distributions whose target quantities are known exactly; the closed forms live once in test/testoracles.jl (the TestOracles module — gaussian_pdf, exponential_pdf, lognormal_pdf, gamma_pdf, beta_pdf, student_t_pdf, uniform_pdf, the Marron–Wand claw_pdf, the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck ou_stationary_std / ou_drift / ou_diffusion / ou_transition, and the trapz / ise helpers), included by every file that needs one. The marginal density density is checked across a representative analytic-PDF suite — Gaussian, uniform (bounded support), exponential, Student $\mathrm{t}_3$ (heavy tail), log-normal (skew), and a bimodal mixture (test/density_convergence.jl) — and across its full option grid (KDE / local-log degree, fixed / Abramson adaptive, the support boundary correction, the asymmetric gamma_kernel / beta_kernel, the parametric start, and normalize) in test/marginaldensity.jl. The conditional / transition estimators use Gaussian marginals ($f_X$, conditional moments) and an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process, whose transition density $p_\tau(y \mid x)$ is closed-form — the end-to-end oracle for the conditional / transition densities (§4.3).
  • Bandwidth selectors. Each bandwidth rule is checked against its analytic target (test/bandwidth.jl): :normal reproduces Silverman $(4/3)^{1/5}$ and the Epanechnikov $2.214\times$ ratio; :sj / :isj $\approx$ the normal reference on Gaussian data and tighten on a bimodal mixture; :aic finds a stable interior optimum; and the asymmetric-kernel Chen :plugin lowers the ISE on skewed (log-normal / gamma) data and stays neutral on the near-exponential boundary case. On the Marron–Wand claw (the field's standard hard-multimodal benchmark — a broad Gaussian with five sharp spikes) the data-driven :sj / :isj resolve the spikes the global-$\hat\sigma_X$ normal reference oversmooths into one bump ($\approx$ an order of magnitude lower ISE).
  • Structural invariants (property-based, oracle-free). Beyond the mergeability monoid laws, density satisfies affine equivariance exactly (test/marginaldensity.jl): for $Z = a_1 X + a_0$ with the range and bandwidth scaled alike, $\hat f_Z(a_1 x + a_0) = \hat f_X(x)/a_1$ to floating point — a guard against a stray normalization / $\sigma_X$-scaling factor a single-scale oracle would miss, and a guarantee of unit-consistency (the estimate cannot depend on micrometres vs metres).
  • Consistency / rate tests. The estimation error falls at its theoretical rate as $n$ grows. For the density this is implemented (test/density_convergence.jl): the ISE falls at $n^{-2q/(2q+1)}$ ($q$ the bias order) — $n^{-4/5}$ for degree 0/1, the steeper $n^{-8/9}$ for degree 2 (the higher-order $n^{-1/9}$ bandwidth), the Abramson variable bandwidth sharing the degree-0 order, and the Epanechnikov reproducing the (kernel-independent) $n^{-4/5}$. The rate is measured on the Gaussian, whose fast asymptotics make it visible by $n = 10^6$; a non-Gaussian does not reach the asymptotic regime there (its slope muddies to $\approx -0.66$ and degree 2 fails to separate from degree 0), a recorded omission rather than a missing test. A companion binning-error test confirms the binned estimate converges to the exact un-binned KDE as the grid refines — at $O(\Delta^2)$ in the bin width $\Delta$ for the default linear/centroid binning (mass at the recovered within-bin centroid, via the stored $S_1$) and at $O(\Delta)$ for binning = :simple (mass at the bin center), the two slopes checked against $2$ and $1$. The conditional-mean $n^{-2/5}$ and conditional-density $n^{-2/3}$ rates are likewise implemented (test/conditional_convergence.jl): a curved-mean ($m(x) = \sin x$) local-linear fit at $h \propto n^{-1/5}$ gives ISE $\propto n^{-4/5}$ (slope $\approx -0.80$), and the bivariate-Gaussian double-kernel at $h \propto n^{-1/6}$ gives the slower 2-D rate ISE $\propto n^{-2/3}$ (slope $\approx -2/3$, measured in $y$ at $x_0 = 0$, the $n$-range starting higher than the 1-D test since the 2-D asymptotic regime is reached later — below $\sim 10^5$ the measured slope runs shallow, the conditional analog of the density test's non-Gaussian muddying).
  • Mergeability tests. For every summary, accumulate-then-merge $=$ accumulate-together (to round-off), plus the incompatible-summary rejection — the monoid laws of invariant 3 (test/mergeability.jl for Histogram1D, test/histograms2d.jl for Histogram2D). The cone laws that extend the monoid (scale! homogeneity and distributivity, the weighted merge! as scale-then-merge, $\texttt{weight} = 1$ $=$ plain merge!, the Float-count guard) are tested alongside the hyperstatistics oracles in test/hyperstatistics.jl. Every post-hoc estimator is then re-checked to be a deterministic functional of the merged summary — uniformly, through the check_point_estimator battery (see The estimator contract) for the query-point estimators, and a dedicated check for the bandwidth selectors and the density start.
  • Allocation audit. test/allocations.jl (Histogram1D) and test/histograms2d.jl (Histogram2D) assert via @allocated that observe! / observe_pairs! / merge! allocate only a bounded amount on a preallocated summary — the KernelAbstractions launch, never an $O(n)$ or $O(\text{bins})$ temporary — the GPU-hot-loop contract of invariant 4.
  • GPU-vs-CPU parity. The gated suite (test/gpu/, run on a machine with a device) exercises observe! / merge! / observe_pairs! on device arrays under allowscalar(false) and compares against the CPU result — counts exactly, power-sums to round-off, including heavy atomic contention. Verified on the AMD RX 6700 XT (ROCm); the vendor stacks (CUDA / AMDGPU) stay test-only dependencies.
  • Package-quality checks. test/aqua.jl (Aqua.test_all: ambiguities, piracy, stale deps, compat support, undefined exports) and test/jet.jl (JET static analysis of every public estimator path — including the asymmetric-kernel, parametric-start, normalize, and bandwidth-selector paths, whose function-typed fields are the type-stability risk) run against the loaded package.

The estimator contract

Every estimator is held to the same uniform set of characteristics, in two tiers plus a few class-specific variations. The Definition of done in CLAUDE.md is the parent spec; this section is the operational form. The universal tier is enforced by construction — check_point_estimator (test/testcontracts.jl) runs it from one call per estimator, so a new estimator cannot silently omit a check (the failure mode that once left conditional_mean without a mergeability test).

Universal — every query-point estimator (a functional of the summary at a query point) satisfies:

  1. Analytic-oracle correctness — recovers a closed-form target (the oracle layer above).
  2. Scalar $=$ vector — the vectorized call equals the per-point scalar calls.
  3. Functional of the merged summary — the estimator of the merged summary equals that of the summary accumulated together — mergeability.
  4. Off-support sentinel — NaN where the estimand is undefined off the data's support (the conditional estimators); a marginal density is defined everywhere and is exempt.
  5. Argument-domain errors — a non-positive bandwidth, and every rejected option combination, throws. The guards are audited for completeness: each throw(ArgumentError(…)) — the bad degree / binning / method / rule symbol, the mutually-exclusive start / adaptive / support / asymmetric-kernel combinations, the $\texttt{degree} \ge 2$ and asymmetric-kernel standard-error gaps — carries a @test_throws.

Characteristics 2–4 are the check_point_estimator battery; 1 and 5 are per-estimator (the oracle, the @test_throws).

Not swept: the valid option space. Every option is tested against an oracle (vary one, hold the rest at default), and the genuinely interacting pairs are tested together (adaptive+support rewrite the same variance; degree×kernel) — but the full Cartesian product of valid option combinations is deliberately not exhausted. The options are largely orthogonal (different code paths), the exotic combinations have no analytic oracle (only a weak "finite" check), and exhaustive coverage is a combinatorial explosion with diminishing returns past the pairwise level. The systematic-and-exhaustive effort goes into the invalid combinations (finite, high-value — characteristic 5), not the valid ones.

Estimand-specific — layered on where the estimand demands it: normalization to a unit integral (the densities), the transition_density alias, and the convergence rate (the matrix).

Class variations. The summaries (Histogram1D / Histogram2D) are accumulators, not query-point estimators: their contract is the moment-recovery oracle, mergeability, the @allocated audit, and GPU parity. The marginal selector bandwidth(::Histogram1D) has no query point: oracle (per rule), mergeability, and argument errors — no scalar/vector or off-support. The local selector bandwidth(::Histogram2D, x) ($h^\star(x)$) is a query-point estimator, so it carries the universal contract (scalar $=$ vector, merged-summary functional, off-support NaN; test/localbandwidth.jl).

Standard errors carry their own contract, check_standard_error (test/testcontracts.jl), on top of the universal one: every *_standard_error is checked for positivity, scalar $=$ vector, and the $n^{-1/2}$ scaling — merging $J$ copies of a summary $T$ ($J\times$ the samples, the same shape) shrinks the standard error by exactly $\sqrt J$ ($\operatorname{SE}(J\cdot T) = \operatorname{SE}(T)/\sqrt J$), the deterministic form of $\operatorname{SE}\propto n^{-1/2}$ and a direct test of merged-histogram error behaviour. For the kernel standard errors (density, conditional_expectation) it also checks the $h^{-1/2}$ bandwidth scaling (check_se_bandwidth_scaling, asymptotic — on a flat density at small $h$, $\operatorname{SE}(2h) \approx \operatorname{SE}(h)/\sqrt{2}$, since changing $h$ also moves the estimate and the $-\hat f_X^2/n$ term carries no $1/h$). It is also run through check_point_estimator (so the error, like the estimate, is a deterministic functional of the merged summary, $\operatorname{SE}(\mathrm{merged}) = \operatorname{SE}(\mathrm{together})$), and carries a calibration check per family: the predicted standard error matches the empirical standard deviation of the estimate across independent datasets (Monte-Carlo — density and conditional_mean at the density peak, expectation of $\cos$). A *_with_standard_error additionally asserts its (value, standard_error) equals the separate estimate / standard-error calls exactly.

Coverage. Complementing calibration across the families, the band $\pm 1.96 \operatorname{SE}$ about the estimate is asked to bracket the oracle truth — not merely the estimate's own (possibly biased) mean, which is what calibration matches — across an interior grid, as an aggregate, lenient fraction. Because a standard error is a variance band and by construction cannot cover the estimator's $O(h^2)$ bias — the same order as the standard error at the mean-squared-error-optimal bandwidth — coverage is checked in the variance-dominated regime: at the working sample size for the one-dimensional readers (density, pushforward_density, conditional_mean, conditional_variance), and at a deliberately small $n$ for the two-dimensional densities (conditional_density, conditional_pushforward_density), whose bias otherwise dominates the tight large-$n$ standard error. The floors (an interior fraction $\ge 0.6$–$0.7$, or $\ge 0.8$ for the independent-dataset expectation) sit well below the observed $\approx 0.8$–$0.95$ and only guard against a broken standard-error scale, which would collapse coverage toward zero.

Merged histograms — two or more. Mergeability is checked at the query-point-functional level (check_point_estimator, with merged a 3-way merge of disjoint thirds for density — associativity beyond two) and at the summary level (test/mergeability.jl, test/histograms2d.jl — the monoid / cone laws), for both the estimates and their standard errors.

HDF5 persistence (test/hdf5.jl). The weak-dependency extension round-trips a summary through an HDF5 group: the reload is bit-exact for a Histogram1D, the count_type = Float64 cone variant (element type preserved), and a lagged Histogram2D (fields, element types, array shapes); estimators (density, mean) are identical after reload; the reload is still mergeable (merging into it matches merging into the original); the attributes are readable as plain HDF5 (self-describing); and the error paths throw (a dataset instead of a group, a missing name, a group with no histogram type tag).

The rate column is now uniform — density (test/density_convergence.jl), conditional_mean ($n^{-4/5}$ ISE), and conditional_density (the slower 2-D $n^{-2/3}$) each carry a log–log ISE-slope test (the conditional pair in test/conditional_convergence.jl); conditional_variance inherits conditional_mean's rate (a second-stage local fit), so it needs no separate test. The power-sum quadrature readouts (expectation, pushforward_density) carry a polynomial-exactness test (they equal the raw empirical mean $\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_i g(X_i)$ to $10^{-10}$ for $g$ of degree $\le \texttt{order}$) and a binning-error bound in place of an asymptotic slope. "via summary" means a host-side functional of the summary, so the device coverage is the summary's row.

Tolerances are seed-robust. The ISE-comparison thresholds (the :plugin gains, the parametric-start gains, the convergence-rate slopes) are not cherry-picked to a fixed seed: they are audited across many seeds, and the threshold sits above the worst case with margin. The audit is the test-suite analog of the Monte-Carlo–averaged MISE the source papers report — its goal is a non-fragile regression guard, not an MISE estimate. (It is why the :plugin-on-$\mathrm{Beta}(2,2)$ test asserts only competitiveness: the rule of thumb is already near-optimal on that smooth, symmetric case, so the gain there is real but marginal.)

Declared harness contracts

Three completeness contracts are declared to the shared tooling package, GBGCodingTools, so its engines run inside Pkg.test() and derive their required rows from the package's public surface (ownership over the modules test/public_surface.jl lists), never from a remembered list:

  • Allocation audit (test/allocations.jl). Every in-place operation — observe!, observe_pairs!, merge!, scale! — has an @alloc_below row, and a fifth one fails the suite until it is measured.
  • Oracle registry (test/oracle_registry.jl). Every public value-returning operation is classified by where its ground truth comes from — a closed form, a direct computation on the raw data, an invariant — citing the test that holds it; none may rest only on agreement with another operation of the package. Each paired *_with_standard_error reader is held to the closed form of its value and of its standard error, not only to the separate calls.
  • Method parity (test/method_parity.jl, test/method_parity_baseline.toml). The method count of every public operation and of every module is frozen; a moved number is refrozen in the commit that moves it, with the reason.

Layout

test/runtests.jl includes each module's tests in its own wrapper module so top-level helpers and constants do not collide; every file is also runnable on its own (julia --project=. test/<file>.jl). The per-module files are accumulators.jl, histograms.jl, histograms2d.jl, kernels.jl, localpolynomial.jl, marginaldensity.jl, density_convergence.jl, bandwidth.jl, localbandwidth.jl, conditionalexpectations.jl, conditionaldensity.jl, conditional_convergence.jl, expectations.jl; the cross-cutting files are oracles.jl (the Gaussian / OU end-to-end oracles), mergeability.jl, hyperstatistics.jl (the cone laws and the superstatistics / forgetting oracles), allocations.jl, method_parity.jl, oracle_registry.jl, public_surface.jl (the three declared contracts above), testoracles.jl (the shared TestOracles analytic library — the closed forms), and testcontracts.jl (the TestContracts universal-contract battery — check_point_estimator). aqua.jl and jet.jl are the package-quality checks; gpu/ is the gated device suite. The test environment is test/Project.toml (Aqua, GBGCodingTools, HDF5, JET, Random, SpecialFunctions, Statistics, TOML, Test, plus the LoweredCodeUtils compat pin JET needs).

Status

The summary suites (Histogram1D, Histogram2D), density (with the asymmetric kernels, the parametric start, and normalize), bandwidth (the normal-reference, Sheather–Jones :sj / :isj, :aic, the asymmetric Chen :plugin, and the joint-summary response-aware local :local $h^\star(x)$), the shared LocalPolynomial.local_fit jet primitive, the §4.2 regression (with the degree / kernel options and the Fan–Yao conditional_variance), the §4.3 conditional_density / transition_density are real — the Gaussian and analytic-PDF oracles, the bivariate-Gaussian, heteroscedastic curved-mean, quadratic-mean, and Ornstein–Uhlenbeck oracles, mergeability, the uniform check_point_estimator contract, density's exact affine equivariance, the Marron–Wand claw stress, the bounded @allocated audit, and CPU-vs-AMD-GPU parity (test/gpu/, ROCm); Aqua.test_all and JET run for real throughout. The Kramers–Moyal / Pawula / Markov diagnostics are archived (in git history at commit 37006b5), pending a consumer. The conditional-mean $n^{-2/5}$ and conditional-density $n^{-2/3}$ rate tests are now implemented (test/conditional_convergence.jl), alongside the density rate test (test/density_convergence.jl) and the direct kernel-constant oracle (test/kernels.jl) — closing the rate column of the matrix.
