Testing

How the tests are built and the per-operation coverage contract. The statistics analog of PF's analytic round-trips: estimators are checked against distributions with closed forms.

Layers

  • Analytic oracles. Estimators are verified against distributions whose target quantities are known exactly; the closed forms live once in test/testoracles.jl (the TestOracles module — gaussian_pdf, exponential_pdf, lognormal_pdf, gamma_pdf, beta_pdf, student_t_pdf, uniform_pdf, the Marron–Wand claw_pdf, the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck ou_stationary_std / ou_drift / ou_diffusion / ou_transition, and the trapz / ise helpers), included by every file that needs one. The marginal density density is checked across a representative analytic-PDF suite — Gaussian, uniform (bounded support), exponential, Student-$t$ $\nu = 3$ (heavy tail), log-normal (skew), and a bimodal mixture (test/density_convergence.jl) — and across its full knob grid (KDE / local-log degree, fixed / Abramson adaptive, the bounds boundary correction, the asymmetric gamma_kernel / beta_kernel, the parametric start, and normalize) in test/densityestimation.jl. The conditional / transition estimators use Gaussian marginals ($f_X$, conditional moments) and an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process, whose drift, diffusion, and transition density $p_\tau(y \mid x)$ are closed-form — the end-to-end oracle for the conditional / transition densities (§4.3) and the Kramers–Moyal functionals (§4.4).
  • Bandwidth selectors. Each bandwidth rule is checked against its analytic target (test/bandwidth.jl): :normal reproduces Silverman $(4/3)^{1/5}$ and the Epanechnikov $2.214\times$ ratio; :sj / :isj $\approx$ the normal reference on Gaussian data and tighten on a bimodal mixture; :aic finds a stable interior optimum; and the asymmetric-kernel Chen :plugin lowers the ISE on skewed (log-normal / gamma) data and stays neutral on the near-exponential boundary case. On the Marron–Wand claw (the field's standard hard-multimodal benchmark — a broad Gaussian with five sharp spikes) the data-driven :sj / :isj resolve the spikes the global-$\hat\sigma$ normal reference oversmooths into one bump ($\approx$ an order of magnitude lower ISE).
  • Structural invariants (property-based, oracle-free). Beyond the mergeability monoid laws, density satisfies affine equivariance exactly (test/densityestimation.jl): for $Y = aX + b$ with the range and bandwidth scaled alike, $\hat f_Y(ax+b) = \hat f_X(x)/a$ to floating point — a guard against a stray normalization / $\sigma$-scaling factor a single-scale oracle would miss, and a guarantee of unit-consistency (the estimate cannot depend on $\mu$m vs m).
  • Consistency / rate tests. The estimation error falls at its theoretical rate as $n$ grows. For the density this is implemented (test/density_convergence.jl): the ISE falls at $n^{-2p/(2p+1)}$ — $n^{-4/5}$ for degree 0/1, the steeper $n^{-8/9}$ for degree 2 (the higher-order $n^{-1/9}$ bandwidth), the Abramson variable bandwidth sharing the degree-0 order, and the Epanechnikov reproducing the (kernel-independent) $n^{-4/5}$. The rate is measured on the Gaussian, whose fast asymptotics make it visible by $n = 10^6$; a non-Gaussian does not reach the asymptotic regime there (its slope muddies to $\approx -0.66$ and degree 2 fails to separate from degree 0), a recorded omission rather than a missing test. A companion binning-error test confirms the binned estimate converges to the exact un-binned KDE as the grid refines, at $O(\Delta)$ in the bin width $\Delta$ (simple binning; linear binning would be $O(\Delta^2)$, a refinement latent in the stored $S_1$). The conditional-mean $n^{-2/5}$ and conditional-density $n^{-2/3}$ rate tests are scheduled.
  • Mergeability tests. For every summary, accumulate-then-merge $=$ accumulate-together (to round-off), plus the incompatible-summary rejection — the monoid laws of invariant 3 (test/mergeability.jl for Histogram1D, test/histograms2d.jl for Histogram2D). Every post-hoc estimator is then re-checked to be a deterministic functional of the merged summary — uniformly, through the check_point_estimator battery (see The estimator contract) for the query-point estimators, and a dedicated check for the bandwidth selectors and the density start.
  • Allocation audit. test/allocations.jl (Histogram1D) and test/histograms2d.jl (Histogram2D) assert via @allocated that observe! / observe_pairs! / merge! allocate only a bounded amount on a preallocated summary — the KernelAbstractions launch, never an $O(n)$ or $O(\text{bins})$ temporary — the GPU-hot-loop contract of invariant 4.
  • GPU-vs-CPU parity. The gated suite (test/gpu/, run on a machine with a device) exercises observe! / merge! / observe_pairs! on device arrays under allowscalar(false) and compares against the CPU result — counts exactly, power-sums to round-off, including heavy atomic contention. Verified on the AMD RX 6700 XT (ROCm); the vendor stacks (CUDA / AMDGPU) stay test-only dependencies.
  • Package-quality checks. test/aqua.jl (Aqua.test_all: ambiguities, piracy, stale deps, compat bounds, undefined exports) and test/jet.jl (JET static analysis of every public operation — including the asymmetric-kernel, parametric-start, normalize, and bandwidth-selector paths, whose function-typed fields are the type-stability risk) run against the loaded package.

The estimator contract

Every estimator is held to the same uniform set of characteristics, in two tiers plus a few class-specific variations. The Definition of done in CLAUDE.md is the parent spec; this section is the operational form. The universal tier is enforced by construction — check_point_estimator (test/testcontracts.jl) runs it from one call per estimator, so a new estimator cannot silently omit a check (the failure mode that once left conditional_mean without a mergeability test).

Universal — every query-point estimator (a functional of the summary at a query point) satisfies:

  1. Analytic-oracle correctness — recovers a closed-form target (the oracle layer above).
  2. Scalar $=$ vector — the vectorized call equals the per-point scalar calls.
  3. Functional of the merged summary — f(merge) $=$ f(accumulate-together) — mergeability.
  4. Off-support sentinel — NaN where the estimand is undefined off the data's support (the conditional estimators); a marginal density is defined everywhere and is exempt.
  5. Argument-domain errors — a non-positive bandwidth, and any rejected knob combination, throws.

Characteristics 2–4 are the check_point_estimator battery; 1 and 5 are per-estimator (the oracle, the @test_throws).

Estimand-specific — layered on where the estimand demands it: normalization $\int\hat f = 1$ (the densities), the Kramers–Moyal identities ($M_1 = \mathbb{E}[Y \mid x] - x$, the variance decomposition, the $1/(n!\tau)$ scaling — drift / diffusion / increment_moment), the transition_density alias, and the convergence rate (the matrix).

Class variations. The summaries (Histogram1D / Histogram2D) are accumulators, not query-point estimators: their contract is the moment-recovery oracle, mergeability, the @allocated audit, and GPU parity. The selector bandwidth has no query point: oracle (per rule), mergeability, and argument errors — no scalar/vector or off-support.

n/a is not applicable; scheduled is a recorded gap with a planned test (the roadmap in CLAUDE.md). The rate is the one characteristic not yet uniform — density has it, the conditional estimators have it scheduled, and drift / diffusion inherit conditional_mean's rate (they are linear functionals of it). "via summary" means a host-side functional of the summary, so the device coverage is the summary's row.

Tolerances are seed-robust. The ISE-comparison thresholds (the :plugin gains, the parametric-start gains, the convergence-rate slopes) are not cherry-picked to a fixed seed: they are audited across many seeds, and the threshold sits above the worst case with margin. The audit is the test-suite analog of the Monte-Carlo–averaged MISE the source papers report — its goal is a non-fragile regression guard, not an MISE estimate. (It is why the :plugin-on-Beta(2,2) test asserts only competitiveness: the rule of thumb is already near-optimal on that smooth, symmetric case, so the gain there is real but marginal.)

Layout

test/runtests.jl includes each module's tests in its own wrapper module so top-level helpers and constants do not collide; every file is also runnable on its own (julia --project=. test/<file>.jl). The per-module files are accumulators.jl, histograms.jl, histograms2d.jl, densityestimation.jl, density_convergence.jl, bandwidth.jl, regression.jl, conditionaldensity.jl, markovinference.jl; the cross-cutting files are oracles.jl (the Gaussian / OU end-to-end oracles), mergeability.jl, allocations.jl, testoracles.jl (the shared TestOracles analytic library — the closed forms), and testcontracts.jl (the TestContracts universal-contract battery — check_point_estimator). aqua.jl and jet.jl are the package-quality checks; gpu/ is the gated device suite. The test environment is test/Project.toml (Aqua, JET, Random, SpecialFunctions, Statistics, Test, plus the LoweredCodeUtils compat pin JET needs).

Status

The summary suites (Histogram1D, Histogram2D), density (with the asymmetric kernels, the parametric start, and normalize), bandwidth (the normal-reference, Sheather–Jones :sj / :isj, :aic, and the asymmetric Chen :plugin), the §4.2 regression, the §4.3 conditional_density / transition_density, and the §4.4 drift / diffusion / increment_moment are real — the Gaussian and analytic-PDF oracles, the bivariate-Gaussian and Ornstein–Uhlenbeck oracles, mergeability, the uniform check_point_estimator contract, density's exact affine equivariance, the Marron–Wand claw stress, the bounded @allocated audit, and CPU-vs-AMD-GPU parity (test/gpu/, ROCm); Aqua.test_all and JET run for real throughout. The response-aware local bandwidth and the Pawula / Markov diagnostics remain placeholders until those land; the conditional-mean $n^{-2/5}$ and conditional-density $n^{-2/3}$ rate tests are scheduled (CLAUDE.md roadmap; the density rate test is implemented — test/density_convergence.jl).
