GBGStreamingStatistics.jl

GBGStreamingStatistics.jl is a backend-agnostic library for online nonparametric statistics. It estimates marginal densities, conditional expectations, and conditional and two-time transition densities, from a stream of samples that is never stored — each sample is observed once, folded into a fixed-size summary, and discarded.

The summary (a fine histogram with per-bin power-sums about the bin center) is accumulated online and is estimand-agnostic: every reported quantity is a post-hoc smoothing functional of that one summary, so the samples themselves are never stored and never re-read. It is a mergeable monoid — in fact a cone (combine across threads, devices, or time-chunks by addition, or weight and superpose for hyperstatistics — a post-hoc mixture over summaries) — and targets both CPU and GPU array backends on a single shared-memory node. The library is domain-neutral and model-neutral — it assumes no statistical model (no stationarity, Markov, diffusion, or parametric premise): it consumes a stream of values (optionally lagged pairs) and returns estimators.

When it fits. It is built for settings where the data is a stream and storing or re-reading it is the bottleneck:

  • inside a time-marching solver — fold each step's samples into the summary and discard, with no trajectory storage and no second pass;
  • one pass, many views — every bandwidth, estimand, and conditioning value is computed post hoc from the one summary, so exploring them never re-reads the raw stream;
  • parallel and chunked — mergeable summaries combine across threads, GPUs, and time windows;
  • memory-tight or GPU-resident — the summary is fixed-size and the accumulation hot path is allocation-free and runs on the GPU.

It is used, for example, in turbulence (conditional and Lagrangian statistics, transition densities) and molecular dynamics (the van Hove and intermediate scattering functions are two-time conditional densities) — but nothing in the library is specific to a domain.

Status

The summaries Histogram1D and Histogram2D (observe! / observe_pairs! / merge! / scale!, with a floating-point-count variant that scales post hoc for hyperstatistics or the exponential forgetting of older samples), the marginal density, global bandwidth selection, the conditional mean and variance (conditional_mean, conditional_variance), and the conditional and transition densities (conditional_density, transition_density) are implemented and tested on CPU and GPU (analytic oracles, mergeability, allocation, GPU results matching the CPU's), including the asymmetric kernels for bounded or positive data, the local bandwidth $h^\star(x)$ fitted to the response at each query point, and the Fan–Yao conditional variance — plus the estimators read by quadrature over the stored power-sums (expectation, its expectation_standard_error, and pushforward_density). Drift and diffusion (the first two Kramers–Moyal coefficients) were built and tested, then archived, restorable when a consumer (a package built on this one) needs them; the higher coefficients and the Pawula / Markov diagnostics are forthcoming. See the Architecture for the design and roadmap.

Installation

This is an unregistered package, so it is not listed in the General Julia registry. To install it, run:

]add https://github.com/GrigorySarnitsky/GBGStreamingStatistics.jl

or

using Pkg

Pkg.add(url="https://github.com/GrigorySarnitsky/GBGStreamingStatistics.jl")

Then you can use it as usual:

using GBGStreamingStatistics

Main components

The package splits into a universal lower half (the summaries and how they accumulate, shared verbatim by every consumer) and an estimator upper half (the estimands):

  • Accumulators: the foundation — AbstractAccumulator, the interface of every mergeable online summary of the empirical measure $\mathbb{P}_n$, which gives a set $A$ the share $\mathbb{P}_n(A) = \#\{i : X_i \in A\}/n$ of the samples lying in it (you observe! samples into it and merge! two together).
  • Histograms: the summaries — Histogram1D and Histogram2D, both <: AbstractAccumulator. Each is a finely binned empirical measure holding per-bin counts and power-sums about the bin center; accumulation runs on CPU or GPU. It also offers observe_pairs! for two-time statistics and, when HDF5 is loaded (a package extension), write_histogram / read_histogram, persisting a summary to an HDF5 group.
  • Kernels: the shared smoothing kernels, which density, the regression fits, the conditional density and the bandwidth selectors use. They are symmetric (gaussian_kernel, epanechnikov_kernel, or any callable kernel with $\int K = 1$) or asymmetric for bounded/positive support (gamma_kernel, beta_kernel, or a custom AsymmetricKernel).
  • Expectations: the power-sum quadrature readouts (expectations computed by contracting the stored per-bin power-sums with a polynomial fit of the functional) — the expectation of a smooth functional expectation ($\mathbb{E}[g(X)]$), its sampling expectation_standard_error (paired by expectation_with_standard_error), and the pushforward density pushforward_density ($f_{g(X)}$) of a transformed variable. The moments (mean / var / std / central_moment) are the monomial case $g = x^p$, one per order.
  • MarginalDensity: the marginal density density ($f_X$) offers, in one call, the binned KDE, the local-log-polynomial estimator (degree), the Abramson variable bandwidth (adaptive, for tails), the asymmetric gamma_kernel / beta_kernel for bounded/positive support, the parametric start (a moment-fitted model corrected by a KDE; Hjort & Glad 1995), and exact normalize — plus density_standard_error, the pointwise variability band, and density_with_standard_error, the estimate and its band together.
  • ConditionalExpectations: the conditional expectation conditional_expectation ($\mathbb{E}[g(Y)\mid X = x]$) of a smooth response functional. The conditional mean conditional_mean ($m(x)$) and variance conditional_variance ($\sigma^2(x)$) sit beside it — the mean as its $g = \mathrm{identity}$ case, the variance's plug-in method built from its $y^2$ case. It also offers conditional_expectation_standard_error (paired by conditional_expectation_with_standard_error) and the conditional pushforward density conditional_pushforward_density ($f_{g(Y)\mid X}$), each with its standard error. All are local-polynomial regressions in $x$ of a $y$-quadrature response (per-bin expectations from the stored response power-sums).
  • ConditionalDensity: the conditional density conditional_density ($f_{Y\mid X}$) and two-time transition density transition_density ($p_\tau(y\mid x)$, the density of the value a lag later) are double-kernel estimators with standard errors and paired _with_standard_error functions.
  • Bandwidth: bandwidth applies the default kernel- and degree-aware normal-reference rule (Silverman's is the Gaussian, degree-0 case), a data-driven plug-in (e.g. Sheather–Jones, rule = :sj), or, for paired samples, the local plug-in $h^\star(x)$ (rule = :local).
