GBGStreamingStatistics.jl

GBGStreamingStatistics.jl is a backend-agnostic library for online nonparametric statistics. It estimates marginal densities, conditional expectations, and conditional and two-time transition densities, from a stream of samples that is never stored — each sample is observed once, folded into a fixed-size summary, and discarded.

The summary (a fine histogram with per-bin power-sums about the bin center) is accumulated online and is estimand-agnostic: every reported quantity is a post-hoc smoothing functional of that one summary, so there is no storage of the underlying state and no recompute-from-raw reruns. It is a mergeable monoid — in fact a cone (combine across threads, devices, or time-chunks by addition, or weight and superpose for hyperstatistics) — and targets both CPU and GPU array backends on a single shared-memory node. The library is domain-neutral and model-neutral — it assumes no statistical model (no stationarity, Markov, diffusion, or parametric premise): it consumes a stream of values (optionally lagged pairs) and returns estimators.

When it fits. It is built for settings where the data is a stream and storing or re-reading it is the bottleneck:

  • inside a time-marching solver — fold each step's samples into the summary and discard, with no trajectory storage and no second pass;
  • one pass, many views — every bandwidth, channel, and conditioning value is computed post hoc from the one summary, so exploring them never re-reads the raw stream;
  • parallel and chunked — mergeable summaries combine across threads, GPUs, and time windows;
  • memory-tight or GPU-resident — the summary is fixed-size and the accumulation hot path is allocation-free and runs on the GPU.

It is used, for example, in turbulence (conditional and Lagrangian statistics, transition densities, drift and diffusion) and molecular dynamics (the van Hove and intermediate scattering functions are two-time conditional densities) — but nothing in the library is specific to a domain.

Status

The summaries Histogram1D and Histogram2D (power-sums about the bin center, observe! / observe_pairs! / merge! / scale!, with a Float-count cone variant for hyperstatistics / forgetting), the marginal density, global bandwidth selection, and the conditional mean and variance (conditional_mean, conditional_variance), the conditional and transition densities (conditional_density, transition_density) are implemented and tested on CPU and GPU (Gaussian, bivariate-Gaussian, and Ornstein–Uhlenbeck oracles, mergeability, allocation, AMD-ROCm parity), including the asymmetric bounded/positive-support kernels, the response-aware local bandwidth $h^\star(x)$, and the Fan–Yao conditional variance — plus the power-sum quadrature readouts (expectation of a smooth functional, its expectation_standard_error, and the pushforward_density of a transformed variable). The Kramers–Moyal / SDE-reading functionals (drift and diffusion), the higher Kramers–Moyal coefficients, and the Pawula / Markov diagnostics are archived — built and Ornstein–Uhlenbeck-tested, then moved out to keep the core focused, and restorable when a consumer needs them; see the Architecture for the design and roadmap.

Installation

This is an unregistered package, so it is not listed in the General Julia registry. To install it, run:

]add https://github.com/GrigorySarnitsky/GBGStreamingStatistics.jl

or

using Pkg

Pkg.add(url="https://github.com/GrigorySarnitsky/GBGStreamingStatistics.jl")

Then you can use it as usual:

using GBGStreamingStatistics

Main components

The package splits into a universal lower half (the summaries and how they accumulate, shared verbatim by every consumer) and an estimator upper half (the estimands):

  • Accumulators: the foundation — AbstractAccumulator, the interface of every mergeable online summary of the empirical measure $\hat\mu_n = \frac{1}{n}\sum_i \delta_{X_i}$ (you observe! samples into it and merge! two together). The concrete summaries live in Histograms.
  • Histograms: the summaries — Histogram1D and Histogram2D, both <: AbstractAccumulator. Each is a finely binned empirical measure — the fine-grained measure that post-hoc smoothing later coarse-grains — holding per-bin counts and power-sums about the bin center; accumulation runs on CPU or GPU, and summaries combine with merge!. With observe_pairs! for two-time statistics, and — when HDF5 is loaded (a package extension) — write_histogram / read_histogram to persist a summary to an HDF5 group.
  • Kernels: the shared smoothing kernels — symmetric (gaussian_kernel, epanechnikov_kernel, or any callable with $\int K = 1$) and asymmetric bounded/positive-support kernels (gamma_kernel, beta_kernel, via the AsymmetricKernel constructor) — used by density, the regression fits, the double-kernel conditional density, and the bandwidth rules.
  • Expectations: the power-sum quadrature readouts — the expectation of a smooth functional expectation ($\mathbb{E}[g(X)]$), its sampling expectation_standard_error (paired with the estimate by expectation_with_standard_error), and the pushforward density pushforward_density ($f_{g(X)}$) of a transformed variable — each with its standard error. Read off the stored power-sums; the recovered moments (mean / var / std / central_moment) are the $g = x^p$ case.
  • MarginalDensity: the marginal density density ($f_X$) — one call over the binned KDE, the local-log-polynomial (degree), and the Abramson variable bandwidth (adaptive, for tails), the asymmetric gamma_kernel / beta_kernel for bounded/positive support, the parametric start (a moment-fitted model corrected by a KDE; Hjort & Glad 1995), and exact normalize — plus density_standard_error, the pointwise variability band (tracking those same options), and density_with_standard_error, the estimate and its band from one option specification.
  • ConditionalExpectations: the conditional expectation conditional_expectation ($\mathbb{E}[g(Y)\mid x]$) of a response functional, with the conditional mean conditional_mean ($m(x)$) and variance conditional_variance ($\sigma^2(x)$, with its residual-based standard error) as its $g = \mathrm{identity}$ / $y^2$ cases, the sampling conditional_expectation_standard_error (paired with the estimate by conditional_expectation_with_standard_error), and the conditional pushforward density conditional_pushforward_density ($f_{g(Y)\mid X}$) (local-polynomial regression in $x$ of a $y$-quadrature response) — each with its standard error.
  • ConditionalDensity: the conditional density conditional_density ($f_{Y\mid X}$) and two-time transition density transition_density ($p_\tau(y\mid x)$; double-kernel), each with its pointwise standard error and paired reader.
  • Bandwidth: bandwidth selection — the default kernel- and degree-aware normal-reference rule (Silverman the Gaussian/degree-0 case), plus data-driven plug-ins (e.g. Sheather–Jones, rule = :sj) and the joint-summary response-aware local plug-in $h^\star(x)$ (rule = :local), among others.
