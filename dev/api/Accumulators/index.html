Accumulators

The accumulation interface: the AbstractAccumulator supertype and the operations every summary supports — observe! (fold a batch of samples into the summary, on CPU or GPU), merge! (combine two summaries by addition, or — weighted — the affine combination), and scale! (reweight a summary by a nonnegative scalar). With merge! and scale! the summaries form a convex cone, the algebra behind hyperstatistics and exponential forgetting (§10). The concrete summary types live in the Histograms module.

The accumulator type

GBGStreamingStatistics.Accumulators.AbstractAccumulator — Type
AbstractAccumulator

Supertype of every mergeable summary $T(\hat\mu_n)$ of the empirical measure $\hat\mu_n = \frac1n \sum_{i=1}^{n} \delta_{X_i}$ of an observed stream $X_1, X_2, \dots$ ($\delta_x$ the unit point mass at $x$, $n$ the number of samples seen).

A summary is finite-dimensional — the $n$ point masses are never stored — mergeable (an additive monoid over the stream; see merge!), and rich enough that every reported estimator is recoverable from it post hoc, with no second pass over the data. The summary is accumulated online and is estimand-agnostic; all smoothing (kernel, bandwidth) is applied post hoc. Concrete summaries (Histogram1D, Histogram2D) subtype this.

Operations

observe! and the plain merge! are the additive monoid; the weighted merge!(into, from; weight) and scale! extend it to the cone (the hyperstatistical parameter-mixture density and exponential forgetting).

GBGStreamingStatistics.Accumulators.observe! — Function
observe!(summary::AbstractAccumulator, batch) -> summary

Fold a batch of samples into summary in place, advancing the accumulated summary $T(\hat\mu_n)$ of the empirical measure (see AbstractAccumulator). Accumulation is estimand-agnostic: it knows nothing of which estimator will later be asked for, nor of the bandwidth or kernel — the architectural invariant that delivers no reruns.

The binning is an atomic scatter-add: one KernelAbstractions + Atomix kernel — one work-item per sample — accumulates, per bin, the count and the power-sums $S_p = \sum_{i\in\text{bin}}(X_i - c_j)^p$ about the bin center $c_j$, directly into the preallocated summary (no scratch). It runs identically on a CPU Array and any GPU backend, and is allocation-free up to the kernel launch. Because $X_i - c_j$ is small within a bin, the local power-sums are well-conditioned (no compensated summation needed); global central moments are recovered post hoc by the shift formula. Accumulated in the summary's accumulator type (Float64 by default), never narrowed to the input element type — accumulate wider than the data.

References:

  • D. W. Scott (2015), Multivariate Density Estimation, 2nd ed.
  • M. D. Cattaneo et al. (2024), On binscatter (the local-moment / WARPing construction).

Base.merge! — Function
merge!(into::AbstractAccumulator, from::AbstractAccumulator; weight = 1) -> into

Combine two summaries into into, forming the summary of the concatenated stream — the monoid operation that makes accumulation additive. For disjoint sample blocks $A$ and $B$, $T(A \uplus B) = T(A) \oplus T(B)$: accumulation $T$ is a monoid homomorphism from sample blocks (under disjoint union $\uplus$) to summaries (under merge!), on the unnormalized counts and power-sums — the normalized $\hat\mu$ would reweight, not add. Because compatible summaries share the same binning (hence the same bin centers), their counts and power-sums simply add elementwise, so merge! is exact and trivially parallel — this is what makes multi-device reduction and time-chunk stitching free (combine per-device or per-time-chunk summaries by addition).

Weighted form — the cone. merge!(into, from; weight) forms $\texttt{into} \mathrel{+}= \texttt{weight} \cdot \texttt{from}$; the default $\texttt{weight} = 1$ is the plain monoid addition above. Because the unnormalized summary is also scalable (scale!), the summaries form a convex cone (closed under nonnegative linear combinations), and the weighted merge realizes the hyperstatistical parameter-mixture density (superstatistics; Beck & Cohen 2003)

\[P(x) = \int f(\beta)\,P(x \mid \beta)\,\mathrm{d}\beta \;\approx\; \sum_k w_k\,f_k(x), \qquad w_k = f(\beta_k)\,\Delta\beta,\]

a superposition over a slowly varying parameter $\beta$ with mixing law $f(\beta)$: keep one summary $T_k$ per value $\beta_k$ (accumulated independently), then after the runs choose $f(\beta)$ and assemble $\sum_k (w_k / N_k)\,T_k$ by weighted merge! ($N_k = \texttt{sum(T\_k.counts)}$ normalizes each component to a probability measure; the conditional / transition estimators then carry the local mixing weight automatically, being ratios of linear functionals). This is post hoc — re-weight without re-accumulating, exactly as bandwidth is a post-hoc knob. A weighted merge ($\texttt{weight} \ne 1$) requires a Float-count into (count = Float64).

merge! is intrinsically a finite-sample operation, so it has no population estimand of its own; its specification is the monoid (and, weighted, the cone) law, tested as accumulate-then-merge = accumulate-together. Extends Base.merge!.

GBGStreamingStatistics.Accumulators.scale! — Function
scale!(summary::AbstractAccumulator, weight) -> summary

Reweight summary in place by a nonnegative weight — multiply every count and power-sum by weight, giving the valid summary of the reweighted measure $\texttt{weight} \cdot \hat\mu$. With merge! this makes the unnormalized summaries a convex cone (closed under nonnegative linear combinations), not merely an additive monoid: scale! is the scalar action, merge! the addition.

Two uses. Exponential forgetting of a non-stationary stream — scale!(summary, λ) with $0 < \lambda < 1$ before folding in the next block down-weights the past geometrically (an effective window $1/(1-\lambda)$). Hyperstatistics — the scalar building block of the parameter-mixture density assembled by weighted merge! (the estimand and recipe are documented there; Beck & Cohen 2003).

Requires a Float-count summary (count = Float64; the default UInt64 counts cannot hold a fractional $\texttt{weight} \cdot n$) — an ArgumentError otherwise. $\texttt{weight} = 1$ is the identity. Like merge!, an intrinsically finite-sample operation with no population estimand; specified by the cone laws (scale!(scale!(s, a), b) equals scaling by $ab$; scale! distributes over merge!) and tested as such.
