Accumulators

The accumulation interface: the AbstractAccumulator supertype and the two operations every summary supports — observe! (fold a batch of samples into the summary, on CPU or GPU) and merge! (combine two summaries by addition). The concrete summary types live in the Histograms module.

GBGStreamingStatistics.Accumulators.AbstractAccumulator — Type
AbstractAccumulator

Supertype of every mergeable summary $T(\hat\mu_n)$ of the empirical measure $\hat\mu_n = \frac1n \sum_{i=1}^{n} \delta_{X_i}$ of an observed stream $X_1, X_2, \dots$ ($\delta_x$ the unit point mass at $x$, $n$ the number of samples seen).

A summary is finite-dimensional — the $n$ point masses are never stored — mergeable (an additive monoid over the stream; see merge!), and rich enough that every reported estimator is recoverable from it post hoc, with no second pass over the data. The summary is accumulated online and is response-agnostic; all smoothing (kernel, bandwidth, channel) is applied post hoc. Concrete summaries (Histogram1D, Histogram2D) subtype this.

Base.merge! — Method
merge!(into::AbstractAccumulator, from::AbstractAccumulator) -> into

Combine two summaries into into, forming the summary of the concatenated stream — the monoid operation that makes accumulation additive: $T(\hat\mu_{A \cup B}) = T(\hat\mu_A) \oplus T(\hat\mu_B)$. Because compatible summaries share the same binning (hence the same bin centers), their counts and power-sums simply add elementwise, so merge! is exact and trivially parallel — this is what makes multi-device reduction and time-chunk stitching free (combine per-device or per-time-chunk summaries by addition).

merge! is intrinsically a finite-sample operation, so it has no population estimand; its specification is the monoid law, tested as accumulate-then-merge = accumulate-together. Extends Base.merge!.

GBGStreamingStatistics.Accumulators.observe! — Function
observe!(summary::AbstractAccumulator, batch) -> summary

Fold a batch of samples into summary in place, advancing the accumulated summary $T(\hat\mu_n)$ of the empirical measure (see AbstractAccumulator). Accumulation is response-agnostic: it knows nothing of bandwidth, kernel, or which estimator will later be asked for — the architectural invariant that delivers no reruns.

The binning is an atomic scatter-add: one KernelAbstractions + Atomix kernel — one work-item per sample — accumulates, per bin, the count and the power-sums $S_p = \sum_{i\in\text{bin}}(X_i - c_j)^p$ about the bin center $c_j$, directly into the preallocated summary (no scratch). It runs identically on a CPU Array and any GPU backend, and is allocation-free up to the kernel launch. Because $X_i - c_j$ is small within a bin, the local power-sums are well-conditioned (no compensated summation needed); global central moments are recovered post hoc by the shift formula (docs/ARCHITECTURE.md §3). Accumulated in the summary's accumulator type (Float64 by default), never narrowed to the input element type — accumulate wider than the data.

Reference: D. W. Scott (2015), Multivariate Density Estimation, 2nd ed.; M. D. Cattaneo et al. (2024), On binscatter (the local-moment / WARPing construction).
