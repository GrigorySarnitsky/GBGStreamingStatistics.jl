Accumulators

The accumulation interface: the AbstractAccumulator supertype and the operations every summary supports — observe! (fold a batch of samples into the summary, on CPU or GPU), merge! (combine two summaries by addition, or — weighted — the nonnegative conic combination), and scale! (reweight a summary by a nonnegative scalar). The concrete summary types live in the Histograms module.

The accumulator type

GBGStreamingStatistics.Accumulators.AbstractAccumulator — Type
AbstractAccumulator

It is the supertype of every mergeable summary $T(n\mathbb{P}_n)$ of the empirical measure $\mathbb{P}_n$ of an observed stream $X_1, X_2, \dots$, which gives a set $A$ the share of samples in it, $\mathbb{P}_n(A) = \#\{i : X_i \in A\}/n$ ($n$ the number of samples seen).

A summary is finite-dimensional — the $n$ samples are never stored — mergeable and scalable (a convex cone over the stream; see merge! and scale!), and rich enough that every reported estimator is recoverable from it post hoc, with no second pass over the data. The summary is accumulated online and is estimand-agnostic, independent of what is later estimated; all smoothing (kernel, bandwidth) is applied post hoc. Concrete summaries (Histogram1D, Histogram2D) subtype this.

Operations

They form the additive monoid (observe!, merge!) and its conic extension (scale!, weighted merge!) — the algebra of hyperstatistics (mixing parameter-indexed summaries post hoc) and exponential forgetting (down-weighting the past).

GBGStreamingStatistics.Accumulators.observe! — Function
observe!(summary::AbstractAccumulator, batch) -> summary

Fold a batch of samples (a vector, or on a CPU summary a single sample) into summary in place, advancing the accumulated summary $T(n\mathbb{P}_n)$ of the empirical measure (see AbstractAccumulator). Accumulation is estimand-agnostic: it knows nothing of which estimator will later be asked for, nor of the bandwidth or smoothing kernel, so nothing is recomputed from the raw stream.

The binning is an atomic scatter-add: one KernelAbstractions + Atomix compute kernel — one work-item per sample — accumulates per bin the count and the power-sums $S_p$ of the samples' offsets from the bin center that a Histogram1D carries, directly into the preallocated summary. The batch form runs identically on a CPU Array and any GPU backend, is allocation-free up to the kernel launch, and accumulates wider than the data (in the element type of the power-sums, Float64 by default, never narrowed to the input element type).

References

  • D. W. Scott (2015), Multivariate Density Estimation, 2nd ed.
  • M. D. Cattaneo et al. (2024), On binscatter (the local-moment construction, WARPing — weighted averaging of rounded points).

Base.merge! — Function
merge!(into::AbstractAccumulator, from::AbstractAccumulator; weight = 1) -> into

Combine two summaries into into, forming the summary of the concatenated stream — the monoid operation that makes accumulation additive. For disjoint sample blocks $\mathcal{A}$ and $\mathcal{B}$, $T(\mathcal{A} \uplus \mathcal{B}) = T(\mathcal{A}) \oplus T(\mathcal{B})$: accumulation $T$, which maps a block to its summary, is a monoid homomorphism from sample blocks (under disjoint union $\uplus$) to summaries (under merge!, the circled plus), on the unnormalized counts and power-sums — the normalized empirical measure $\mathbb{P}_n$ would reweight, not add. Because compatible summaries share the same binning (hence the same bin centers), their counts and power-sums simply add elementwise, so merge! is exact and trivially parallel — this is what makes multi-device reduction and time-chunk stitching free.

The weighted form is the cone. merge!(into, from; weight), the multiplier weight written $\lambda$, forms $\texttt{into} \mathrel{+}= \lambda \cdot \texttt{from}$; the default $\lambda = 1$ is the plain monoid addition above. Because the unnormalized summary is also scalable (scale!), the summaries form a convex cone (closed under nonnegative linear combinations), and the weighted merge realizes the hyperstatistical parameter-mixture density (superstatistics; Beck & Cohen 2003)

\[f_X(x) = \int \pi(\theta) f_\theta(x)\,\mathrm{d}\theta \approx \sum_k \pi_k f_{\theta_k}(x), \qquad \pi_k = \pi(\theta_k) (\theta_{k+1} - \theta_k),\]

a superposition, over a slowly varying parameter $\theta$ with mixing law $\pi(\theta)$, of the densities of the samples drawn at each fixed parameter value: keep one summary $T_k$ per value $\theta_k$, then, after the runs, choose $\pi(\theta)$ and assemble $\sum_k (\pi_k / n(T_k)) T_k$ by weighted merge! (the total count $n(T_k) = \texttt{sum(T\_k.counts)}$ normalizes each component to a probability measure; the conditional and transition estimators, read off a Histogram2D, then weight each component by its mass near the conditioning value, being ratios of linear functionals). This is post hoc, like the bandwidth. A weighted merge ($\lambda \ne 1$) requires into to have floating-point counts (built with count_type = Float64).

Being intrinsically a finite-sample operation, merge! has no population estimand of its own; its specification is the monoid (and, weighted, the cone) law, tested as accumulate-then-merge equals accumulate-together. Extends Base.merge!.

Arguments

  • into — the summary that receives the merge, updated in place.
  • from — the summary folded into into; both share one binning (range, bin count, power-sum order), and from is left unchanged.
  • weight — the nonnegative weight of from in the cone combination (default 1, the plain monoid addition); a weight other than 1 needs floating-point counts in into.

GBGStreamingStatistics.Accumulators.scale! — Function
scale!(summary::AbstractAccumulator, weight) -> summary

Reweight summary in place by a nonnegative multiplier weight, written $\lambda$ — multiply every count and power-sum by it, giving the summary of the reweighted counting measure $\lambda \cdot n\mathbb{P}_n$ of the samples seen. With merge! this makes the unnormalized summaries a convex cone (closed under nonnegative linear combinations), not merely an additive monoid: scale! is the scalar action, merge! the addition.

It has two uses:

  • Exponential forgetting of a non-stationary stream: scale!(summary, λ) with $0 < \lambda < 1$ before folding in the next block down-weights the past geometrically (an effective window $1/(1-\lambda)$).
  • Hyperstatistics uses it as the scalar building block of the parameter-mixture density assembled by weighted merge! (the estimand and recipe are documented there; Beck & Cohen 2003).

It requires floating-point counts (count_type = Float64; the default UInt64 counts cannot hold a fractional count $\lambda n$) and throws an ArgumentError otherwise. The value $\lambda = 1$ is the identity. Like merge!, it is an intrinsically finite-sample operation with no population estimand, specified by the cone laws (scaling by $\lambda$ then by $\lambda'$ equals scaling by $\lambda\lambda'$; scale! distributes over merge!).
