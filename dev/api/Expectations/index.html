Expectations

The functions on this page read a smooth functional of $X$ off the power-sums a Histogram1D of its samples stores (the summed powers of each bin's sample offsets from its center), with no kernel and no query point. The function expectation estimates the expectation $\mathbb{E}[g(X)]$ of a smooth g; expectation_standard_error its sampling standard error (paired with the estimate by expectation_with_standard_error); and pushforward_density the density of the transformed variable $g(X)$.

All three use the same weighted sum of the power-sums as the sample moments (mean / var / std / central_moment), so those are the $g = x^p$ case; a general g's per-bin coefficients come from a local polynomial interpolant (exact on monomials), and the pushforward is the expectation of the kernel composed with g.

GBGStreamingStatistics.Expectations.expectation — Function
expectation(summary::Histogram1D, g)

Estimand. The estimand is the population expectation of a smooth functional g of $X$, $\mathbb{E}[g(X)] = \int g(x) f_X(x)\,\mathrm{d}x$.

Estimator (power-sum quadrature). From the Histogram1D summary, whose bin $j$ (center $c_j$) carries the count $n_j$ and its per-bin power-sums $S_p^{(j)}$ about the center up to the stored order order — the summed powers of its samples' offsets (with $S_0^{(j)} = n_j$) — Taylor-expanding $g$ about each center gives

\[\widehat{\mathbb{E}}[g(X)] = \frac{1}{n} \sum_j \sum_{p=0}^{\texttt{order}} \frac{g^{(p)}(c_j)}{p!} S_p^{(j)} ,\]

which is exact for a polynomial $g$ of degree $\le \texttt{order}$ (the remainder terminates; for $g = x^p$ this is the shift formula behind mean, var and central_moment, the binomial re-expansion from the bin center to the sample mean) and $O(\Delta^{ \texttt{order}+1})$ in the bin width $\Delta$ for a general smooth $g$. The local coefficients $g^{(p)}(c_j)/p!$ are read off a degree-$\texttt{order}$ polynomial interpolant of $g$ on each bin, so any callable g works; g is probed only on non-empty bins, hence only within the data range. The estimand needs $g$ smooth on the bin scale and the mass inside the summary's range. An empty summary gives NaN.

The arguments are summary, the Histogram1D of $X$, and g, the functional to average: any callable $x \mapsto g(x)$ (smooth on the bin scale) — the moments are the $g = x^p$ case (mean is g = identity).

julia> h = Histogram1D(-4.0, 4.0, 800);

julia> # the g = identity case is the recovered mean 𝔼[X] = 1/3
       observe!(h, [-1.0, 0.0, 2.0]);

julia> expectation(h, identity) ≈ mean(h)
true

GBGStreamingStatistics.Expectations.expectation_standard_error — Function
expectation_standard_error(summary::Histogram1D, g)

Estimand. The estimand is the sampling standard error of the estimator expectation, $\operatorname{SE}\bigl(\widehat{\mathbb{E}}[g(X)]\bigr) = \sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(g(X))/n}$ with $\operatorname{Var}(g(X)) = \mathbb{E}[g(X)^2] - \mathbb{E}[g(X)]^2$.

Estimator. The estimator is $\sqrt{(\widehat{\mathbb{E}}[g^2] - \widehat{\mathbb{E}}[g]^2)/n}$, where both moments come from the same power-sum quadrature ($\mathbb{E}[g^2]$ is expectation with the smooth integrand $g^2$) and $n$ is the sample count. This is the plug-in sampling error of the empirical mean of $g(X)$; it does not include the $O(\Delta^{ \texttt{order}+1})$ quadrature bias, which is separate. An empty summary gives NaN.

Arguments: as for expectation.

GBGStreamingStatistics.Expectations.expectation_with_standard_error — Function
expectation_with_standard_error(summary::Histogram1D, g)

Returns the estimate and its standard error from two power-sum quadrature passes (the functional, then its square) — the NamedTuple $(\texttt{value}, \texttt{standard\_error})$, with value the expectation $\widehat{\mathbb{E}}[g(X)]$ and standard_error its expectation_standard_error. They describe the same estimator by construction, and $\widehat{\mathbb{E}}[g]$ is computed once and shared.

Arguments: as for expectation.

julia> using GBGStreamingStatistics

julia> h = Histogram1D(-8.0, 8.0, 800); observe!(h, [-1.0, 0.0, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0]);

julia> r = expectation_with_standard_error(h, cos);

julia> (r.value isa Float64, r.standard_error > 0)
(true, true)

GBGStreamingStatistics.Expectations.pushforward_density — Function
pushforward_density(summary::Histogram1D, g, z;
                    kernel = gaussian_kernel, h)

Estimand. The estimand is the density of the transformed variable $Z = g(X)$ under the transform g, the pushforward $f_{g(X)}(z) = \sum_{x: g(x)=z} f_X(x) / |g'(x)|$ (the change-of-variables sum over the preimages of $z$).

Estimator. The estimator is the kernel density estimate of the pushforward, with the kernel scaled to the bandwidth h, written as an expectation and evaluated by expectation:

\[\hat f_{g(X)}(z) = \frac{1}{n} \sum_i K_h(g(X_i) - z) = \widehat{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[ K_h(g(X) - z) \bigr] ,\]

so the composed test function $x \mapsto K_h(g(x) - z)$ is smoothed by the power-sum quadrature — no inverse $g^{-1}$ and no explicit Jacobian are formed; a non-monotone $g$ is handled, every preimage contributing, and a flat spot $g'(x) = 0$ shows up as an integrable caustic pile-up. For a symmetric kernel ($\int K = 1$) the surviving $1/h$ makes $\int \hat f_{g(X)}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z = 1$ exactly. It needs $g$ smooth on the bin scale and the bandwidth $h$ at least of order $|g'| \Delta$ (so $K_h \circ g$ is resolved across a bin). A vector z returns the density curve over those points.

Arguments

  • summary — the Histogram1D of $X$.
  • g — the transform defining $Z = g(X)$ (any callable, smooth on the bin scale).
  • z — the point(s) $z$ at which the pushforward density is evaluated (a scalar, or a vector for the curve).
  • kernel — the $z$-direction kernel (any callable integrating to one, $\int K = 1$; default gaussian_kernel).
  • h — the $z$-direction bandwidth ($h > 0$), supplied.

Reference: B. W. Silverman (1986), Density Estimation for Statistics and Data Analysis, Chapman & Hall.

GBGStreamingStatistics.Expectations.pushforward_density_standard_error — Function
pushforward_density_standard_error(summary::Histogram1D, g, z;
                                   kernel = gaussian_kernel, h)

Estimand. The estimand is the pointwise standard error of the pushforward density pushforward_density $\equiv f_{g(X)}$, $\operatorname{SE}(z) = \sqrt{\operatorname{Var}\hat f_{g(X)}(z)}$ with the two-term kernel-density variance $\operatorname{Var}\hat f_{g(X)}(z) \approx R(K) f_{g(X)}(z)/(nh) - f_{g(X)}(z)^2/n$ ($R(K) = \int K^2$).

Estimator. Because $\hat f_{g(X)}(z) = \widehat{\mathbb{E}}[K_h(g(X) - z)]$ is a sample mean of the composed kernel, its standard error is exactly the expectation_standard_error of that integrand — $\sqrt{(\widehat{\mathbb{E}}[K_h(g(X)-z)^2] - \hat f_{g(X)}(z)^2)/n}$, which reduces to the two-term form above. A vector z returns a vector of standard errors.

Arguments: as for pushforward_density.

Recovered moments

The global marginal moments — the $g = x^p$ case — are recovered exactly from the power-sums by the shift formula, the binomial re-expansion of each bin's powers about its center, and extend Statistics.

Statistics.mean — Method
mean(summary::Histogram1D)

Estimand. The estimand is the population mean $\mathbb{E}[X]$.

Estimator. The estimator is the sample mean $\bar X = \frac{1}{n} \sum_i X_i$, recovered exactly, not smoothed, from the per-bin counts and first power-sums by the shift formula, which binomially re-expands each bin's powers about its center (the power-sums use the actual $X_i$, not the bin center; the $g = x$ case of expectation). It extends Statistics.mean, and an empty summary gives NaN.

Statistics.var — Method
var(summary::Histogram1D; corrected = true)

Estimand. The estimand is the population variance $\operatorname{Var}(X) = \mathbb{E}\bigl[(X - \mathbb{E}[X])^2\bigr]$.

Estimator. The estimator is the recovered variance, $\bar X$ the recovered mean: with corrected = false it is the order-2 central moment $\hat\sigma_X^2 = \hat\mu_2 = \frac{1}{n} \sum_i (X_i - \bar X)^2$, the $\hat\sigma_X$ that bandwidth uses; with the default corrected = true, matching Statistics.var, it is the unbiased $n\hat\sigma_X^2/(n-1)$ (NaN for $n \le 1$). Exact by the shift formula, it extends Statistics.var.

Arguments

  • summary — the Histogram1D of $X$.
  • corrected — true (the default) for the unbiased $1/(n-1)$ normalization, false for $1/n$.

Statistics.std — Method
std(summary::Histogram1D; corrected = true)

Estimand. The estimand is the population standard deviation $\sigma_X = \sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(X)}$.

Estimator. The estimator is the square root of var: $\hat\sigma_X = \sqrt{\hat\mu_2}$ with corrected = false, $\hat\sigma_X \sqrt{n/(n-1)}$ with the default corrected = true. It extends Statistics.std.

Arguments

  • summary — the Histogram1D of $X$.
  • corrected — as for var: true (the default) divides the variance by $n - 1$, false by $n$.

GBGStreamingStatistics.Expectations.central_moment — Function
central_moment(summary::Histogram1D, p)

Estimand. The estimand is the population $p$-th central moment $\mu_p(X) = \mathbb{E}\bigl[(X - \mathbb{E}[X])^p\bigr]$.

Estimator. The estimator is the sample central moment $\hat\mu_p = \frac{1}{n} \sum_i (X_i - \bar X)^p$ ($\bar X$ the recovered mean), recovered exactly from the power-sums by the shift formula for $2 \le p \le \texttt{order}$, with order the highest power-sum order the summary stores; any other order throws an ArgumentError. The order $p = 2$ is the uncorrected variance (var with corrected = false); even orders recover stably, odd orders ($\approx 0$) to weaker relative precision. This is the $g = (x - \bar X)^p$ case of the power-sum quadrature expectation, sharing its weighted sum over the power-sums.
