Expectations

The power-sum quadrature readouts read a smooth functional of $X$ straight off the stored power-sums (per bin, the summed powers of the samples' offsets), with no kernel and no query point. The reader expectation estimates the expectation $\mathbb{E}[g(X)]$ of a general smooth g; expectation_standard_error its sampling standard error (paired with the estimate by expectation_with_standard_error); and pushforward_density the density of the transformed variable $g(X)$. All three are the same weighted sum over the power-sums as the recovered moments (mean / var / std / central_moment), so those are the $g = x^p$ case; a general g's per-bin coefficients come from a local polynomial interpolant (exact on monomials), and the pushforward is expectation of the composed kernel.

GBGStreamingStatistics.Expectations.expectation — Function
expectation(summary::Histogram1D, g)   # ≡ 𝔼[g(X)]

Estimand. The population expectation of a smooth functional of $X$, $\mathbb{E}[g(X)] = \int g(x) f_X(x)\,\mathrm{d}x$.

Estimator (power-sum quadrature). From the Histogram1D summary, whose bin $j$ (center $c_j$) carries the count $n_j$ and its per-bin power-sums $S_p^{(j)}$ about the center — the summed powers of its samples' offsets (with $S_0^{(j)} = n_j$) — Taylor-expanding $g$ about each center gives

\[\widehat{\mathbb{E}}[g(X)] = \frac{1}{n} \sum_j \sum_{p=0}^{\texttt{order}} \frac{g^{(p)}(c_j)}{p!} S_p^{(j)} ,\]

which is exact for a polynomial $g$ of degree $\le \texttt{order}$ (the remainder terminates — this is the shift formula behind mean / var / central_moment — the binomial re-expansion from the bin center to the sample mean — the $g = x^p$ case) and $O(\Delta^{ \texttt{order}+1})$ in the bin width $\Delta$ for a general smooth $g$. The local coefficients $g^{(p)}(c_j)/p!$ are read off a degree-$\texttt{order}$ polynomial interpolant of $g$ on each bin, so any callable g works; g is probed only on non-empty bins, hence only within the data range. The estimand needs $g$ smooth on the bin scale and the mass inside the summary's range. An empty summary gives NaN.

The arguments are summary, the Histogram1D of $X$, and g, the functional to average: any callable $x \mapsto g(x)$ (smooth on the bin scale) — the moments are the $g = x^p$ case (mean is g = identity).

julia> h = Histogram1D(-4.0, 4.0, 800);

julia> observe!(h, [-1.0, 0.0, 2.0]);   # the g = identity case is the recovered mean E[X] = 1/3

julia> expectation(h, identity) ≈ mean(h)
true

GBGStreamingStatistics.Expectations.expectation_standard_error — Function
expectation_standard_error(summary::Histogram1D, g)

Estimand. The sampling standard error of the estimator expectation, $\mathrm{SE}\bigl(\widehat{\mathbb{E}}[g(X)]\bigr) = \sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(g(X))/n}$ with $\operatorname{Var}(g(X)) = \mathbb{E}[g(X)^2] - \mathbb{E}[g(X)]^2$.

Estimator. The estimator is $\sqrt{(\widehat{\mathbb{E}}[g^2] - \widehat{\mathbb{E}}[g]^2)/n}$, both moments by the same power-sum quadrature ($\mathbb{E}[g^2]$ is expectation with the smooth integrand $g^2$), with $n$ the sample count. This is the plug-in sampling error of the empirical mean of $g(X)$; it does not include the $O(\Delta^{ \texttt{order}+1})$ quadrature bias, which is separate. NaN for an empty summary.

Arguments: as for expectation.

GBGStreamingStatistics.Expectations.expectation_with_standard_error — Function
expectation_with_standard_error(summary::Histogram1D, g)

Returns the estimate and its standard error from two power-sum quadrature passes (the functional, then its square) — the NamedTuple $(\texttt{value}, \texttt{standard\_error})$, with value the expectation $\widehat{\mathbb{E}}[g(X)]$ and standard_error its expectation_standard_error. They describe the same estimator by construction, and $\widehat{\mathbb{E}}[g]$ is computed once and shared.

Arguments: as for expectation.

julia> using GBGStreamingStatistics

julia> h = Histogram1D(-8.0, 8.0, 800); observe!(h, [-1.0, 0.0, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0]);

julia> r = expectation_with_standard_error(h, cos); (r.value isa Float64, r.standard_error > 0)
(true, true)

GBGStreamingStatistics.Expectations.pushforward_density — Function
pushforward_density(summary::Histogram1D, g, y;   # ≡ f_{g(X)}
                    kernel = gaussian_kernel, h)

Estimand. The density of the transformed variable $Y = g(X)$, the pushforward $f_{g(X)}(y) = \sum_{x: g(x)=y} f_X(x) / |g'(x)|$ (the change-of-variables sum over the preimages of $y$).

Estimator. The kernel density estimate of the pushforward, written as an expectation and evaluated by expectation:

\[\hat f_{g(X)}(y) = \frac{1}{n} \sum_i K_h(g(X_i) - y) = \widehat{\mathbb{E}}\bigl[ K_h(g(X) - y) \bigr] ,\]

so the composed test function $x \mapsto K_h(g(x) - y)$ is smoothed by the power-sum quadrature — no inverse $g^{-1}$ and no explicit Jacobian are formed; a non-monotone $g$ is handled automatically (every preimage contributes), and a flat spot $g'(x) = 0$ shows up as the expected (integrable) caustic pile-up. For a symmetric kernel ($\int K = 1$) the surviving $1/h$ makes $\int \hat f\,\mathrm{d}y = 1$ exactly. Needs $g$ smooth on the bin scale and the response bandwidth $h$ at least of order $|g'| \Delta$ (so $K_h \circ g$ is resolved across a bin). A vector y returns the density curve over those points.

Arguments

  • summary — the Histogram1D of $X$.
  • g — the transform defining $Y = g(X)$ (any callable, smooth on the bin scale).
  • y — the response point(s) at which the pushforward density is evaluated (a scalar, or a vector for the curve).
  • kernel — the response-direction kernel (any $\int K = 1$ callable; default gaussian_kernel).
  • h — the response-direction bandwidth ($h > 0$), supplied.

Reference: B. W. Silverman (1986), Density Estimation for Statistics and Data Analysis, Chapman & Hall.

GBGStreamingStatistics.Expectations.pushforward_density_standard_error — Function
pushforward_density_standard_error(summary::Histogram1D, g, y;
                                   kernel = gaussian_kernel, h)

Estimand. The pointwise standard error of the pushforward density pushforward_density $\equiv f_{g(X)}$, $\mathrm{SE}(y) = \sqrt{\operatorname{Var}\hat f_{g(X)}(y)}$ with the two-term kernel-density variance $\operatorname{Var}\hat f_{g(X)}(y) \approx R(K) f_{g(X)}(y)/(nh) - f_{g(X)}(y)^2/n$ ($R(K) = \int K^2$).

Estimator. Because $\hat f_{g(X)}(y) = \widehat{\mathbb{E}}[K_h(g(X) - y)]$ is a sample mean of the composed kernel, its standard error is exactly the expectation_standard_error of that integrand — $\sqrt{(\widehat{\mathbb{E}}[K_h(g(X)-y)^2] - \hat f_{g(X)}(y)^2)/n}$, which reduces to the two-term form above. A vector y returns a vector of standard errors.

Arguments: as for pushforward_density.

Recovered moments

The global marginal moments — the $g = x^p$ case — are recovered post hoc from the power-sums by the shift formula, exactly, extending Statistics.

Statistics.mean — Method
mean(summary::Histogram1D)

Estimand. The estimand is the population mean $\mathbb{E}[X]$.

Estimator. The estimator is the sample mean $\bar X = \frac{1}{n} \sum_i X_i$, recovered exactly (not a smoothed estimate) from the per-bin counts and first power-sums by the shift formula (the power-sums use the actual $X_i$, not the bin center; the $g = x$ case of expectation). Extends Statistics.mean; an empty summary gives NaN.

Statistics.var — Method
var(summary::Histogram1D; corrected = true)

Estimand. The population variance $\operatorname{Var}(X) = \mathbb{E}\bigl[(X - \mathbb{E}[X])^2\bigr]$.

Estimator. The estimator is the recovered sample variance ($\bar X$ the recovered mean). With corrected = true (the default, matching Statistics.var) the unbiased $\frac{1}{n-1} \sum_i (X_i - \bar X)^2$ (Bessel's correction; NaN for $n \le 1$); corrected = false the $\frac{1}{n} \sum_i (X_i - \bar X)^2$ order-2 central moment (central_moment at $p = 2$). Exact via the shift formula. Extends Statistics.var.

Arguments

  • summary — the Histogram1D of $X$.
  • corrected — true (the default) for the unbiased $1/(n-1)$ normalization, false for $1/n$.

Statistics.std — Method
std(summary::Histogram1D; corrected = true)

Estimand. The population standard deviation $\sigma = \sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(X)}$.

Estimator. The estimator is the recovered sample standard deviation $\sqrt{s^2}$, where $s^2$ is the recovered variance (var). Exact via the shift formula. Extends Statistics.std.

Arguments

  • summary — the Histogram1D of $X$.
  • corrected — as for var: true (the default) divides the variance by $n - 1$, false by $n$.

GBGStreamingStatistics.Expectations.central_moment — Function
central_moment(summary::Histogram1D, p)

Estimand. The population $p$-th central moment $\mu_p = \mathbb{E}\bigl[(X - \mathbb{E}[X])^p\bigr]$.

Estimator. The estimator is the sample central moment $\frac{1}{n} \sum_i (X_i - \bar X)^p$ ($\bar X$ the recovered mean), recovered exactly from the power-sums by the shift formula for $2 \le p \le \texttt{order}$ (any other order throws an ArgumentError). The order $p = 2$ is the uncorrected variance (var with corrected = false); even orders recover stably, odd orders ($\approx 0$) to weaker relative precision. This is the $g = (x - \bar X)^p$ case of the power-sum quadrature expectation, with which it shares the weighted sum over the power-sums.
