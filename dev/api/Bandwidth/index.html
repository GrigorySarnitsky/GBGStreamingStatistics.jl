Bandwidth

Bandwidth selection: the kernel- and degree-aware normal-reference rule (the default; Silverman the Gaussian/degree-0 case), the data-driven Sheather–Jones (rule = :sj classic, :isj improved-via-DCT) and fit-based AIC (rule = :aic) selectors (leave-one-out CV a recorded binned-ties gap), and the response-aware local plug-in $h^\star(x)$ (§4.5).

GBGStreamingStatistics.Bandwidth.bandwidth — Method
bandwidth(summary::Histogram1D; rule = :normal, kernel = gaussian_kernel, degree = 0) -> h

Estimand. the AMISE-optimal smoothing bandwidth for the marginal density density of summary — the $h$ minimising $\mathrm{MISE}(\hat f_h) = \int \mathbb{E}\bigl[(\hat f_h(x) - f_X(x))^2\bigr]\,\mathrm{d}x$ for the smoothing kernel $K$ and local-polynomial degree $d$.

Estimator (rule = :normal, the kernel- and degree-aware normal-reference plug-in). From the recovered $\hat\sigma$ and the sample size $n$,

\[h = C(K, d)\,\hat\sigma\,n^{-1/(2p+1)},\]

where $p$ is the estimator's leading bias order — $p = 2$ for degree 0 or 1 (the rate $n^{-1/5}$), $p = 4$ for degree 2 or 3 (a higher-order, $O(h^4)$, fit, hence the wider, more slowly shrinking rate $n^{-1/9}$). At degree 0/1 the constant is the canonical bandwidth

\[C(K, d) = (8\sqrt{\pi}/3)^{1/5}\,\delta(K), \qquad \delta(K) = \bigl[R(K)/\mu_2(K)^2\bigr]^{1/5}, \quad R(K) = \int K^2,\ \mu_2(K) = \int u^2 K(u)\,\mathrm{d}u,\]

and at degree 2/3 the analogous fourth-order equivalent-kernel constant (Loader 1996). $R(K)$ and $\mu_2(K)$ are computed by quadrature, so any $K$ is supported. For the Gaussian kernel at degree 0 this is Silverman's rule $(4/3)^{1/5}\hat\sigma\,n^{-1/5}$; for the Epanechnikov kernel it is $\approx 2.34\,\hat\sigma\,n^{-1/5}$ ($2.21\times$ the Gaussian $h$). Every input is read back from the one stored summary, post hoc.

Estimator (rule = :sj, the data-driven Sheather–Jones plug-in; degree 0/1). The curvature $\psi_4 = R(f'')$ and its $\psi_6$ pilot are estimated from the bin counts as Hermite-weighted reductions, and the solve-the-equation bandwidth (Sheather & Jones 1991) is found by root-finding; the eval kernel then enters as the same canonical $\delta(K)$ rescale as for :normal (the curvature is a Gaussian-pilot estimate, kernel-independent). It adapts to the true shape — on a bimodal sample, where the normal reference oversmooths from an inflated $\hat\sigma$, it picks a much smaller $h$. Both functionals are reductions of the bin counts, so they are mergeable; a degree $\ge 2$ request is a recorded coverage gap (use rule = :normal).

Estimator (rule = :isj, the Improved Sheather–Jones plug-in; Botev, Grotowski & Kroese 2010; degree 0/1). The same solve-the-equation idea, but the curvature norms $\|f^{(s)}\|^2$ are read off the DCT of the bin counts (Plancherel) rather than from a normal-reference pilot — so it stays reliable on strongly multimodal data, where the pilot scale of :sj can mislead. Mergeable (a functional of the counts' cosine transform); the eval kernel again enters as the $\delta(K)$ rescale.

Estimator (rule = :aic, the fit-based information criterion; degree 0/1). Over a log-spaced $h$-grid around the normal reference, minimise $\mathrm{AIC}(h) = -2\sum_j n_j \log \hat f_h(c_j) + 2\,\mathrm{df}(h)$ of the binned KDE, with $\mathrm{df}(h) = (K(0)/h)\sum_j n_j/(n\hat f_h(c_j))$ the kernel self-influence trace. Both terms diverge as $h \to 0$, so the optimum is a stable interior point; like :sj it is a deterministic functional of the counts (mergeable), and the kernel enters as the canonical $\delta(K)$ rescale. The leave-one-out cross-validation selectors (likelihood-CV and least-squares LSCV) are a deliberate coverage gap — binning ties drive their optimum to $h \to 0$ (use :sj for a data-driven alternative).

Asymmetric kernels (gamma_kernel, beta_kernel) carry their width in the varying shape, so they take their own scale $b$ (rate $n^{-2/5}$), not the symmetric $h$, and accept two rules. rule = :normal (default) is the $\hat\sigma\,n^{-2/5}$ rule of thumb. rule = :plugin is the Chen plug-in, the asymmetric analog of Silverman's rule: from the AMISE $A b^2 + C b^{-1/2}/n$ (Chen 1999, 2000; the modified $f_2$ kernels) with $A = \tfrac14\int w(x)^2 f''(x)^2\,\mathrm{d}x$ and $C = (2\sqrt\pi)^{-1}\,\mathbb{E}[w(X)^{-1/2}]$, where $w(x) = x$ (gamma) or $x(1-x)$ (beta) is the kernel's local-scale weight,

\[b^\star = \left[\frac{\mathbb{E}[w(X)^{-1/2}]}{2\sqrt\pi\,n \int w(x)^2 f''(x)^2\,\mathrm{d}x}\right]^{2/5}.\]

$\mathbb{E}[w(X)^{-1/2}]$ is an exact moment of the bin counts; the curvature $\int w^2 (f'')^2$ is estimated from a pilot KDE's second derivative by grid quadrature (valid at the boundary because $w \to 0$ there, unlike an integration-by-parts functional). It lowers the ISE by $\approx 1.4\text{–}1.7\times$ on skewed positive data — where the rule of thumb mis-scales — and is otherwise near it. The symmetric selectors :sj / :isj / :aic do not apply to an asymmetric kernel; a fully data-adaptive curvature pilot is a recorded refinement.

The response-aware local plug-in $h^\star(x) = \bigl[R(K)\,\sigma^2(x) / (\mu_2(K)^2\, m''(x)^2\, f_X(x)\, n)\bigr]^{1/5}$ — each response channel its own bandwidth from the conditional variance $\sigma^2(x)$ and regression curvature $m''(x)$ — is forthcoming (CLAUDE.md roadmap).

References: B. W. Silverman (1986), Density Estimation for Statistics and Data Analysis, Chapman & Hall (the normal-reference rule and the canonical bandwidth); S. P. Sheather & M. C. Jones (1991), A reliable data-based bandwidth selection method for kernel density estimation, J. R. Statist. Soc. B 53(3) (the solve-the-equation plug-in); Z. I. Botev, J. F. Grotowski & D. P. Kroese (2010), Kernel density estimation via diffusion, Ann. Statist. 38(5) (the DCT solve-the-equation, :isj); C. R. Loader (1996), Local likelihood density estimation, Ann. Statist. 24(4) (the degree / equivalent- kernel factor).
