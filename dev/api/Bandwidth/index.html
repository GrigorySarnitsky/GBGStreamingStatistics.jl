Bandwidth

Bandwidth selection for the density estimators. For the marginal (Histogram1D): the kernel- and degree-aware normal-reference rule (the default; Silverman the Gaussian/degree-0 case), the data-driven Sheather–Jones (rule = :sj classic, :isj improved-via-DCT), fit-based AIC (rule = :aic), and asymmetric-kernel Chen plug-in (rule = :plugin) selectors; for a data-driven choice prefer rule = :sj or :isj. For the joint (Histogram2D): the response-aware local plug-in $h^\star(x)$ (rule = :local).

GBGStreamingStatistics.Bandwidth.bandwidth — Function
bandwidth(summary::Histogram1D; rule = :normal, kernel = gaussian_kernel,
          degree = 0) -> h

Estimand. The AMISE-optimal smoothing bandwidth for the marginal density density of summary — the $h$ minimising $\mathrm{MISE}(\hat f_h) = \int \mathbb{E}\bigl[(\hat f_h(x) - f_X(x))^2\bigr]\,\mathrm{d}x$ for the smoothing kernel $K$ and local-polynomial degree $d$.

Estimator (rule = :normal, the kernel- and degree-aware normal-reference plug-in). From the recovered $\hat\sigma$ and the sample size $n$,

\[h = C(K, d)\,\hat\sigma\,n^{-1/(2p+1)},\]

where $p$ is the estimator's leading bias order — $p = 2$ for degree 0 or 1 (the rate $n^{-1/5}$), $p = 4$ for degree 2 or 3 (a higher-order, $O(h^4)$, fit, hence the wider, more slowly shrinking rate $n^{-1/9}$). At degree 0/1 the constant is the canonical bandwidth

\[\begin{aligned} C(K, d) &= (8\sqrt{\pi}/3)^{1/5}\,\delta(K), \\ \delta(K) &= \bigl[R(K)/\mu_2(K)^2\bigr]^{1/5}, \\ R(K) &= \int K^2, \qquad \mu_2(K) = \int u^2 K(u)\,\mathrm{d}u, \end{aligned}\]

and at degree 2/3 the analogous fourth-order equivalent-kernel constant (Loader 1996). $R(K)$ and $\mu_2(K)$ are computed by quadrature, so any $K$ is supported. For the Gaussian kernel at degree 0 this is Silverman's rule $(4/3)^{1/5}\hat\sigma\,n^{-1/5}$; for the Epanechnikov kernel it is $\approx 2.34\,\hat\sigma\,n^{-1/5}$ ($2.21\times$ the Gaussian $h$). Every input is read back from the one stored summary, post hoc.

Estimator (rule = :sj, the data-driven Sheather–Jones plug-in; degree 0/1). The curvature $\psi_4 = R(f'')$ and its $\psi_6$ pilot are estimated from the bin counts as Hermite-weighted reductions, and the solve-the-equation bandwidth (Sheather & Jones 1991) is found by root-finding; the eval kernel then enters as the same canonical $\delta(K)$ rescale as for :normal (the curvature is a Gaussian-pilot estimate, kernel-independent). It adapts to the true shape — on a bimodal sample, where the normal reference oversmooths from an inflated $\hat\sigma$, it picks a much smaller $h$. Both functionals are reductions of the bin counts, so they are mergeable; :sj is a degree-0/1 selector, so for a degree $\ge 2$ fit use rule = :normal.

Estimator (rule = :isj, the Improved Sheather–Jones plug-in; Botev, Grotowski & Kroese 2010; degree 0/1). The same solve-the-equation idea, but the curvature norms $\|f^{(s)}\|^2$ are read off the DCT of the bin counts (Plancherel) rather than from a normal-reference pilot — so it stays reliable on strongly multimodal data, where the pilot scale of :sj can mislead. The eval kernel again enters as the $\delta(K)$ rescale.

Estimator (rule = :aic, the fit-based information criterion; degree 0/1). Over a log-spaced $h$-grid around the normal reference, minimise $\mathrm{AIC}(h) = -2\sum_j n_j \log \hat f_h(c_j) + 2\,\mathrm{df}(h)$ of the binned Gaussian KDE, with $\mathrm{df}(h) = (\varphi(0)/h)\sum_j n_j/(n\hat f_h(c_j))$ the kernel self-influence trace ($\varphi$ the Gaussian). Both terms diverge as $h \to 0$, so the optimum is a stable interior point, and the eval kernel enters only as the canonical $\delta(K)$ rescale of the selected $h$. The leave-one-out cross-validation selectors (likelihood-CV and least-squares LSCV) are not offered — binning ties drive their optimum to $h \to 0$; use :sj or :isj for a data-driven alternative.

Asymmetric kernels (gamma_kernel, beta_kernel) carry their width in the varying shape, so they take their own scale $b$ (rate $n^{-2/5}$), not the symmetric $h$, and accept two rules. rule = :normal (default) is the $\hat\sigma\,n^{-2/5}$ rule of thumb. rule = :plugin is the Chen plug-in, the asymmetric analog of Silverman's rule: from the AMISE $A b^2 + C b^{-1/2}/n$ (Chen 1999, 2000; the modified $f_2$ kernels) with $A = \tfrac14\int w(x)^2 f''(x)^2\,\mathrm{d}x$ and $C = (2\sqrt\pi)^{-1}\,\mathbb{E}[w(X)^{-1/2}]$, where $w(x) = x$ (gamma) or $x(1-x)$ (beta) is the kernel's local-scale weight,

\[b^\star = \left[\frac{\mathbb{E}[w(X)^{-1/2}]}{2\sqrt\pi\,n \int w(x)^2 f''(x)^2\,\mathrm{d}x}\right]^{2/5}.\]

$\mathbb{E}[w(X)^{-1/2}]$ is an exact moment of the bin counts; the curvature $\int w^2 (f'')^2$ is estimated from a pilot KDE's second derivative by grid quadrature (valid at the boundary because $w \to 0$ there, unlike an integration-by-parts functional). It lowers the ISE by $\approx 1.4\text{–}1.7\times$ on skewed positive data — where the rule of thumb mis-scales — and is otherwise near it. rule = :plugin also accepts a custom AsymmetricKernel, provided it was built with its AMISE weight $w(x)$ (the three-argument constructor); a weightless (two-argument) AsymmetricKernel carries no $w(x)$, so :plugin rejects it — use rule = :normal. The symmetric selectors :sj / :isj / :aic do not apply to an asymmetric kernel; a fully data-adaptive curvature pilot is a recorded refinement.

The response-aware local plug-in $h^\star(x) = \bigl[R(K)\,\sigma^2(x) / (\mu_2(K)^2\, m''(x)^2\, f_X(x)\, n)\bigr]^{1/5}$ — each conditioning value its own bandwidth from the conditional variance $\sigma^2(x)$ and regression curvature $m''(x)$ — is the separate joint-summary selector bandwidth(summary::Histogram2D, x; rule = :local) (below).

Arguments:

  • summary — the Histogram1D of $X$; its recovered $\hat\sigma$ (and, for the data-driven rules, its bin counts) are the inputs.
  • rule — the selector (each described above): :normal (default), :sj, :isj, or :aic for a symmetric kernel; :normal (default) or :plugin for an asymmetric one.
  • kernel — the smoothing kernel the bandwidth is for (default gaussian_kernel); it enters through the canonical factor $\delta(K) = [R(K)/\mu_2(K)^2]^{1/5}$.
  • degree — the local-polynomial order the bandwidth is for (default 0); it sets the bias order (the data-driven rules are degree 0/1).

References:

  • B. W. Silverman (1986), Density Estimation for Statistics and Data Analysis, Chapman & Hall (the normal-reference rule and the canonical bandwidth).
  • S. P. Sheather & M. C. Jones (1991), A reliable data-based bandwidth selection method for kernel density estimation, J. R. Statist. Soc. B 53(3) (the solve-the-equation plug-in).
  • Z. I. Botev, J. F. Grotowski & D. P. Kroese (2010), Kernel density estimation via diffusion, Ann. Statist. 38(5) (the DCT solve-the-equation, :isj).
  • C. R. Loader (1996), Local likelihood density estimation, Ann. Statist. 24(4) (the degree / equivalent-kernel factor).
source
bandwidth(summary::Histogram2D, x;
          rule = :local, kernel = gaussian_kernel, hpilot = nothing)   # ≡ h⋆(x)

Estimand. The response-aware local AMISE-optimal bandwidth $h^\star(x)$ for the conditional mean conditional_mean $m(x)$ — the bandwidth that, at each conditioning value $x$, minimises the local mean squared error of the local-linear estimate $\hat m_h(x)$ by balancing its squared bias against its variance:

\[h^\star(x) = \left[\frac{R(K)\,\sigma^2(x)}{\mu_2(K)^2\,m''(x)^2\,f_X(x)\,n}\right]^{1/5},\]

with $\sigma^2(x)$ the conditional variance, $m''(x)$ the regression curvature, $f_X(x)$ the marginal density of the conditioning variable $X$, $n$ the sample size, and the kernel constants $R(K) = \int K^2$, $\mu_2(K) = \int u^2 K(u)\,\mathrm{d}u$.

Estimator (rule = :local). The three $x$-local quantities are read from the one joint summary, each at a single pilot bandwidth: the conditional variance $\sigma^2(x)$ from the bias-reduced Fan & Yao conditional_variance (its default residual estimator), the curvature $m''(x) = 2\beta_2$ from the degree-2 local-polynomial fit, and $f_X(x)$ from the binned KDE of the X-marginal. $R(K)$ and $\mu_2(K)$ are computed by quadrature, so any kernel works. Because estimating a second derivative needs a wider window than the final fit, the default pilot is the curvature-rate rule of thumb $\hat\sigma_X\, n^{-1/9}$; where the regression is locally linear ($m''(x) \to 0$) the optimal bandwidth is unbounded, so it is capped at the X-range. A vector x returns a vector; NaN marks an $x$ off the data's support.

Arguments:

  • summary — the joint Histogram2D of $(X, Y)$.
  • x — the conditioning value(s) at which $h^\star$ is evaluated (a scalar, or a vector for the whole bandwidth profile).
  • rule — the selector; only :local (the response-aware local plug-in) is defined for a joint summary.
  • kernel — the smoothing kernel $K$ (any callable with $\int K = 1$; default gaussian_kernel).
  • hpilot — the pilot bandwidth for the $\sigma^2(x)$, $m''(x)$, $f_X(x)$ estimates; nothing (default) uses the curvature-rate rule of thumb $\hat\sigma_X\, n^{-1/9}$.

Reference: J. Fan & I. Gijbels (1996), Local Polynomial Modelling and Its Applications, Chapman & Hall.
