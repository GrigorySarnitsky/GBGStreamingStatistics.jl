MarginalDensity

The marginal density $\equiv f_X$ is estimated by density; its pointwise standard error is density_standard_error, density_with_standard_error returns both, and the parametric starts serve its semiparametric mode. The estimator density accepts any symmetric or bounded-support kernel from the Kernels page.

Density

GBGStreamingStatistics.MarginalDensity.density — Function
density(summary::Histogram1D, x;   # ≡ f_X
        kernel = gaussian_kernel, degree = 0,
        h = bandwidth(summary; kernel, degree), adaptive = false,
        support = nothing, start = nothing, normalize = false, binning = :linear)

Estimand. The estimand is the marginal (Lebesgue) density $f_X = \mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}_X/\mathrm{d}x$ of the variate $X$ with law $\mathbb{P}_X$, defined by $\mathbb{P}_X(A) = \int_A f_X(x)\,\mathrm{d}x$.

Estimator. The estimator is the smoothed estimate $\hat f_X(x)$ at bandwidth h from the accumulated summary: a binned local smoother of the counts $n_j$ at bin locations $\tilde c_j$ (the within-bin centroid by default; see the binning option) — a binned KDE (WARPing, weighted averaging of rounded points) at degree = 0, or a local-log-polynomial fit of $\log f_X$ at degree $\ge 1$.

Arguments

  • summary — the Histogram1D of $X$.
  • x — the query point(s): a scalar for the density at one point, or a vector for the whole curve.
  • kernel — the smoothing weight $K$ (any callable with $\int K = 1$), default the symmetric gaussian_kernel. The compact-support symmetric epanechnikov_kernel and, for bounded / positive support, the asymmetric gamma_kernel ($[0,\infty)$) and beta_kernel ($[0,1]$) — boundary-bias-free by construction — are catalogued on the Kernels page; supply a custom AsymmetricKernel for another support. An asymmetric kernel is its own degree-0, boundary-free estimator: degree and support are refused with it as redundant (a second boundary cure), adaptive because the square-root law is unproved for a varying-shape kernel, and a parametric start because that composition is not built; it takes its own scale $b$ in place of h.
  • degree — the local-polynomial order. degree = 0 (default) is the binned KDE / WARPing $\hat f_X(x) = \frac{1}{n} \sum_j (n_j/h_j) K((x-\tilde c_j)/h_j)$ ($\int K = 1$), normalized by the total count; each bin's bandwidth is h unless adaptive. degree $\ge 1$ is the local-log-polynomial (local-likelihood) estimator (Loader 1996): fit $\log f_X$ by a degree-degree polynomial through a binned local Poisson likelihood and report the exponential of its fitted intercept, $\hat f_X(x) = e^{\hat\beta_0}$. degree = 1 (log-linear) is exact for an exponential density, so it tracks the exponential / stretched-exponential tails a flat KDE smears; degree = 2 (log-quadratic) is exact for a Gaussian. It falls back to the KDE where the local fit is degenerate or runs away in a sparse tail.
  • h — the smoothing bandwidth: the kernel scale, which sets the bias–variance trade-off (larger h smooths more). The default is the kernel- and degree-aware normal-reference rule, bandwidth(summary; kernel, degree) (bandwidth — Silverman's $(4/3)^{1/5}\hat\sigma_X n^{-1/5}$ (from the sample standard deviation) for the Gaussian kernel at degree 0/1, wider at the $n^{-1/9}$ rate for degree 2), which needs at least two samples. Pass a positive number to override, or, for a shape-adaptive choice, bandwidth(summary; rule = :sj) (or :isj / :aic). The asymmetric gamma_kernel / beta_kernel take their own scale $b$ here (rate $n^{-2/5}$; the default is their rule of thumb) in place of the symmetric h.
  • adaptive — whether the bandwidth varies with the local density. false (default) holds h fixed everywhere; true uses the Abramson variable bandwidth — the kernel width scales by $(\tilde f_X/G)^{-1/2}$ (a pilot $\tilde f_X$ at the bandwidth h, $G$ its geometric mean), widening in the sparse tails for far less tail variance (Abramson 1982). It composes with any choice of h, such as h = bandwidth(summary; rule = :sj), which becomes the Abramson pilot. (The degree-0 KDE uses the sample-point $h_j$ per bin; the local-log fit uses one balloon $h(x)$ at the query.)
  • support — the support of the density, passed as the tuple (lower, upper) (e.g. (0.0, Inf) for a positive quantity), or nothing (default, unbounded). When given, the degree-0 KDE is renormalized by the mass the kernel, rescaled to bandwidth h, puts inside the support: $\hat f_X(x) \big/ \int_{\texttt{lower}}^{\texttt{upper}} K_h(x-v)\,\mathrm{d}v$ (Jones 1993). This corrects the boundary bias that otherwise drops the estimate to $\approx$ half the true value at the edge. (degree $\ge 1$ is already boundary-adaptive, so support is a degree-0 option.)
  • start — a parametric start (Hjort & Glad 1995), or nothing (default). Given one, density returns the semiparametric estimate $\hat f_X(x) = f_{\hat\theta}(x) \frac{1}{nh}\sum_j \frac{n_j}{f_{\hat\theta}(\tilde c_j)} K((x-\tilde c_j)/h)$ — a moment-fitted model $f_{\hat\theta}$ times a KDE of its correction, near-exact wherever the start is correct (the correction is then $\equiv 1$) and degrading only to a bias-corrected KDE where it is not. $\hat\theta$ is fit by method of moments from the sample mean $\bar X$ and variance $\hat\sigma_X^2$ read off the summary, so the start is itself a functional of the summary. Pass a built-in normal_start, lognormal_start, gamma_start, exponential_start, or inverse_gaussian_start — the Parametric starts section lists each with its moment fit — or supply your own ParametricStart (a moment fit, a log-density, a support). It is a degree-0, fixed-h, symmetric-kernel estimator: degree $\ge 1$, adaptive, support, and the asymmetric kernels are refused with it, each a definable combination the package has not built (the local-likelihood one is Hjort & Jones 1996); use normalize for $\int\hat f_X = 1$.
  • normalize — when true, divide the estimate by $\int\hat f_X$ over the summary's range (intersected with the estimator's support), so $\int\hat f_X = 1$ exactly. The symmetric KDE already integrates to $\approx 1$; the asymmetric kernels and a parametric start do not, so this is their finishing step.
  • binning — where each bin's mass sits for the post-hoc evaluation. :linear (the default) places it at the within-bin centroid $\tilde c_j = c_j + S_1^{(j)}/n_j$ (the bin center plus its samples' mean offset, from the stored first power-sum $S_1$, their summed offsets), which matches the bin's own first moment, so the binned estimate converges to the exact KDE at $O(\Delta^2)$ in the bin width $\Delta$; :simple places it at the geometric bin center $\tilde c_j = c_j$, the $O(\Delta)$ mass-at-center approximation. The centroid costs nothing extra and applies to every path (KDE, local-log, start, asymmetric kernel).

The four corners (fixed/variable $\times$ KDE/local-log) and everything between are one call. A vector of query points returns a vector.

julia> summary = Histogram1D(-1.0, 1.0, 2);

julia> # one sample at 0.4
       observe!(summary, [0.4]);

julia> # linear binning centers the bump at 0.4 (the centroid)
       round(density(summary, 0.4; h = 0.2); digits = 4)
1.9947

References

  • I. S. Abramson (1982), On bandwidth variation in kernel estimates—a square root law, Ann. Statist. 10(4) (variable bandwidth).
  • C. R. Loader (1996), Local likelihood density estimation, Ann. Statist. 24(4) (the local-log-polynomial estimator).
  • M. C. Jones (1993), Simple boundary correction for kernel density estimation, Statist. Comput. 3(3) (the support renormalization).
  • N. L. Hjort & I. K. Glad (1995), Nonparametric density estimation with a parametric start, Ann. Statist. 23(3) (the start estimator).

GBGStreamingStatistics.MarginalDensity.density_standard_error — Function
density_standard_error(summary::Histogram1D, x; kernel = gaussian_kernel,
                       degree = 0, h = bandwidth(summary; kernel, degree),
                       adaptive = false, support = nothing, start = nothing,
                       normalize = false, binning = :linear)

Estimand. The estimand is the pointwise standard error of the density estimate at $x$, $\operatorname{SE}(x) = \sqrt{\operatorname{Var}\hat f_X(x)}$ with the kernel-density asymptotic variance

\[\operatorname{Var}\hat f_X(x) \approx \frac{R(K) f_X(x)}{n h} - \frac{f_X(x)^2}{n}, \qquad R(K) = \int K^2.\]

Estimator. The estimator is $\sqrt{R(K) \hat f_X(x)/(n h) - \hat f_X(x)^2/n}$, from the estimate $\hat f_X(x)$, the count $n$, the bandwidth h (default the normal reference), and the roughness $R(K) = \int K^2$ of the smoothing kernel $K$ — any symmetric $\int K = 1$ callable, default gaussian_kernel — by quadrature. The pointwise variability band is $\hat f_X(x) \pm 1.96 \operatorname{SE}(x)$ (95\%) — a band about $\mathbb{E}\hat f_X$, so it covers $f_X$ where the $O(h^2)$ smoothing bias is small and narrows that coverage at a sharp mode or in a tail.

It tracks the estimator's options — pass the same options you gave density and the standard error is that of that estimate (or use density_with_standard_error for both from one call). The variance is adjusted per option: adaptive replaces $h$ by the local Abramson bandwidth $h(x)$ (a wider window — hence smaller variance — in the tail; the sample-point pilot's own variability is an unmodelled higher-order term, so the band runs slightly narrow in the deep tail); support (Jones renormalization) replaces $R(K)$ by the boundary-restricted roughness $R_a(x) = \frac{1}{h}\int_{\text{support}} K((x-v)/h)^2\,\mathrm dv$ and divides by the squared in-support kernel mass $a(x)^2$, inflating the variance near an edge; a parametric start leaves the leading variance $R(K)\hat f_X/(nh)$ unchanged (the fitted-parameter uncertainty is an $O(1/n)$ omission). Only degree 0/1 and a symmetric kernel are supported ($\texttt{degree} \ge 2$ and an asymmetric gamma/beta kernel are refused); a vector of query points returns a vector.

Arguments: as for density — the options specify the estimator whose standard error is returned.

julia> summary = Histogram1D(-6.0, 6.0, 600);

julia> observe!(summary, [-0.4, 0.1, 0.2, 0.9, 1.3]);

julia> density_standard_error(summary, 0.5) > 0
true

Reference: M. P. Wand & M. C. Jones (1995), Kernel Smoothing, Chapman & Hall.

GBGStreamingStatistics.MarginalDensity.density_with_standard_error — Function
density_with_standard_error(summary::Histogram1D, x; kernel = gaussian_kernel,
                            degree = 0, h = bandwidth(summary; kernel, degree),
                            adaptive = false, support = nothing, start = nothing,
                            normalize = false, binning = :linear)

This reader returns the density estimate and its pointwise density_standard_error from a single option specification, so the estimate and its error describe the same estimator by construction. The result is the NamedTuple (value, standard_error), value the estimate and standard_error its standard error; a vector x gives a (value, standard_error) of vectors. The standard error's estimand and per-option variance are documented at density_standard_error.

Arguments: as for density.

julia> summary = Histogram1D(-6.0, 6.0, 600);

julia> observe!(summary, [-0.4, 0.1, 0.2, 0.9, 1.3]);

julia> r = density_with_standard_error(summary, 0.5);

julia> (r.value > 0, r.standard_error > 0)
(true, true)

Parametric starts

The moment-fitted ParametricStart models serve the Hjort–Glad semiparametric density(…; start = …); each is documented below.

GBGStreamingStatistics.MarginalDensity.normal_start — Constant
normal_start

The Gaussian (normal) ParametricStart on $(-\infty,\infty)$ takes the density $f_{\hat\theta}$ of $\mathcal{N}(\bar X, \hat\sigma_X^2)$, with $\bar X$, $\hat\sigma_X^2$ the sample mean and variance. Pass start = normal_start to density.

source
GBGStreamingStatistics.MarginalDensity.lognormal_start — Constant
lognormal_start

The log-normal ParametricStart on $(0,\infty)$ fits its parameters by method of moments (the log-variance $\log(1+\hat\sigma_X^2/\bar X^2)$, the log-mean $\log\bar X$ minus half the log-variance). It is a natural start for a positively skewed quantity such as a droplet radius; pass start = lognormal_start.

source
GBGStreamingStatistics.MarginalDensity.gamma_start — Constant
gamma_start

The gamma ParametricStart on $(0,\infty)$ fits the shape $\hat\alpha = \bar X^2/\hat\sigma_X^2$ and the scale $\hat\sigma_X^2/\bar X$ by method of moments. It suits a non-negative quantity; pass start = gamma_start.

source
GBGStreamingStatistics.MarginalDensity.exponential_start — Constant
exponential_start

The exponential ParametricStart on $[0,\infty)$ fits the rate $1/\bar X$ (a one-parameter start, from the mean alone). Pass start = exponential_start; it pairs with the degree = 1 log-linear fit as the two exponential-tail tools.

source
GBGStreamingStatistics.MarginalDensity.inverse_gaussian_start — Constant
inverse_gaussian_start

The inverse-Gaussian ParametricStart on $(0,\infty)$ fits the mean $\bar X$ and the shape $\bar X^3/\hat\sigma_X^2$ by method of moments; it is a positive start with an exponentially decaying right tail (lighter than the log-normal's). Pass start = inverse_gaussian_start.

source
GBGStreamingStatistics.MarginalDensity.ParametricStart — Type
ParametricStart(fit, logpdf, support)

A parametric start serves the Hjort–Glad semiparametric density (Hjort & Glad 1995): passed as density(…; start = …), it multiplies the moment-fitted model $f_{\hat\theta}$ by a kernel estimate of the correction $f_X/f_\theta$ (the true density over the model's) — near-exact where the model fits (the bias is driven by $(f_X/f_\theta)''$, not $f_X''$), else degrading only to a kernel-of-the-ratio bias. The built-ins are normal_start, lognormal_start, gamma_start, exponential_start, and inverse_gaussian_start; construct one for a custom model.

Arguments

The constructor takes the three fields:

  • fit(mean, variance) — fit the model parameters by method of moments from the mean and variance, returning a parameter tuple. Fitting from the moments, not the raw data, keeps the estimate mergeable (two summaries' estimates combine by adding them): $\hat\theta$ is itself a post-hoc functional of the summary.
  • logpdf(x, params) — the model log-density $\log f_{\hat\theta}(x)$, returning $-\infty$ off the support. density reweights each bin by $1/f_{\hat\theta}(\tilde c_j)$ at the bin location $\tilde c_j$ (center or centroid, per binning) through this log form (stable in the tails, where $f_{\hat\theta}$ is tiny).
  • support — the domain the model is positive on, as a (lower, upper) tuple (e.g. (0.0, Inf)).
julia> rayleigh = ParametricStart(
           # σ from the mean
           (mean, variance) -> (mean * sqrt(2 / π),),
           # Rayleigh log-density
           (x, p) -> x > 0 ? log(x) - 2 * log(p[1]) - x^2 / (2 * p[1]^2) : -Inf,
           (0.0, Inf));

julia> rayleigh isa ParametricStart
true

Reference: N. L. Hjort & I. K. Glad (1995), Nonparametric density estimation with a parametric start, Ann. Statist. 23(3).

Fields

  • fit — the moment fit, a callable (mean, variance) returning the parameter tuple.
  • logpdf — the model log-density, a callable (x, params).
  • support — the domain the model is positive on, a (lower, upper) tuple.
